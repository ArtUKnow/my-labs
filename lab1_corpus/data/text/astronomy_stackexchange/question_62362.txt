Is this a valid robustness test of the Union3 compressed distance-modulus matrix?
I am checking the public Union3 compressed distance-modulus matrix, specifically mu_mat_union3_cosmo=2_mu.fits, and I would like to know whether the following is a statistically meaningful robustness test of this compressed representation.
Using the compressed Union3 matrix, I reproduce the Union3-only flat ΛCDM best fit at approximately:
$$
\Omega_m \approx 0.3559.
$$
If I fix the matter density to a DESI-like value,
$$
\Omega_m = 0.2975,
$$
the fit is penalized by approximately:
$$
\Delta \chi^2 \approx 5.152.
$$
At this fixed $\Omega_m$, a CPL $w_0,w_a$ model improves the fit by:
$$
\Delta \chi^2 \approx 7.536,
$$
corresponding to an AIC gain of about +3.536.
I then tested a simple one-parameter redshift-dependent nuisance template:
$$
T(z)=G(z;0.09,0.06)-G(z;0.775,0.10),
$$
standardized to zero mean and unit RMS, with one fitted amplitude. This template improves the same fixed-$\Omega_m$ fit by:
$$
\Delta \chi^2 \approx 17.120,
$$
corresponding to an AIC gain of about +15.120.
After including this low-z / mid-z nuisance mode, the additional improvement from CPL drops to only:
$$
\Delta \chi^2 \approx 0.870,
$$
so CPL becomes AIC-disfavored relative to the nuisance-template model.
The result also appears not to be driven by the high-z tail: removing $z>1$ still gives a low-z / mid-z template improvement of about:
$$
\Delta \chi^2 \approx 17.52.
$$
The two template regions also roughly correspond to changes in survey composition: around $z\approx0.10$, the sample is mainly SDSS / Foundation / Pan-STARRS, while around $z\approx0.75$, it is mainly SNLS / ESSENCE / DES3_Deep.
My question is:
Is this a meaningful robustness test of the Union3 compressed spline distance-modulus representation, or is there a technical reason why this compressed matrix should not be tested with an additional redshift-dependent nuisance mode in this way?
In particular, could the spline-based compression or the way the covariance/precision matrix is constructed make the apparent $\Delta\chi^2$ improvement from such a template misleading?
I am not claiming that this proves anything about the underlying physics. I am asking whether this is a valid compressed-data diagnostic, and whether the corresponding nuisance direction would need to be tested in the full DESI+CMB+Union3 likelihood.
