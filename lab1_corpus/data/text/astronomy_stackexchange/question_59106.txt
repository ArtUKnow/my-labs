Wouldn't searches for repeating patterns in the sky to detect a "pacman" geometry of the universe have to account for different light travel times?
I'm not into physics, so these thoughts may not make sense at all:
I wonder whether searches for repeating patterns in the sky (or CMB) to detect a "pacman" geometry of the universe wouldn't have to account for different light travel times of the observed instances of the repeated object (or whether this is actual practice).
When we see objects multiple times, the light of each instance followed a different path, each with a different distance. For example, in such a geometry we could reach the moon "straight away" or we could travel in some other direction and reach the moon "from behind". As a consequence, the various instances of one and the same object would look different, each snapshot showing it at a different point of time. So how can we find repeated views of the same things when they do not appear to us in an identical way?
