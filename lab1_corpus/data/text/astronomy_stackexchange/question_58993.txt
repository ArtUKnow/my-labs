Gregorian lunar correction
In 1582, when the Gregorian calendar was instituted, in addition to removing ten days from the month of October, four days were subtracted from the age of the Moon. Was this number observed or computed? My computation is that in the 12x1257 (= 15084) months between the council of Nicea in 325 and the Gregorian reform in 1582, the discrepancy between the nominal synodic month (29.5 days) and the astronomical synodic month (29.53059 days) would amount to a difference of 461 days (= .03*15084)! Even allowing for the loss of full synodic months, the difference is on the order of 18 days (= Mod[461, 29.5]). If the four-day discrepancy was observed, is there any way to reconcile it with the 18 day value? That is, can the four days be explained computationally?
