Is Astronomical Twilight precisely 18 degrees?
As I understand it, Astronomical Twilight is defined as ending ("Astronomical Dusk") when the sun is 18 degrees below the horizon.
At this point (to quote Wikipedia):
Theoretically, the faintest stars detectable by the naked eye (those
of approximately the sixth magnitude) will become visible in the
evening at astronomical dusk, and become invisible at astronomical
dawn
Is 18 degrees the precise point of maximum darkness (assuming no artificial light), or is it a convenient approximation? It feels like it would be a very strange coincidence that it should be a neat round number. The answers to this question here seem to suggest that 18 degrees is arbitrary, but does a precise measure exist at all for:
a) The point at which all stars are visible?
b) The point at which maximum darkness is reached?
Does the degree amount for the above measure vary depending on location/time/atmospheric behavior? And by how much?
