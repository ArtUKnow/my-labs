Rotation of a planet orbiting in a polar orbit w.r.t a binary system
I was quite surprised to hear the news of the discovery of 2M1510, a system with a central binary formed by dwarfs on highly elliptical orbits with a planet revolving at almost 90 degrees wrt the binary system. There is also a more remote dwarf orbiting the central system. In my ignorance I thought that stable planetary systems had to be approximately coplanar and hierarchical (approximable with a sequence of two body systems). If you look at this question of 2015 (Orbits in a binary star system) there is no mention of polar orbits. So I searched around and found the paper "Polar planets around highly eccentric binaries are the most stable" by Chen et al. which 6 years ago seemed to have already predicted the stability of systems like 2M1510 (which kind of makes sense if you think about the symmetry of the system). How would the rotation of such planets be affected by the gravitational force of two separate bodies as far as speed and axis are concerned?
