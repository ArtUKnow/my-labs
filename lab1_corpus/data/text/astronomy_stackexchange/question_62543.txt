If black hole stars exist, can they produce heavier elements like regular stars?
Black hole stars are a very unusual hypothetical type of stars. Because these stars are way too massive, they don’t produce energy via nuclear fusion. Instead the core collapses into a black hole directly and begins to accrete surrounding matters, which releases enormous amounts of energy that heats up the outer envelope.
My question is whether these stars can produce new elements and eject them into the interstellar space. Normal stars are in hydrostatic equilibrium. As the nuclear fusion goes on, the core becomes hotter and denser, which enables the star to fuse progressively heavier elements until it reaches the iron peak. However, black hole stars don’t have a hydrostatic equilibrium. Instead the black hole is like a vacuum cleaner that drains away the hottest part of the star. Even if some elements are created in the process, they are unlikely to escape.
