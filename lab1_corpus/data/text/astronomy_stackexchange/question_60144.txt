Can Alcubierre drive reach the "edge" of the universe?
It is well known that Alcubierre drive is, for now, the only theory that can prove superluminal space travel whilst still obeying Einstein's field equations.
I have read the paper regarding the relativistic nature of Alcubierre drive (arXiv:gr-qc/0009013), and know that it requires a spacetime bubble created with negative and positive energy to "bring an object to itself", rather than the spacecraft directly going to the object.
However, if this promises interstellar or intergalactic distances that can be reached in a proper time of less than a human lifetime, could this also mean it is physically possible for the warp drive to reach the "edge" of the universe, (though it is continuously accelerating)?
Additional question:
Assuming, Alcubierre warp drive is able to reach the fastest expanding region of spacetime, when and how will its laws of physics break?
Will it be confined to a fixed geodesic at the "edge" of the universe? To what extent are the distances Alcubierre warp drive cannot theoretically reach?
