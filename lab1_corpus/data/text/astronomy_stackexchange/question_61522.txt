Detection of overgrown black holes
In Schwarzschild radius definition $$r_{\text{s}}={\frac {2GM}{c^{2}}} \tag 1$$
multiplying both sides by $1/{r_s^2}$, it can be re-stated as
$$ \tag 2 \frac 1{r_s} = {\frac {GM}{r_s^2}\cdot\frac{2}{c^2}}$$
In RHS of equation first term classically is Surface gravity, so (2) can be re-written as,
$$ \tag 3 g_s = \frac {c^2}{2r_s} = \frac {c^2}{D_s}, $$
where $D_s$ is Schwarzschild diameter.
So as per (3),- when SMBH size increases,- it's gravitational acceleration at the event horizon - decreases.
EDIT
Surface gravity for the Schwarzschild solution of mass $M$ is defined as $$\kappa =\frac{c^{4}}{4GM} \tag 4$$
and this gives same classical value defined in (3). Also shows that when black hole mass increases, then it's surface gravity decreases.
According to this overgrown black holes (OBH) with mass about trillions of solar masses or with event horizon diameter $\approx 1~ly$ or more will have gravitational acceleration at the event horizon $\leq 10~m/s^2$, like here in Earth. So such OBH to outside observer by gravitation strength will be indistinguishable from Earth-type planets.
Omitting question can such OBH exist, I want to know- is it feasible to detect such black holes, and what methods should help to detect such "weak" space-time anomalies, since at such lazy black hole, not much tidal events or capture events can happen.
