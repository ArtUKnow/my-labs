Path of sun in the sky on a planet with no axial tilt
Some sources online (e.g., Solar Analemma on the Time and Date website, see section "Effect of Earth’s Eccentricity") claim that if the Earth's axis were not tilted, then the analemma would have the shape of an ellipse/oval. I think if there were no tilt, then the midday analemma would simply be a line along the celestial equator from any latitude (i.e. sun would have constant elevation at midday throughout the year). And if the analemma was not taken at midday, then its shape would be close to a line with constant celestial latitude: there would be some small variation in solar elevation due to the true solar time differing from the mean solar time (the analemma is taken at the same mean solar time every day). Am I missing something, or are those sources simply inaccurate?
