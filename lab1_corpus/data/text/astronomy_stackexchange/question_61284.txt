Why years like 1700, 1800, and 1900 are not leap years when they should be?
Earth's revolution period around the Sun is 365 days, 6 hours, 9 minutes which means we need to have at least one leap year every four years. Yet, the Gregorian calendar tries to correct itself by not including years like 1700, 1800 and 1900 as leap years. Is it possible when the calendar was created, Earth's exact revolution period wasn't correctly known or am I missing something? With the extra 9-minute offset, we not only need to have 1700, 1800 and 1900 as leap years but also every 160th year needs to have two leap days.
