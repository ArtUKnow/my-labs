Eclipse Saros Cycle vs Nodal Precession Cycle
I'm trying to understand how the Earth, Moon, Sun and stars line up after one Saros cycle is over.
According to this Article a Saros cycle is a period of 223 synodic months after which the Sun, Earth, and Moon return to approximately the same geometry. "Any two eclipses separated by one Saros cycle share similar characteristics : they occur at the same node with the Moon at nearly the same distance from Earth and at the same time of year".
223 synodic months is 6585.32 days, or about 18 years, 11 days and 8h.
So ignoring the 8h shift, the earth, moon and sun will be in the same relative geometry, and almost same geometry relative to the stars (same time of year + ~11 days).
The rate of lunar Nodal Precession however is about 6793 days, or 18.6 years.
18.6 years don't fit into a Saros nicely. What i don't understand is how the lunar nodes can be back to where they were after one Saros cycle is over if their rotation period is greater than one Saros.
To clarify, i'm aware of the structure of a Saros cycle and what makes it work for eclipse predictions. What is asked here is how to resolve the apparent contradiction with the Nodal Precession cycle, whose period seems to imply that one Saros after an eclipse there won't be another eclipse because the nodes will be out of alignment.
