In Which Locations Would It Be Possible To Have 4 “Constant” Constellations?
Suppose a theoretical world with an axis tilt of 45°.
Suppose people in the Northern Hemisphere have also assigned 12 constellations, similar to Zodiac signs. By that, I mean that it doesn't matter how far or close the stars are, but instead that they are more or less evenly spread around the visible night sky throughout the year.
Would it then be possible to live in a place where only a third, 4 constellations, are always (throughout the year) in the night sky, while the other 8 are each only visible during certain parts of the year?
I think that on the equator, you would see one half of the galaxy in summer, and the other half in winter, so there are no "constant" constellations.
In contrast, on either pole, you would always see the same part of the galaxy (even though technically, in summer, there wouldn't really be a night).
So, a position 2/3rds between that, 60° off the North Pole, should roughly give me those 4 "constant" constellations. However, am I forgetting anything here?
