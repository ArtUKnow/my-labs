Population III stars CNO cycle vs Triple Alpha
What type of fusion powered Population III stars with around 150 solar masses? Population III stars were the first stars born in the universe and were made from pristine Big Bang material. They were born with hydrogen, helium and a little bit of lithium, but no heavier elements like carbon, nitrogen, and oxygen. So the only type of fusion initially allowed would be the proton chain fusion instead of CNO cycle that normally powers massive stars. But the proton chain is not energetic enough to balance the weight of these stars so the the core heats up enough to allow the Triple Alpha process to begin fusing helium into carbon. As carbon builds up in the core the CNO cycle starts.
But the CNO cycle generates much more energy once it kicks in. The core needs to be around 100 million degrees Celsius to fuse helium. Given the increased energy from the CNO cycle, I would imagine the core might cool down once the CNO cycle starts and then the Triple Alpha Process might end until after all of the core's hydrogen is used up by the CNO cycle. Is that what happens? Another possibility is the core stays hot enough to generate energy by both the CNO cycle and the Triple Alpha process, but the helium supply might only last 500,000 years or so and then all that would be left would be the CNO cycle until the core heats up enough to fuse carbon into elements heavier than oxygen.
The carbon from the CNO cycle is generated in the stellar core. Does the carbon migrate throughout the the stars envelope allowing the switch from Proton chain fusion to CNO cycle fusion outside of the stellar nucleus?
