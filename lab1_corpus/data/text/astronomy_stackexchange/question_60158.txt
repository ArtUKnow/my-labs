Why is estimating the age of an individual star so complicated?
I have several books on stellar evolution (where one comes from the American Astronomical Society's IOP Astronomy textbook: Understanding Stellar Evolution, by Henry J. G. L. M. Lamers and Emily M. Levesque). I have studied and read all of the specific theories and calculations used to describe any star in a specific stage of its life. But how come stellar astrophysicists still struggle in estimating the age of stars despite having a rather robust foundation on how stars evolve in specific conditions?
I understand that astronomers currently estimate the ages of stars by plotting the stars of a globular cluster on a HR diagram where "astronomers can determine the cluster's age by looking at the main sequence turnoff point and comparing it with models of stellar evolution" as given by:
Staff, Astronomy. “How Do We Measure the Age of a Globular Cluster?” Astronomy Magazine, 25 Feb. 2019.
However why can't astronomers plot a single star on a HR diagram, instead of an entire cluster, to estimate the age of the star by comparing observations with stellar evolution models that could estimate the evolutionary track? I would also be interested if anyone could give an empirical or theoretical method to estimate the age of a star with reasonable accuracy
