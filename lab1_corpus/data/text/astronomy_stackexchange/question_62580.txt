Why does Schlyter's iterative sunrise/sunset algorithm use 15.04107°/ hour when 15.0°/ hour gives more accurate results?
I am implementing Paul Schlyter's sunrise/sunset algorithm from his page:
https://stjarnhimlen.se/comp/riset.html
In the basic calculation, the Sun's transit time is computed from:
$$
LST = RA
$$
with
$$
GMST_0 + UT \times 15.0^\circ + longitude = RA
$$
therefore:
$$
UT_{\rm transit}
=
\frac{RA-GMST_0-longitude}{15.0}
$$
For the rise/set calculation, I use:
$$
\cos H_0 =
\frac{\sin h_0-\sin\phi\sin\delta}
{\cos\phi\cos\delta}
$$
where I use the standard solar altitude:
$$
h_0=-0.833^\circ
$$
Then:
$$
H_0=\arccos(\cos H_0)
$$
and, without iteration:
$$
H_0({\rm hours})=\frac{H_0({\rm degrees})}{15.0}
$$
so that:
$$
UT_{\rm rise}=UT_{\rm transit}-H_0
$$
$$
UT_{\rm set}=UT_{\rm transit}+H_0
$$
This gives results that are already very close to Stellarium in my tests.
The problem appears when I apply Schlyter's "Higher accuracy: iteration"
In section 3 of Schlyter's page, the iteration recommends recalculating the Sun's RA and declination at the estimated rise/set time and then recalculating the event time.
Most importantly, Schlyter says to use:
$$
\boxed{15.04107}
$$
instead of 15.0 when converting the local hour angle into hours:
$$
H_0({\rm hours})
=
\frac{H_0({\rm degrees})}{15.04107}
$$
I implemented this literally.
However, I get a surprising result: the iterative result becomes significantly worse when compared with Stellarium.
For example, at:
$$
\phi=24.68773^\circ N
$$
$$
\lambda=46.72185^\circ E
$$
The non-iterative calculation differs from the "Stellarium" program's calculations by approximately 4 seconds for sunrise and 7 seconds for sunset.
When I enable the iteration and use:
$$
\boxed{15.04107}
$$
the result becomes differs from the "Stellarium" program's calculations by approximately 52 seconds for sunrise and 51 seconds for sunset.
If I change only this one factor in the iterative calculation from:
$$
\frac{H_0}{15.04107}
$$
to:
$$
\boxed{\frac{H_0}{15.0}}
$$
the difference from Stellarium becomes approximately zero for the sunrise and sunset in this test.
I tried to isolate the source of the error
I checked whether the problem was caused by the Sun's RA/Dec.
At the exact sunrise time reported by Stellarium, my independent solar-position routine gives a solar altitude of approximately:
$$
h=-0.833^\circ
$$
which indicates that the RA and declination used by the rise/set calculation are consistent with the geometric altitude condition I am using.
I also compared the final RA and declination from my iterative calculation with the values obtained by my independent solar-position routine at the same time, and they agree.
This makes it appear that the disparity in the result is specifically related to the conversion:
$$
\boxed{H_0({\rm degrees})\rightarrow H_0({\rm hours})}
$$
rather than to RA, declination, GMST0, or the solar-position calculation.
Why is the difference close to one minute?
For example, if the hour angle is around (80^\circ), then:
$$
\frac{80}{15.0}=5.333333\ {\rm h}
$$
while:
$$
\frac{80}{15.04107}=5.318820\ {\rm h}
$$
The difference is:
$$
0.014513\ {\rm h}
$$
or approximately:
$$
52.25\ {\rm seconds}
$$
which is very close to the ~52-second difference I observe for the sunrise.
So experimentally, the effect is exactly of the expected magnitude.
My question
Why does Schlyter's iterative algorithm use:
$$
\boxed{15.04107^\circ/{\rm hour}}
$$
for converting the hour angle to hours, while the rest of the calculation uses:
$$
\boxed{15.0^\circ/{\rm hour}}
$$
and, in my implementation, replacing 15.04107 by 15.0 makes the result substantially more accurate when compared with Stellarium?
More specifically:
Is using (15.04107) in
$$
H_0({\rm hours})=\frac{H_0}{15.04107}
$$
actually mathematically consistent with the preceding equation
$$
GMST_0+15.0\,UT+\lambda=RA
$$
or are these two uses of the hour/degree conversion based on different time scales?
Is there an additional correction or transformation required when using (15.04107), which is missing from my implementation?
Does Schlyter's iterative formula assume a different definition of the time variable in the (H_0/15.04107) step?
Why would (15.0) produce a result closer to Stellarium even though (15.04107) is the Earth's rotation rate relative to the celestial reference frame?
Could the issue be related to the distinction between mean solar time and sidereal time, rather than simply choosing the correct numerical angular velocity?
Is the recommendation to use 15.04107 in Schlyter's iteration intended to compensate for the change in the Sun's apparent position during the iteration, or is it derived from a more specific relationship between solar time and sidereal time?
I am interested in the mathematically consistent derivation, rather than simply choosing whichever constant happens to produce the smallest error against Stellarium.
I would particularly appreciate an explanation of what exactly changes between the equations using (15.0) and (15.04107), and whether Schlyter's iteration as written is internally consistent.
