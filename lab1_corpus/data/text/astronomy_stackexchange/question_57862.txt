In astronomical applications, light sources are so distant that the
concentric wavefronts become a series of equally spaced parallel
planes across the width of any practical telescope aperture. To
illustrate: across the aperture of a 1 meter (39.4") telescope, light

at 384,403 kilometers, diverge from parallel by no more than
1/384403000 of a radian or 0.0000026 millimeter, which is 0.0047 or
1/200 a wavelength of "green" light. Since the fabrication limits of
the highest quality astronomical optics are around λ/20 wave, or 10
times larger than the wavefront divergence, optical calculations can
assume perfectly flat and parallel wavefronts from a distant light
source.
