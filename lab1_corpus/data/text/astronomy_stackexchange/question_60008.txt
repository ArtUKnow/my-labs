Is the S2 gravitational redshift showing a singularity?
S2 also known as S0–2, is a star in the star cluster close to the supermassive black hole Sagittarius A* (Sgr A*), orbiting it with a period of about 16 years.
S2 gravitational redshift has been measured and reported in Abuter et al. 2018.
My question is very specific to Fig.3 in the paper, showing a spike of Residual velocity of over 200 km/s observed around 2018.
Residual velocity $c∆z=c(z_{GR}−z_{K})$ for the best fitting prior Keplerian $K_{prior}$ ($f =0$, grey) and the same orbit with $f =1$ (red $GR_{prior}$). $K_{prior}$
was constructed from all 1992-2018 astrometric data with NACO &
GRAVITY and the SINFONI data between 2004 and 2016 (open black
circles). The 2017/2018 SINFONI data points (black circles with cyan
shading) can then be added to test if the spectroscopic data around pericentre follow $K_{prior}$ or the $GR_{prior}$ predicted from $K_{prior}$. The new data
points near and up to pericentre, where the $β^2$
effects in radial velocity
are expected to be important, fall close to the predicted $GR_{prior}$ curve,
and exclude the Keplerian prior orbit.
I find the shape of that curve outstanding (cusp, mathematical singularity).
It makes me think that the redshift actually gets much higher for a very short time.
Especially the fact that the curve was fitted based on a limited number of readings around the pericentre (no readings between May 4 and 24, 2018, see Fig.2 in the paper [Edit: "outliers" note deleted])
My main question is this:
Can we expect that if we had better data coverage at the right time (around the cusp) our readings could shoot up to very large values? (as the shape of the curve seems to point asymptotically towards infinity)
Minor: Are there other stars orbiting Sgr* that are getting closer to the black hole before S2 gets there again (in 2034)?
