If the scale factor is defined to be unity at a different epoch, how is the radius of the observable universe changed?
The scale factor of the universe is defined to be unity now. In terms of this scale factor, the radius of the observable universe is defined as
$$ r = \int^{t_0}_0 \frac{c}{a(t)}\ dt\ , $$
where $t_0$ is the current epoch and $a(t)$ describes how the scale factor evolves with time.
But what if the scale factor was redefined to be unity at some other epoch? Say the epoch where the CMB formed at $z \simeq 1100$, such that $ a \rightarrow a\times 1100$. That would seem to make $r$ smaller in the equation above. How is that reconciled with presumably fixed "radius of the observable universe" at the current epoch?
