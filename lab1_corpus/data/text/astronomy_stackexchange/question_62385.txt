At what distance from Sagittarius A* would an astronaut receive a lethal dose of radiation?
The supermassive black hole at the center of our galaxy, Sagittarius A*, is a relatively quiet black hole. However, it does at times, emit a violent surge of X-rays in brief flares, that are so powerful that leave molecular clouds glowing for manu decades even after the flare has ended.
Even in its dormant state, according to this paper, the virial temperature of ions in the inner accretion disk can be as high as $10^{12}$ K, whereas the electron temperatures can be as high as $10^9$-$10^{10}$ K. From my basic understanding of Wien's Law, this would correspond to a blackbody wavelength of 0.000002898 nm, which is far off in the gamma-ray regime.
Which brings me to this thought experiment:
Suppose I parked a warp drive around a 1 light year from Sagittarius A*, and I jumped out from an airlock with a space suit into the vacuum of space, equipped with a geiger counter measuring in milliroentgens.
Assume that hunger, thirst and oxygen are not a problem here for the sake of the question, and I start to slowly drift in the general direction of Sagittarius A* in freefall.
Assume I am approaching the black hole while it is in its dormant phase, aka when it is not flaring or anything, and the X-rays are only coming from the accretion disk, and the nascent jet it possesses.
I'm approaching it in the plane of the accretion disk, aka I'm approaching it "sideways", instead of approaching it in the direction of the astrophysical jets
The spacesuit in question is similar to the extravehicular mobility units worn by astronauts for spacewalks around the ISS.
At what distance from Sagittarius A would the radiation levels be equivalent to that of standing near the Elephant's foot near Chernobyl ($10^5$ milliroentgens), aka enough to kill a human within a few hours?
