Delta v in gravity assistance with impact parameter
In the following lecture, at page 110, relation 5.12:
https://people.unipi.it/tommei/wp-content/uploads/sites/124/2019/04/spacemechanics.pdf
We get:
$$\Delta v=2\dfrac{v_\infty}{e}$$
I tried to find, in the literature, the case taking into account the impact parameter $b_\infty$, but I failed.
So I tried to derive the result based on the following figure:
Reasoning as follows:
$$\dfrac{b_\infty}{-a\cdot e}=\sin(\beta)$$
But as $\beta=\pi/2-\delta/2$ we have:
$$\begin{aligned}
\dfrac{b_\infty}{-a\cdot e}
& =\sin(\beta)\\
& =\sin\left(\frac\pi2-\frac\delta2\right)\\
& =\cos\left(\dfrac{\delta}{2}\right)\\
& =\pm\sqrt{\dfrac{1+\cos(\delta)}{2}}
\end{aligned}$$
Hence:
$$\cos(\delta)=-\dfrac{2b^2_\infty}{a^2e^2}-1$$
Using the cosine law as in the lecture notes mentioned above (5.12):
$$\begin{aligned}
\Delta v
& =\sqrt{v_{\infty}^2+v_{\infty}^2-2 v_{\infty}^2 \cos (\delta)}\\
& =\sqrt{2 v_{\infty}^2(1-\cos (\delta))}\\
& =\sqrt{-\dfrac{4b^2_\infty v^2_\infty}{a^2e^2}}\\
& =\dfrac{2b_\infty v_\infty}{ae}
\end{aligned}$$
However:
I don't know if the result is correct.
If it is correct, I don't know if it's the traditional way to present the result.
Any help, remark, critic is greatly appreciated.
