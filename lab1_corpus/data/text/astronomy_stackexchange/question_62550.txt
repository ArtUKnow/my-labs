How should GMST0 be calculated in the Stjarnhimlen solar-position algorithm?
I am implementing the solar-position algorithm described by Paul Schlyter on Stjarnhimlen:
Stjarnhimlen's Sidereal time and hour angle. Altitude and azimuth
I am unsure about the intended interpretation of GMST0 in the algorithm.
The page states:
"GMST0 is the Sidereal Time at the Greenwich meridian at 00:00 right now"
and gives:
$$
\mathrm{GMST0} = \frac{L + 180^\circ}{15}
$$
where (L) is the Sun's mean longitude.
It then gives:
$$
\mathrm{SIDTIME} = \mathrm{GMST0} + UT + \frac{\mathrm{LON}}{15}
$$
where (UT) is UT in hours and (LON) is terrestrial longitude in degrees.
Earlier in the same tutorial, the day number is defined as:
$$
d = JD - 2451543.5
$$
The text explains that at midnight UT, (d) is an integer, while for another time of day:
$$
d = d_0 + \frac{UT}{24}
$$
The Sun's mean longitude is then calculated from:
$$
w = 282.9404 + 4.70935 \times 10^{-5} d
$$
$$
M = 356.0470 + 0.9856002585 d
$$
$$
L = w + M
$$
My initial interpretation was therefore:
Compute (d) at 00:00 UT for the given date.
Compute (w), (M), and (L) from that value of (d).
Compute:
$$
\mathrm{GMST0} = \frac{L + 180^\circ}{15}
$$
Then compute:
$$
\mathrm{SIDTIME} = \mathrm{GMST0} + UT + \frac{\mathrm{LON}}{15}
$$
However, I noticed an unexpected numerical result.
If I instead compute (w), (M), and (L) using the full fractional day number for the actual UTC time,
$$
d = d_0 + \frac{UT}{24}
$$
and then still use:
$$
\mathrm{SIDTIME} = \mathrm{GMST0} + UT + \frac{\mathrm{LON}}{15}
$$
the resulting local sidereal time—and consequently the calculated solar altitude and azimuth—are noticeably closer to a higher-accuracy reference.
This seems contradictory because the effect of (UT) appears to be included twice: once through the fractional part of (d), and once explicitly in:
$$
\mathrm{GMST0} + UT
$$
For example, if (d) is evaluated at 00:00 UT, GMST0 remains constant throughout the day. If the fractional part (UT/24) is included in (d), then GMST0 itself changes during the day.
So I would like to clarify the following:
In Schlyter's intended implementation, should the Sun's mean longitude (L) used for GMST0 be calculated from (d) at 00:00 UT, or from (d) at the actual UTC time?
If GMST0 is specifically the Greenwich Mean Sidereal Time at 00:00 UT, why does using the fractional day number corresponding to the actual time produce a more accurate result when (UT) is subsequently added again?
Is this an intentional approximation in Schlyter's algorithm, where the variation of (L) with the fractional day is used to approximate the difference between a solar day and a sidereal day?
Or is the apparent improvement simply an accidental cancellation of errors in the approximate solar-orbital equations?
I am specifically interested in understanding the intended interpretation of Schlyter's original Stjarnhimlen algorithm, rather than replacing it with a modern high-precision GMST formula.
