The state of icy worlds during differentiation and accretion, and how they survive with their water
There are several small icy worlds in the Solar System (Pluto, Ceres, Ganymede, Titan, Mimas, Oberon, etc.). During formation, these worlds would have gotten hot enough to melt their water-ice into liquid or even evaporate it to steam, which was needed for them to differentiate into an outer icy shell and an inner rocky core.
But here is where my question comes in: From my understanding, a small, warm world should not be able to hold onto liquid or gaseous water, as vapors would escape into space as a result of thermal escape, removing all the water over time. So how do these worlds avoid becoming completely dried-out rocky worlds, and what would a world in the middle of this process be/look like?
