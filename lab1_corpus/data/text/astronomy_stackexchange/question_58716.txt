Why do some of the Galilean moons receive so much less radiation than others?
There's a wide range of variation in the Galilean moons' exposure to radiation:
Callisto receives only 0.01 rem (0.1 mSv) per day, which is about 10 times the amount of background radiation on Earth. It is about 1.88 million kilometers from Jupiter.
Ganymede receives 5-8 rem (50–80 mSv) per day, which is about 500-800 times the amount of radiation on Callisto, despite being about 1.07 million kilometers from Jupiter, nearly 57% of the distance from Callisto to Jupiter.
Europa receives 540 rem (5.4 Sv) per day, which is about 67.5-108 times the amount of radiation on Ganymede and 54,000 times the radiation on Callisto, despite being at about 0.671 million kilometers from Jupiter, which is nearly 63% of the distance from Ganymede to Jupiter and nearly 36% of the distance from Callisto.
Io receives 3600 rem (36 Sv) per day, roughly 6.7 times as much as Europa, 450-720 times as much as Ganymede, and 360,000 times as much as Callisto. It is about 0.422 million kilometers from Jupiter, which is nearly 63% of the distance from Jupiter to Europa, more than 39% of the distance from Jupiter to Ganymede, and more than 22% of the distance from Jupiter to Callisto.
It might seem obvious that some are much further away from the source of the radiation, which is mainly Jupiter, than others - except that the difference in distance doesn't seem like enough to explain the drastic differences in the amount of radiation each moon receives.
Using the Inverse Square Law, which radiation follows, and knowing that Callisto receives 0.01 rem (0.1 mSv) per day, based on the distance you would predict that Ganymede would receive around 0.03 rem (0.3 mSv) per day, Europa would receive around 0.075 rem (0.75 mSv) per day, and Io would receive around 0.2 rem (2 mSv) per day. If you work off Io's 3600 rem (36 Sv) per day, then you would get about 1364 rem (13.6 Sv) per day for Europa, about 55 rem (0.55 Sv) per day for Ganymede, and about 18 rem (0.18 Sv) per day for Callisto. These values, however, are extremely far off from the actual ones.
As far as I know, there are no other radiation sources that would cause this much variation. So, why do some of the Galilean moons have so much less radiation than others, significantly more than could be explained by their distance from Jupiter?
