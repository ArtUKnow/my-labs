Computing eclipses and shadow transits of Jupter's moons and the light-time - Meeus, Ch.44?
I am confused by a single statement at the end of Chapter 44 "Satellites of Jupiter", J. Meeus, Astronomical Algorithms, 2nd ed. In the last paragraph, on p.315, he says this:
Eclipses and shadow transits can be calculated in the same way, except
that one should replace $X$ and $Y$ by the apparent coordinates $X_0$ and
$Y_0$, as seen from the Sun. These coordinates are obtained by putting $R = 0$
in expressions (44.1). Moreover, the light-time $\tau$ to the Earth should be added to the true times of the eclipses or to those of the
shadow transits, because we on Earth see these events later by the
amount $\tau$.
OK - all is clear, except for the "the light-time $\tau$ to the Earth should be added" part.
Earlier in the same chapter, p.305, there is this paragraph:
Let t be the time measured in ephemeris days from 1976 August 10 at 0h TD = JDE 2443000.5, decreased by the light-time $\tau$. In other words, if JDE is the Julian Ephemeris Day corresponding to the given instant,
$$t = JDE - 2443000.5 — \tau$$
Does he mean that that $\tau$ should now be added, instead of subtracted? If so, why? What is so different from the satellite positions proper?
If not so, then what exactly does he mean? Is he referring to something else?
Or is it just a redundant re-statement of what has already been prescribed by the above formula, i.e. $\tau$ is "added to $t$" by virtue of being subtracted from the other side of the equation? Why would he repeat that and add emphasis?
