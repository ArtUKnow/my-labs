Accessing detailed bolide trajectory/spectroscopy data
I was reading about the interstellar asteroid CNEOS 2014-01-08 and learned that the US Space Force Command is able to track asteroids and meteors and that they share some of their data with NASA. The Fireballs API appears to provide some very high level data on where and when various bolides were detected, but little in the way of raw data that could be useful for origin analysis science. The following paragraph from this paper provides one example of what I mean by "useful"...
... interstellar meteors ...
are optimal for a systematic study of physical extrasolar material (in addition to the spectroscopy of the hot gases as the meteor burns up). Since we expect interstellar meteors of this size to strike the Earth at least a few times per year, a network of all-sky camera systems monitoring the sky above all land on Earth could detect an interstellar meteor of this size every few years. Such detections can be made with science-grade video cameras, such as those used in AMOS, CAMO, and CAMS6 (Toth et al. 2015; Weryk et al. 2013).
We therefore advocate for a network of all-sky camera
systems to conduct real-time remote spectroscopy of the
hot gases as r ≥ 5 × 10−2 m interstellar meteors burn
up, and to precisely determine their trajectories for the
immediate retrieval of interstellar meteorite samples.
Also, as sky surveys improve, smaller and smaller asteroids are regularly being discovered. Potentially, with early detection, more advanced telescopes and radars could be alerted to improve the quality of the collected data.
Is this kind of data already out there? If so, can a citizen scientist to get their hands on it?
