Maximum stellar flux for a volcanically active CO₂-dominated planet to retain water
This question is based on the assumption that, during the first several hundred million years, a sufficiently massive planet undergoes vigorous volcanic activity that produces a predominantly carbon dioxide atmosphere with a pressure of several megapascals. Such conditions (along with other gases, including water) would generate a strong greenhouse effect. Under these circumstances, what is the highest stellar flux the planet could receive without its potential oceans being lost to space over hundreds of millions of years of intense volcanism? Changes in stellar luminosity over that timescale are neglected. The planet’s mass is also likely to play an important role, as it influences both the duration of volcanic activity and the planet’s gravitational strength and thus its capacity to retain water. Might the magnitude of the greenhouse effect depend strongly on the star’s spectrum? If so, the question is restricted to spectral classes G and K.
