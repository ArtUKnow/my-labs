Could Black Hole Jets Help Recycle Matter Back into the Universe?
I'm not a physicist, just someone interested in space and science. I’ve read a lot of articles over the years and often come here to learn. Recently, I saw a newspaper article talking about a paper published in the Journal of Cosmology and Astroparticle Physics (JCAP) by Heino Falcke and others (https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1475-7516/2025/05/023/pdf). It made me think — and also left me with some serious questions.
Part 1: My Concerns About That Paper
The article said that some scientists now think the universe will slowly fade into nothing — a so-called "heat death" — much earlier than people used to think. They believe that black holes, white dwarfs, and neutron stars will eventually evaporate over time and leave behind only cold, low-energy radiation. They estimate this could happen in about 10^78 years.
That’s a big idea. But what bothers me is that it’s based on something that hasn’t been proven yet — Hawking radiation. We’ve never observed a black hole evaporating or seen this kind of radiation in real life. And the theory doesn’t really explain what happens to all the matter black holes have swallowed. Doesn’t the law of conservation of energy and matter still apply? Shouldn’t that matter go somewhere?
Part 2: My Idea – A Recycle Cycle
So here’s what I started thinking:
What if black holes aren’t just one-way doors that suck in
everything forever?
What if, after reaching a certain point, they release some of that
energy and matter back into space?
What if those plasma jets we see shooting out of black holes are
part of this process?
We already know that many black holes shoot out huge jets of plasma — some stretching thousands of light-years. These jets contain charged particles and interact with the gas and dust in galaxies. Some scientists even believe they help form new stars. That made me wonder: could black holes be like pressure cookers that, when they get too full, vent off material into space? Could this be part of a larger cycle where black holes help “recycle” matter into the universe over time?
Before I get to my specific questions, there’s something else I’m trying to understand:
If Hawking radiation is real and black holes eventually evaporate completely, how does that fit with the law of conservation of matter and energy? Is there a conflict between Hawking’s theory and Lavoisier’s principle that "nothing is lost, nothing is created, everything is transformed"? If not, how are both ideas supposed to work together?
What I’m Wondering:
Has anyone proposed that black holes might recycle matter through
their jets, instead of just swallowing everything?
Are there any studies that show black hole jets helping to create or feed nebulae?
Could the amount of material or energy in these jets depend on how
big or full the black hole gets — like a safety valve releasing
pressure?
Would this idea clash with what we currently believe about how the
universe is supposed to end?
Could it be that Hawking’s theory is wrong, and instead of evaporating, black holes act as cosmic fertilizers — gradually returning matter and energy to the universe through their jets, in accordance with conservation laws like Lavoisier’s?
I know this idea might sound simple or even naive, but I’m honestly curious. It seems to make sense based on what we see black holes doing and how nature tends to reuse and recycle things.
Thanks in advance for any thoughts or feedback!
