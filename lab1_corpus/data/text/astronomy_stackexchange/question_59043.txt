Why is there a vector dot product term in the denominator in the definition for specific intensity?
I'm reading section 2.3 in Sara Seager's Exoplanet Atmospheres, and have encountered the definition for specific intensity as such:
“The intensity $I$ is formally defined as the amount of energy passing through a surface area $dA$, within a differential solid angle $d\Omega$ centered about $\hat{n}$, per frequency interval, per unit time”
which gives us the definition:
My question is, why bother including the dot product, instead of just using $I(\vec x, \hat{n }, \nu, t)= \frac{dE}{dAd\Omega d\nu dt}$? I mean, $\hat n$ is already included among the variables for $I$ (as we have $I(\vec x, \hat{n}, \nu, t)$), and $\hat k$ is just the normal vector of dA, which should be possible to infer from $\vec x$, given the geometry of the object. So technically you can shove any difference made by the absence of $\hat{n}\cdot \hat{k}$ into a different-valued $I(\vec x, \hat{n }, \nu, t)$.
To dicuss with something specific, let's take a random surface element radiating energy in all directions and try to describe it. Let's then look at a specific direction of $\hat{n_0}$ where the angle between $\hat{n_0}$ and $\hat{k}$ is $60^{\circ}$, which means $\hat{n_0}\cdot\hat{k}=\frac 1 2$. Now, since we're dealing with the same radiating surface element, $\frac{dE}{dAdtd\nu d\Omega}$ is just the same for both definitions, and we have $I(\vec x, \hat{n_0}, \nu, t)_{definition with the dot product}=2\cdot I(\vec x, \hat{n_0}, \nu, t)_{no dot product}$.
With the example above, my question then equates to this: as for describing the innate property of our radiating element, why is it more intuitional or fundamental to opt for the dot product and have the two-fold $I(\vec x, \hat{n_0}, \nu, t)$ at $<\hat{n_0}, \hat{k}>=60^\circ$?
I'm dimly aware that the dot product is there to eliminate the influence of the $I(\vec x, \hat{n}, \nu, t)$ definition being geometry or directional dependent or something, though I would really appreciate if someone may provide a more lucid argument, and preferably with the example I had provided above.
Any help is appreciated!
