The final-parsec-problem and rogue planets
I am citing what I found on the internet - sorry if this is not accurate, I am just a astronomical layman.
Two merging supermassive black holes can't bridge the final gap of around one parsec because the dynamical friction (ejection of massive bodies near the SMBH's orbits) runs out of steam due to the desertion of the area of required massive bodies, and because at this distance the energy loss to gravitational waves is not yet large enough to effect a meaningful convergence of the orbits.
The first argument (dynamical friction) though is an assumption based on the observed baryonic matter in common central galactic regions I suppose. BUT we have strong evidence of a boatload of unobservable massive objects, namely rogue planets of quite noticeable mass, a substantial number of which should have galactic orbits not as well-behaving and convergent to the bulk of stars, as these planets were ejected from their home systems. What if enough of these planets trickle down into the central region to finally reach the SMBH's orbit to be flung out into eternity, thus steadily supplying the SMBH's with ejection mass? How many Jupiters would be necessary for the final parsec?
