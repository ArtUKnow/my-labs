Detecting the Extended Nature of objects via Orbital Dynamics?
Background
So Kepler's second law of equal areas is a consequence of the conservation of angular momentum:
$$L = I \omega$$
where $L$ is the angular momentum, $I$ is the momentum of inertia and $\omega$ is the angular velocity.
or:
$$ L = M r^2 \dot \theta^2$$
where $M$ is the mass, $r$ is the radius and $\dot \theta$ is the angular coordinate derivate.
My concern is over here the moment of inertia tensor is treats the astrophysical object as a point like object. However, if we treat the astrophysical object of radius $a$ then I have:
$$ L = (1 +\frac{2}{5}\Big(\frac{a}{r} \Big)^2) Mr^2 \dot \theta^2 $$
where $a$ is the radius.
This is a negligible effect and almost impossible to detect for planets. This gets even more trickier in relativity since it only affects point like objects. However, if I assume
$\frac{2}{5}\Big(\frac{a}{r} \Big)^2$ is the limiting case of a correction term of some more complicated theory.
Question
Is there any promising data of astrophysical objects where where this effect is not concealed by the error?
