Bothering consequence of particle horizon calculation independent from the scale factor definition for the present cosmic time
Initially, I was wondering how would the particle horizon calculation change after redefining the scale factor from its present value of $1$ to $1$ at the time of the CMB emission, and from $1/(1100+1)$ at the time of the CMB emission to $1100+1$ at the present time. It wouldn't change at all due to the formula
$$d(t_0)=a(t_0)\int_{0}^{t_0}cdt/a(t)$$
where $t_0$ is the age of the universe. My redefinition is just additional scaling of the original scale factor $a(t)$ by $1100+1$ that cancels out with the same factor outside the integral, so the result of this calculation is exactly the same. It doesn't depend on my chosen value for $a(t_0)$, but now there is something else that bothers me.
We get $a(t)$ by solving the Friedmann equations, so we know every ratio of two scale factor values at different times. Particularly, we know the ratio $a(t_0)/a(t_P)$ where $t_P$ is the Planck time. Now I'm making a tiny change in the proper distance calculation
$$d(t_0)=a(t_0)\int_{t_P}^{t_0}cdt/a(t)$$
I've changed the bottom limit of integration from $0$ to $t_P$, which is so tiny that $t_0-t_P$ period of time can be very well approximated by $t_0$. The ratio $a(t_0)/a(t_P)$ is irrelevant to this calculation. Let me repeat. It's irrelevant to the proper distance calculation of the observable universe radius or particle horizon accounting for both the expansion and light travel, how many times the universe has expanded during $t_0-t_P$ period of time that can be very well approximated by $t_0$.
It doesn't matter whether the universe has expanded $\times 10^{50}$ or $\times 10^{100}$ since the Planck time. We get the same result of the calculation of the observable universe radius, whatever its current size is.
Why do we assume zero scale factor at the time of the BB? There wasn't even such time, because there was no time, nor space at that event. If I'm wrong and spacetime already existed at the BB, then the universe already had its initial size, but we don't define the scale factor to be $1$ instead of $0$ at the BB, because we would have no idea what $a(t_0)$ should be in this case.
