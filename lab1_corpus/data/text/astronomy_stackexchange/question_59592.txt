What are the chances that a star currently in the Sun's galactic neighbourhood will eventually revisit the Sun in the distant future?
There are thousands of known stars visible to the naked eye, and have been mapped in constellations for millennia.
However, the stars that exist in our neighbourhood are currently there by chance, and we are seeing an incredibly small snapshot in time.
After just a few million years, our night skies will be completely unrecognisable, with most of the stars visible to the naked eye today having receded hundreds or even thousands of lightyears away, far out of view of the human eye, and instead a whole host of new stars, some of which will not even currently be known to science, will be in our neighbourhood and visible in the night sky.
On the timescale of the Sun's life, the solar neighbourhood is incredibly dynamic and constantly changing.
With this in mind, out of the several hundred stars within, say, 100 lightyears of us, what are the chances that at least one of these stars will eventually in the distant future visit the Solar neighbourhood once more, and be within 100 lightyears of the Sun again, and how long is it likely to take? If we gave near-infinite time, then all of them (or their stellar corpses) will eventually revisit, and the question becomes boring! So to make it more interesting, we will say within the Sun's remaining lifetime, which we'll define here as the remainder of the Sun's main sequence, and it's post-main sequence phase, so around 7.9 billion years into the future.
