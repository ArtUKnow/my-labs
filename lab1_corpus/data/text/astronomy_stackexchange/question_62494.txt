Could the Milky Way be hiding our neighbors?
I've recently been working on a project cataloguing and mapping the Local Group. Once I got to IC 10 and it's numerous dubious/highly imprecise distance measurements using the TRGB method, shifted by the thin Cepheus-area Milky Way's interference, it made me wonder. Could there be any relatively massive (as in WLM or Fornax dwarf at the very least) galaxies, gravitationally bound to the Local Group, hidden behind the much thicker, central, low declination areas of the Milky Way? Have radio-astronomers been able to rule this out?
