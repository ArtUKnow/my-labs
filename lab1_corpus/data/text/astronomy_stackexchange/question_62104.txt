Possible explanations for why majority of spiral galaxies were observed to have similar rotation
In a paper, The distribution of galaxy rotation in JWST Advanced Deep Extragalactic Survey, detailing the rotation of 263 spiral galaxies, Shamir reported observations of a clockwise rotation in about two-thirds of these galaxies. A plausible explanation given was that the universe could have an angular momentum i.e. it rotates. Another explanation is that due to the Doppler shift effect galaxies that rotate in the opposite direction relative to the Milky Way, the results seen is therefore, an observational bias.
My questions are as follows:
Is there a way of figuring out which of these two possibilities are more likely e.g. through more observations i.e. a larger data set? Or could there be other possibilities/explanations?
Related to the first possibility, the paper suggested that one explanation is that our universe exists within a black hole. I wonder if the Big Bounce hypothesis could also helped explain why the universe had an angular momentum to begin with? Meaning the rotation was simply carried over from the Big Crunch to the Big Bang and so on; adhering to the conservation of angular momentum.
