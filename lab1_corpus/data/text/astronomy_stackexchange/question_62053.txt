Shortest wavelength with diffraction-limited telescopes
In many cases telescope resolution is diffraction-limited and no further improvement of mirror polish will improve image quality. Even ground based systems if they use adaptive optics to overcome atmospheric turbulence.
This is also true for longer wavelengths of light such as radio. These frequencies are low enough to simulate a larger telescope by combining their time-domain signals.
But x-ray telescopes are limited by the accuracy of the materials. The wavelength is so short that reaching the diffraction limit is very hard.
Where is the cutoff? What is the shortest wavelength of light for which an image-forming diffraction-limited telescope has been built? Are there plans to push this to lower wavelengths?
