Could the Universe itself be spinning?
I'm having a bit of a disagreement with a peer of mine and wanted to get some external input. With the recent study by Szigeti and Co. claiming the entire Universe could be rotating, a debate has come up about whether or not this is even possible. My peer claims it is completely impossible because rotation requires one object to be relative to another object, and since the Universe can't be relative to another object, it can't be rotating. I am unsure where my peer came by this belief, but by my understanding, all that is required for an object to rotate is for it to have a net angular momentum when it is formed, which is why pretty much everything in the Universe rotates. The presence of another object relative to a rotating object can influence said rotation, but is not required for an object to rotate. Sadly, neither of us are professional scientists and have been struggling to find research papers that settle the matter one way or the other. Would anyone be able to provide references to peer-reviewed sources that cover this topic?
EDIT 1 (05/09/2025): For some extra clarification, this is in reference to the recent paper published by Szigeti and Co. titled "Can Rotation Solve the Hubble Puzzle?". The paper proposes the possibility that a slowly rotating universe could resolve the current "Hubble Tension". The disagreement between myself and my peer is primarily about whether the argument makes logical sense, regardless of whether it is provable or not.
