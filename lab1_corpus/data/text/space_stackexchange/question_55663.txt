Pre-deorbit explosive disassembly of the ISS is possible using propellant. Is this a useful way to reduce smaller debris impacts?
The ISS could be explosively disassembled post retro-burn to provide smaller pieces for burn-up. If the hull space were filled with a stoichiometric mix of propellant from the deorbiting "tug" vessel, it would auto-detonate on re-entry.
The last crew would depower and passivate the ISS, and leave it vented to space at the attachment point of the arriving "tug". The "tug" would latch on, with its thrust axis aligned with the ISS center of mass. After the de-orbit burn, an appropriate mix of remaining propellant gas (?methane and oxygen?) would be vented into the cold, dead interior of ISS. The mixture would ignite spontaneously when the pressure hull burned through upon re-entry.
If Mr. Musk volunteers one of his company's vehicles for the task, a generous supply of methane and oxygen could be available.
The proposed self-demolition would improve the fireworks display, but would it significantly affect the mass and number of smaller fragments reaching Earth's surface? Discrete dense parts such as over-wrapped pressure vessels and rocket engines will reach the surface regardless of any de-orbit strategy, but what about the large number of other fragments? Satellite busses are designed for controlled fragmentation during re-entry so as to reduce the number and size of fragments reaching the ground.
