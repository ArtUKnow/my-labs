Would a hybrid engine with fuel-side full-flow staged combustion and a gas generator for the oxidizer pump be feasible in a hydrolox engine?
Fuel-rich staged combustion offers excellent performance for hydrolox engines, but it introduces a serious engineering challenge: the seal between the fuel-rich preburner turbine and the LOX turbopump. This so-called “interseal” must separate extremely high-pressure hydrogen from liquid oxygen — any leakage risks catastrophic failure. In engines like the SSME (RS-25), this seal was notoriously problematic.
To avoid the dangers and complexity of oxidizer-rich turbines (which are also difficult due to hot, corrosive oxygen-rich gas), a hybrid engine architecture could be a viable alternative:
On the fuel side, use full-flow staged combustion: all liquid hydrogen (LH₂) passes through a fuel-rich preburner and turbine before entering the combustion chamber.
On the oxidizer side, use a simple gas generator to power the LOX turbopump, dumping the exhaust rather than routing it into the combustion chamber.
Because LH₂ has a much lower density, the fuel turbopump in hydrolox engines does most of the heavy lifting. As one NASA source put it, in the SSME:
high-pressure fuel turbopump alone delivered as much horsepower as 28 locomotives, while its high-oxidizer turbopump delivered the equivalent horsepower for 11 more
(from ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20120001539/downloads/20120001539.pdf)
This suggests that a significant portion of the performance gain from full-flow staged combustion comes from applying it to the fuel side, where most of the energy is concentrated. A hybrid architecture might retain ~70% of the benefits of FFSC while avoiding the biggest technical hurdles.
So my question is:
Has such an engine cycle — using full-flow staged combustion for the fuel side and a gas generator for the oxidizer side — ever been proposed, studied, or built? What would be the likely trade-offs in terms of efficiency, complexity, or reliability?
