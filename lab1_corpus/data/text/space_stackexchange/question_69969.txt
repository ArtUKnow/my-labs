What are the attitude dynamics of the ISS during a Cygnus reboost?
The International Space Station requires regular reboosting due to its relatively low orbit causing high atmospheric drag. Most of these boosts are performed by the Russian Progress supply vehicle. This vehicle docks to the rearmost port of the station, which makes for very convenient boosting since it is already oriented perfectly to provide the required prograde thrust.
In recent years, the Cygnus cargo vehicle has also demonstrated reboost capability. However, this vehicle berths to the Unity Nadir port, which presents two interesting differences.
First: it is oriented incorrectly to provide prograde thrust. I am guessing that the station needs to rotate 90 degrees to orient its nadir port into the retrograde direction so that Cygnus is oriented properly. Can this be confirmed? Does the station use its normal control moment gyros for this? How far in advance does it do the attitude maneuver? How long does the rotation take? Does this have any other implications on the station's operations, such as any changes to received power due to reorienting the solar arrays?
Second, during the boost maneuver itself, how do they avoid having Cygnus impart torques on the station? How close is the station center of mass to the Unity port? Does Cygnus fire any of its attitude thrusters to assist with this? Is there an issue with the control moment gyros saturating?
