Are there actually any good sources of usable energy on Mars?
When talking about colonising Mars, talk of natural resources is usually focused on elements and molecules; We talk about how to find water, how to make metals and cement, where to get soil nutrients for plants, etc. What we do not often talk about, though, is energy, which would seem to be foolish, as the functional abundance of any given material is related to the amount of energy that can be used for processing and refining.
When we do start thinking about energy, though, it seems to me that Mars has very little available in usable forms; The thin atmosphere of Mars prevents wind power, yet blows enough dust around to make solar power inefficient, and Mars has only trace amounts of geothermal energy and fissile material...
Without developing nuclear fusion, I can't honestly see how a Mars colony would power itself without importing uranium or thorium from Earth. I do realise, though, that I may be wrong.
Are any of the energy sources I mentioned more likely to be effective on Mars than I thought? Is there an energy source I forgot? Or does Mars simply, as I consider likely, have no good native energy resources? I am eager to know.
