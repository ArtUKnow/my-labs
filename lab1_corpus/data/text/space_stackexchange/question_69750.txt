Could a Crewed Jupiter Mission use a Shadow Shield?
Inspired by this question.
The main radiation hazard in the Jupiter system is from charged particles. The charged particles are caught in Jupiter's magnetic field, and therefore have some kind of directionality; they flow in patterns that are driven by Jupiter's magnetic poles.
So: Is there enough directionality in the particle flow that a manned Jupiter mission could survive by placing shadow shields on the "hot" side (or sides) of the ship as their primary shielding?
The obvious candidate for this shielding would be a fission reactor, since it is very dense and would require dense shielding itself. (Possibly combined with a wide, thin, metal "umbrella" to provide a good shadow of particle free space near the ship.)
Obviously, attempting to land on one of the Galilean moons would require a change in orientation that would render the shadow shield ineffective. This is outside the scope of this question, but could be a separate question if there's interest.
