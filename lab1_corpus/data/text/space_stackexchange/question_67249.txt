Is an ablative/buoyant “lander” a reasonable design for a Jupiter probe?
It is difficult to design a “lander” for a Jupiter mission for several reasons:
There is no “land” to “land on” since Jupiter’s atmosphere just gets
denser towards the planetary center.
There is a literally astronomic amount of kinetic energy to be shed
as the descent vehicle arrives from orbit.
One possible design is to make the vehicle almost entirely from ablative heat shield material. The vehicle’s surface would ablate as it descended, until enough speed was lost that its trajectory became ballistic. At that point, it would fall until the ambient atmospheric density matched its own density. Once densities matched, the vehicle would be, in effect, a balloon tracking the isopycnal (constant density) contour.
The density of Carbon phenolic heat shield material is 1.31 - 1.55 g/cc
