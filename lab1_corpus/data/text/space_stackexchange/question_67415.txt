How can I determine the baud rate and bandwidth for a hypothetical satellite orbiting the Moon?
I am trying to calculate a link budget for a hypothetical Earth-Moon CubeSat. The parameters I am using are:
Distance: $384,400 \, \text{km}$
Frequency: $8.250 \, \text{GHz}$
Transmit Power ($ P_t $): $ 33 \, \text{dBm} $
Transmit Antenna Gain ($ G_t $): $ 18 \, \text{dBi}$
Receive Antenna Gain ($ G_r $): $ 74.66 \, \text{dBi} $ (Madrid 70 m Dish)
Miscellaneous Losses ($ L_{\text{misc}} $): $ 5.4 \, \text{dB}$
Data Rate: $ 1 \, \text{Mbps}$
Modulation: BPSK
Roll-Off Factor ($ \alpha $): $ 0.2 $
Noise Figure ($ NF $): $ 1 \, \text{dB} $
Here are my calculations:
Path Loss Calculation ($ L_p $):
$
L_p = 20 \log(384,400) + 20 \log(8.250) + 92.44 \approx 222.46 \, \text{dB}.
$
Received Power ($ P_r $):
Using the link budget equation:
$
P_r = P_t + G_t + G_r - L_p - L_{\text{misc}}
$
$
P_r = 33 + 18 + 74.66 - 222.46 - 5.4 = -101.2 \, \text{dBm}.
$
Baud Rate Calculation ($ f_{\text{baud}} $):
For BPSK:
$
f_{\text{baud}} = \frac{\text{Data Rate}}{\log_2(M)} = \frac{1 \, \text{Mbps}}{1} = 1 \, \text{MBaud}.
$
Bandwidth Calculation ($ BW $):
$
BW = f_{\text{baud}} \times (1 + \alpha)
$
$
BW = 1 \, \text{MBaud} \times 1.2 = 1.2 \, \text{MHz}.
$
However, I’ve also seen bandwidth calculated using Carson’s Rule as:
$
BW_{\text{transmission}} = 2 \Delta f + f_{\text{baud}}.
$
Which method is correct in this case?
Noise Power ($ P_n $):
I calculated the noise power as:
$
P_n = -174 \, \text{dBm/Hz} + 10 \log_{10}(BW) + NF
$
$
P_n = -174 + 10 \log_{10}(1.2 \times 10^6) + 1
$
$
P_n = -174 + 60.79 + 1 = -112.21 \, \text{dBm}.
$
Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR):
data rate is sufficient`?
$
SNR = P_r - P_n = -101.2 - (-112.21) = -101.2 + 112.21 = 11.01 \, \text{dB}.
$
The required SNR for BPSK is $ 9.6 \, \text{dB} $, so the link should work???
