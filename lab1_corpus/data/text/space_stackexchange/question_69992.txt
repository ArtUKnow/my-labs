Saving fuel by capturing the spacecraft with a vehicle at arrival
Let's say a spacecraft (SC) reached a planet but does not have any fuel left for orbit insertion around it and aerobraking is not possible.
Another spacecraft already on this orbit, call it arrival vehicle (A) carrying additional fuel, is accelerated to rendevous with SC and dock such that both can now get into orbit.
Investigating, when this catching maneuver can save fuel, compared to the usual way where the SC itself carries the fuel for insertion, I found that this is beneficial if and only if
$$\Delta v_\text{start} > \Delta v_\text{insert},$$
where $\Delta v_\text{start}$ is the $\Delta v$ required to reach the planet (all the burns that happend before the insertion along its trip) and $\Delta v_\text{insert}$ to only get into orbit.
Is my result legit? Has a similar maneuver ever been considered or are there better ways to save fuel during orbit insertion?
Calculations:
Assume instant burns, let $c=\frac{1}{g_0 I_\text{sp}}$, where $g_0$ is standard gravity and $I_\text{sp}$ the specific impulse. According to the rocket equation the fuel mass $f$ required, for a spacecraft of dry mass $M$, to execute a maneuver with given $\Delta v$ is
$$f = M (\exp(c \Delta v )-1).$$
Let $M_\text{SC}$, $M_\text{A}$ be dry mass of the spacecraft and the arrival vehicle.
Doing this the conventional way we get for the fuel mass required
\begin{align}
f_\text{old} &= f_\text{SCinsert} + f_\text{wetstart}\\
f_\text{SCinsert} &= M_\text{SC} (\exp( c\Delta v_\text{insert} )-1)\\
f_\text{drystart} &= M_\text{SC} (\exp(c \Delta v_\text{start} )-1) \\
f_\text{wetstart}&= f_\text{SCinsert} (\exp(c \Delta v_\text{start})-1) + f_\text{drystart}
\end{align}
Using the catching we have
\begin{align}
f_\text{new} &= f_\text{rendevous} + f_\text{Ainsert} + f_\text{SCinsert} + f_\text{drystart} \\
f_\text{Ainsert} &= M_\text{A} (\exp(c\Delta v_\text{insert})-1) \\
f_\text{rendevous} &= (f_\text{Ainsert} + f_\text{SCinsert}) (\exp(c\Delta v_\text{insert})-1) + f_\text{Ainsert}
\end{align}
In comparison
\begin{align}
f_\text{old} - f_\text{new} &= f_\text{wetstart} - f_\text{rendevous} - f_\text{Ainsert} - f_\text{drystart}\\
&= f_\text{SCinsert} (\exp(c \Delta v_\text{start})-1) - 2f_\text{Ainsert} - (f_\text{Ainsert} + f_\text{SCinsert}) (\exp(c\Delta v_\text{insert})-1) \\
&= f_\text{SCinsert} \left(\exp(c \Delta v_\text{start}) - \exp(c\Delta v_\text{insert})\right) - f_\text{Ainsert} (\exp(c\Delta v_\text{insert})+1),
\end{align}
which means for negligible vehicle dry mass, we save fuel if and only if $\Delta v_\text{start} > \Delta v_\text{insert}$.
