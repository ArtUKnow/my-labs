Lambert's problem without collisions
Given two fixed positions in space, a source of gravity and a time of flight to travel, the theory about Lambert's problem provides a way to calculate corresponding Keplerian orbital transfers.
But, if and only if the central body is a point source! When we assume a body of a certain radius, in some cases, there does not seem to be a valid transfer (including pro/retrograde & multi-revs) not going through the central body (see white patches in the Porkchop plot below).
So, how to extend/constraint a Lambert solver (e.g. Izzo's) to always get a valid transfer not colliding with the body, or a valid transfer at all?
Thanks in advance!
Remark: For example the Juno spacecraft gets extremely close to Jupiter during its flybys.
Ideas I was contemplating:
modify solution space (Lambert is an inverse but not a minimization problem, so that's not gonna work)
somehow force multiple revs (usually depending on time of flight and orbits might get even tighter)
make non-Keplerian transfers (not familiar with these)
add another point and patch two valid Lambert transits together (doable, but how to pick a point always leading to two valid sub-transfers?)
