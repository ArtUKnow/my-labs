Switching to Earth-centered satellite trajectory propagation using JPL ephemerides
With the holidays coming up, I have some free time and would like to continue working on an orbit propagator I’m developing in Python.
Current setup:
Planetary and lunar positions are taken from JPL SPICE ephemerides for each timestep done in the propagation.
I integrate the full n-body equations of motion to propagate a spacecraft in the ICRF, with the Solar System Barycenter (SSB) as the origin (so per definition a strictly inertial frame)
Only point-mass gravity is modeled, no additional perturbations.
My main focus is the cislunar region and the influence of other planets on the trajectory of a spacecraft
I’ve been struggling a while with the following issue and would appreciate your thoughts:
I would like to change the propagation frame from the SSB to an Earth-centered frame due to the fear of numerical issues. Since propagating relative to the SSB involves very large distances, I’m concerned about round-off errors from dividing by large $r^3$ terms. Using Earth as the origin may improve numerical robustness.
Using SPICE, this is straightforward, as it returns state vectors relative to any body while keeping the axes aligned with the ICRF.
My concern is conceptual:
Is this Earth-centered frame still inertial, and is it valid to integrate the equations of motion in it? For one, Earth is "wobbling" around due to the gravitational acceleration of the n-bodies, then there are other effects modeled in SPICE such as tides, viscous effects etc. This is the equation according to Vallado:
$$\newcommand{\rv}[1]{\vec{\mathbf{r}}_{#1}} \newcommand{\rm}[1]{r^3_{#1}} \ddot{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}_{1s} = -\frac{G(m_1+m_s) \, \rv{1s}}{\rm{1s}} + G\sum_{j=3}^n m_j \left( \frac{\rv{sj}}{\rm{sj}} - \frac{\rv{1j}}{\rm{1j}} \right)$$
Does this mean that propagating in an Earth-centered frame (or any other planet) using SPICE ephemerides would, in principle, introduce errors and yield an incorrect spacecraft trajectory compared to an SSB-centered integration, as it is not an inertial system?
At least from my results so far, I get initially similar, but in the end very different trajectories for the propagation in the different origins. I don't know and can't say yet why this is (numerical round-off being different? Accelerations of the Earth why make the frame non-inertial?), which is the reason I am posting here.
