Would large fauna besides humans make a CELSS more stable?
The Biosphere II project showed us - by failing - that building a CLESS (Closed Ecological Life Support System) is very difficult, although (obviously) one failure does not prove impossibility.
I have read (although I do not remember where!) that one of the reasons that the project failed was that, since humans were the only large fauna in the system, and humans live a very long time, there was very little decomposition of animal tissue going on, which meant that those biochemical cycles which rely on animal decomposition to function were severely hampered.
If the hypothesis I have described in the above paragraph is true, it would seem to me that adding some kind of large but relatively short-lived fauna should make the CLESS more stable and less likely to fail, but I cannot be sure.
To summarise the question, then: Would adding some kind of large but relatively short-lived non-human animal to a Closed Ecological Life Support System make the system's biochemical cycles less likely to break down?
