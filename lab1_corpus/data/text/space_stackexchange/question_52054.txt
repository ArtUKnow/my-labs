What are the advantages and disadvantages of certain radial symmetries in spacecraft construction?
I'm mainly considering the merits and drawbacks of threefold versus fourfold (or higher, like sixfold and eightfold) radial symmetries when it comes to the construction of a fuselage for a spacecraft designed for interplanetary trips.
It appears to me that fourfold construction (or a multiple of it) is optimal, barring going for a completely cylindrical shape for optimal surface area to volume ratio. This is particularly pertinent to a reaction control system perspective, wherein it seems trivially easy to simply place thrusters at 90 degrees to each other with no real need for directional thrust control, creating a system with fewer moving parts and thus a lower failure rate, though appearances can be deceiving.
This is just one possible advantage, though, and there could be other advantages to a threefold or sixfold radial symmetry construction that aren't immediately apparent to me that are beneficial enough to warrant choosing them over a simplified RCS.
