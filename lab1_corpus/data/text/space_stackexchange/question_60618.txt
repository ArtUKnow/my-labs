If anything, weren't the first people in orbit the first who broke the sound barrier in free fall?
Felix Baumgartner and Alan Eustace are said to have broken the sound barrier during their space jumps. But in space, there is no sound. In fact, they were still within the Earth's atmosphere so there would still be weak sound waves, but anyone who went to orbit orbited within the Earth's atmosphere as well. Therefore, Yuri Gagarin may be considered the first man who broke the sound barrier in free fall; he didn't do so in relation to his surrounding atmosphere, however.
Therefore, the first person to break the sound barrier in his own free fall would be the first orbital spacewalker Alexey Leonov. But if one doesn't count him because he was tethered to his spacecraft, the first person who broke the sound barrier in free fall would be Bruce McCandless, or not?
