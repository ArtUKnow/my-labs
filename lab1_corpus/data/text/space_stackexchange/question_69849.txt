Why don’t rotating detonation engines ignite detonation waves in both directions which then self-extinguish when they meet?
Rotating detonation engines (RDE) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotating_detonation_engine are annular and symmetric. At ignition, the detonation front propagates in both directions. When they meet 180* later, they should extinguish as they attempt to propagate into the “exhaust” of the fellow detonation front.
How do RDE establish a single stable propagation front? And how is the direction of that single front determined ?
The illustrations below are from https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/70511 . They show the cylindrical combustion chamber “unwrapped”. Unfortunately, the detonation wave is travelling in opposite directions in the two sets of diagrams, which can be a bit confusing. The areas of interest are “A” (detonation front) and “F” (blocked injectors). The injectors are blocked since the post detonation pressure is much higher than the propellant pump pressure. In fact, combustion gasses are forced backwards into the injectors. In both models, it requires a significant proportion of the cycle to re-introduce propellant into the combustion chamber.
A second detonation wave travelling in the opposite direction would need to “jump the firewall”. Two equal detonation waves meeting head-on would be expected to extinguish each other.
In the case of unequal waves meeting, the stronger wave could “jump the smaller firewall” and extinguish the weaker wave. This would favor designs which have a strong directional bias for ignition, as illustrated in @ Dragongeek's answer.
A is the primary detonation front
B is an oblique shock wave that propagates from the top of the detonation wave
C is a slip line between the freshly detonated products and older products from the previous cycle
D is a secondary shock wave
E is a mixing region between the fresh premixture and the product gases, where deflagration may occur
F is the region where the injector nozzles are blocked
G is the unreacted premixture.
There have been unreferenced reports of RDE maintaining sustained counter-rotating detonation fronts, so extinguishment is obviously not a deal-breaker problem.
