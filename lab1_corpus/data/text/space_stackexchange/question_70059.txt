What capabilities do Europa Clipper/Hera have to analyze the ion tail of 3I/ATLAS?
According to a few sources it is very likely that the Europa Clipper probe and the Hera planetary defense spacecraft will pass within the ion tail of interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS very soon (in the late-October to mid-November 2025 timespan).
This has been covered in a couple places (e.g. this preprint on arXiv, this space.com article referencing it, as well as a couple other sites), and is very interesting since it poses an opportunity to get the first ever direct measurements of stuff that came off/interacted directly with an interstellar object.
A while back it was proposed that the Juno spacecraft around Jupiter be sent to investigate very closely, but that depends on the engine having either a higher specific impulse than it probably does or a lot more fuel in the tank, and there was a question here a while back on the matter. Notably that paper was published by Avi Loeb, who is a fine astronomer but also prone to making extreme claims at times, including that it may pose a serious planetary defense problem and that it could actually be aliens.
So, Loeb aside, we will actually have a very good chance to make reasonably-direct observations of it before it leaves the solar system with Europa Clipper and Hera rather than the Juno spacecraft. So: What reasonable expectations can we have about what we could learn with these spacecraft when the ion tail hits them? Depending on what fraction of the ions actually came from the comet, there could be a significant amount of matter from another star system coming the way of those spacecraft; what specific interesting aspects of 3I/ATLAS could we expect them to be able to learn about, given the instruments onboard? And would this significantly advance our understanding of the comet from what we can already glean with telescopes?
While researching this a bit further, it turns out that the US government shutdown might affect whether or not Clipper can be brought online to actually make the measurements -- see the space.com article linked above -- so I am operating under the ideal assumption that either the US government will be funded again, or that it can still be activated regardless.
