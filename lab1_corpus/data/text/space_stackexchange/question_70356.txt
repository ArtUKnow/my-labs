How do Mars colonization plans address 0.38G being insufficient for healthy human development?
If humans plan to live on Mars long-term, how do proponents address the fact that Mars gravity (0.38g) is likely insufficient for healthy human development?
There's been a lot of excitement lately — particularly from high-profile figures in the private space sector — about the idea of humans not just visiting Mars, but actually living there permanently and raising generations of people on the surface.
I find myself puzzled by something that doesn't seem to get much attention in these conversations: Mars has roughly 38% of Earth's gravity (approximately 3.71 m/s² versus Earth's 9.81 m/s²). The human body evolved over millions of years under 1G. We already know from ISS research that even partial reductions in gravity cause measurable bone loss, muscle atrophy, cardiovascular changes, and vision problems — and that's in adults whose bodies are already fully formed.
My question is specifically about children and fetal development. What happens to a child conceived and born on Mars, growing up their entire life under 0.38G? Their skeletal system, musculature, cardiovascular system, and neurological development would all occur under gravitational conditions that our biology never adapted for. Would they even be capable of surviving a trip to Earth if they ever needed to?
I haven't seen the people championing permanent Mars colonization seriously grapple with this. It seems like a fundamental biological obstacle, not just an engineering problem you can throw money at.
Are there credible proposals or ongoing research that address:
The effects of 0.38G on fetal development and childhood growth?
Whether rotating habitats or centrifuges on the Martian surface could realistically provide 1G environments for pregnant women and children full-time?
Whether there is any scientific consensus (or even a working hypothesis) on a minimum gravity threshold for healthy human development?
I'm not asking whether adults can survive on Mars short-term — that's fairly well studied. I'm asking about multi-generational colonization specifically, and whether the gravity problem has a realistic solution that's actually being planned for.
Are "we" kidding ourselves?
