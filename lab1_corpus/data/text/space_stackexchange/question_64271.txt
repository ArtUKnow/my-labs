Are there any gaseous monopropellants?
There are numerous liquid monopropellants: Hydrazine, Hydrogen Peroxide, Nitrous Oxide, Isopropyl Nitrate, and more.
There are of course also solid monopropellants. (Edit: composite propellants not monopropellants.) The Space Shuttle used APCP in its solid boosters, but there are many other solid chemicals possible, even "Rocket Candy" using sugars.
However, I have never heard of a gaseous monopropellant.
Do they exist? Is there something in chemistry that makes this highly unlikely? Cannot imagine how or why and I am extremely curious about this.
Edit Note: Any substance can be a gas at the right temperature and pressure. I'm looking for gaseous substances at normal temperatures, and stored at relatively high pressure.
Note, cold gas thrusters don't count. Those have no chemical reaction whatsoever. A gaseous monopropellant has to decompose from a catalyst or else burn more conventionally like Isopropyl Nitrate.
