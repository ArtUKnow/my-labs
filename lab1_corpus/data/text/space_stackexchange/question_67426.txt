Ionospheric delay calculation
I can't figure out how to calculate ionospheric delay from GNSS measurements. People write everywhere that you can determine ionospheric delay quite accurately from the difference in pseudorange measurements for two signals:
$$\text{delay} = \frac{p_1 - p_2}{1 - \frac{f_2^2}{f_1^2}}$$
But when I try to do this with real data (that is, with measurements that I got directly from my receiver, or with data from RTCM messages that a third-party base station broadcasts) I see that, firstly, in order of magnitude this is no more than a few meters (Klobuchar model gives 10-20 meters) and, secondly, I see in real data that for GPS sometimes $p_1 > p_2$, and sometimes $p_2 > p_1$, that is, the delay that I calculate with this formula sometimes turns out to be negative. I am almost completely confident in my data: I checked with the data that my receiver gave me (.ubx) and with the data that I received in the RTCM message stream from a commercial operator. Why could this be a problem? What should I check?
