Does SpaceX have plans to utilize Starship to set up orbital infrastructure?
SpaceX has often expressed its ambition to send a million people to Mars and is developing Starship to launch them cost-effectively. However, I believe they might be overlooking the broader potential of space. Space offers numerous solutions that can significantly enhance life on Earth. Unfortunately, these possibilities are seldom discussed, and space travel is frequently associated with environmental harm, which necessarily have to be the case. Several issues such as Overpopulation, food scarcity, energy crisis, climate change, manufacturing, resource scarcity & launch costs have space based solutions. - See previous edit for more details.
Serval alternative launch systemss can provide quicker, easier and simpler access to space. I would like to emphasize that the planetary ring can be constructed extremely cheaply. An orbital ring would only require a starting cable, but once that is in place, more mass can be carried up using the existing infrastructure, making the rest of the construction cheaper. A sea cable costs around 25 000 dollars/ km making one that is 40,000 km long (Earth's circumference) about 1 billion dollars. With around 1.4 tons per km that is 56,000 tons or equivalent to 280 V2 Starship launches. With more than one launch per day, it can be completed in under a year. At 10 dollars per kg, the launch cost is 560 million dollars. So this is 1.2 billion dollars and the main infrastructure is in place. So the total is 1.56 billion dollars, and the main infrastructure is in place. Paul Birch did similar calculations in his paper about orbital rings and had a total cost of about 8 billion dollars using 1970s values. However, he was working with Space Shuttle price estimates and had other assumptions, such as a moon base being the main resource supplier.
Even with slightly different assumptions, the estimated price per kilogram using an orbital ring would be in the cent range, much less than even the most optimistic scenarios for Starship. In addition, they would be able to bring much more into orbit and could potentially deploy satellites multiple times within a couple of minutes.
