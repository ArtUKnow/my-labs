Feasibility of lithium or aluminum as a tripropellant liner for ablatively-cooled engines?
Studies have been previously conducted on the feasibility of using metal powder as a tripropellant in rocket engines (i.e. lithium/fluorine/hydrogen or aluminum/hydrolox). For example, this NASA study I found details possible combinations of tripropellants which use metals.
The current idea for tripropellant rocket engines involves the injection of the metal into the combustion chamber to increase specific impulse, which introduces technical challenges involving moving said metal from the fuel tanks to the engines: most metals are solids, and require high temperatures to liquefy them, and powders introduce additional risks/challenges.
We already have ablatively-cooled engines like the RS-68 which use silica/carbon phenolics as the ablator. These materials don't interact heavily with the rest of the exhaust, which overall hurts performance as extra mass is being used for the ablator. Why haven't tripropellant-based ablators been used/tested to boost performance?
