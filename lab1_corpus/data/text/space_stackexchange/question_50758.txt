Entering elliptical orbit at periapse
Consider a rocket entering an elliptical orbit from launch.
Say you want the rocket to enter exactly at periapse---not a few degrees ahead of it, not a few degrees behind it.
Can this be done?
I have the mathematical machinery to control a rocket launch to given altitude, velocity, orbital inclination, longitude of ascending node, and orbital eccentricity---but not argument of periapse, which would set the location of the periapse.
Between them, those variables make five unknowns to the six degrees of freedom of my rocket (three translational and three rotational), suggesting I have room to control one more variable.
That said, all the papers I've seen on orbital insertion seem to leave out the argument of periapse. Plus, the five variables I already control are the only variables targeted in the space shuttle papers that I've seen (sorry, I don't have links).
So I don't know if argument of periapse is a variable that I can target. But even if it isn't, it seems possible that it might be fully determined from the other variables over which I already have control... in which case all I need is an equation relating my desired argument of periapse to the other orbital variables.
Is there such an equation?
