Adhesives use in space need to withstand a large range of temperature. Low temperature (to -115F) is a particular challenge. Approved adhesives also need to meet strict standards of outgassing and radiation resistance. https://www.masterbond.com/techtips/adhesives-space-applications

Chewing gum’s high pliability allows it to flow into microscopic crevices, increasing contact area. https://www.sciencefocus.com/science/what-makes-chewing-gum-bond-so-well . Vacuum desiccation and low temperatures increase bond strength so, once applied, conditions in space could enhance adhesive performance. However, radiation exposure would be expected to degrade polymers and therefore weaken the gum matrix with time.
The adhesive properties of chewing gum are substantial, as demonstrated by this study https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/tdcpublication/57/1/57_1/_pdf However, when gum is pulled off a surface, most of the energy goes into stretching the polymers, rather than breaking the bonds between the surface and the gum. https://illumin.usc.edu/chewing-gum Presumably this is why frozen gum is easier to remove: reduced elasticity of the polymer transmits force to the gum/surface interface.
