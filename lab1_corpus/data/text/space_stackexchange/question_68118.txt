How (in)efficient would a rocket be that flew to orbital heights, hovered for a while, and then fell back down instead of going into orbit and back?
I understand in a vague and not mathematically rigorous way that most of the energy required to get a spacecraft into orbit goes into gaining "forward" speed, not actually in gaining altitude. Assuming my understanding is correct—could you save fuel on short flights by going straight up, hovering in place for a bit, then just falling back down, instead of ever bothering to get into orbit properly?
Obviously the longer you stay up, the worse this "quick hop" method will be, but I could see it being an efficient method if you only need, say, a few minutes in space to take atmosphere-clear pictures, or to drop passengers off with a larger, more capable spacecraft. For quick trips, does this actually save enough fuel to be worth it? If so, how long would it be efficient for—minutes? Hours? Days?
