How do satellite trackers work when showing a "satellite"?
There are multiple sat trackers online where you can follow spacecraft orbiting Earth such as the ISS or the HST. I wonder how those trackers actually work when showing something near Earth, as the tracker must know that the object in question actually orbits the Earth, i.e. that it's a satellite. So how do those satellite trackers actually work?
Do they just show about any object above a certain altitude (e.g. 80, 100 or 120 km), regardless of whether it's on an orbital, suborbital or escape trajectory? If so, does it also show natural objects such as flying-by meteoroids or the two temporary moons Earth had when they were in Earth's orbit?
Or do satellite trackers show any object beyond a minimum speed, e.g. 7.5 km/s (4.7 mi/s) or faster. If so, a minimum speed would need to count at a specific altitude only, as the necessary orbital speed gets lower the higher you go until no ground speed at all in geostationary orbit. If there's a speed limit, would the SpaceX Starship (which didn't reach full orbital speed) have already been shown on a satellite tracker?
And then I wonder how far away from Earth satellite trackers work. Is there an altitude/distance limit such as geosynchronous altitude (35800 km) or 1.5 million km (edge of Earth's hill sphere, L1 and L2) or a random number like 250 thousand km or miles?
I also wonder if there's an upper speed limit so to exclude objects at escape velocity. Again, this would need to be altitude-dependent.
