Can ISS astronauts collect space debris?
In the Booker Prize winning novel "Orbital" by Samantha Harvey, one of the ISS astronauts on a space walk does a "litter pick" before returning to the airlock.
How realistic is this? I would have thought that all the pieces of space junk would be flying around in different orbits and different speeds so the prospects of calmly picking up bits and pieces seems unlikely to me. Happy to be proved wrong though, if anyone has any insight into this.
Edit: Additional info.
As there have been some great responses asking about the origin of the debris (ie whether generated on that particular EVA), I went back to check the text:
You do a litter-pick of debris, plucking from space a few of those tens of thousands of remnants of jettisoned or exploded satellites and launch vehicles and craft......
So - yes, totally unrealistic but I guess the Booker judging committee doesn't include many scientists.
