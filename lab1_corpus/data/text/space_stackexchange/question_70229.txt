Would Voyager missions be realistic without gravitational slingshot?
I understand that a fair amount of the velocity of Voyagers are based on gravitational slingshot effect. For example, this Wikipedia animation shows increase from 13 km/s to 24 km/s after utilizing the slingshot effect of Jupiter.
In the future, a Voyager-like mission might be needed to send spacecraft to the focal point of Sun as a gravitational lens, about 550 AU away, more than 170 AU of Voyager 1. The problem is that not only is the focal point further away than anything sent by humans to space, also the focal point is dependent on the exoplanet to observe, so the direction the spacecraft is going to can't be optimized to give maximal gravitational slingshot effects; instead, it must go exactly to the opposite direction from the exoplanet.
So my question is: how feasible is sending a spacecraft to distances similar of Voyagers, with speeds similar to that of Voyagers, without using any gravitational slingshot effects?
