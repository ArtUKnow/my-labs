Would it be a good idea to use a quadruped robot on the next Mars exploration mission?
Quadruped robot technology is becoming increasingly mature, with commercial models demonstrating non-trivial off-road mobility that allows them to traverse complex and extreme terrains with ease. Compared to wheeled vehicles, these legged machines are less susceptible to issues like getting stuck or suffering wheel damage and wear;
We can envision a mission scenario where a quadruped robot equipped with a sampling manipulator and high-resolution camera is deployed alongside a traditional wheeled Mars rover on the Martian surface. The quadruped robot leverages its superior terrain adaptability to explore rugged terrain and collect samples, which are then transferred to the rover for analysis and testing. Upon completing investigations in one area, the robotic team proceeds to the next location via optimal routes. This approach eliminates sampling gaps caused by the rover's inability to access certain regions or geological features.
