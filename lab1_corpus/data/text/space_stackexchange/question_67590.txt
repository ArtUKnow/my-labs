Where in the world does GPS time proceed at one second per second? Is there a map?
The definition, measurement, and distribution of what time it is is a complicated thing, and only certain sturdy folks have the patience to understand it really and fully.
It suddenly dawned on me that GPS time (for example) is a purely artificial construct. Zillions of things on Earth depend on it to varying levels of precision. I think the internet and cellular data does, the Event Horizon Telescope interferometry does.
But each receiver reconstructs GPS time from several Doppler-shifted signals from things flying overhead constantly changing distance. GPS time doesn't exist until someone turns on a radio + computer and reconstructs it.
Yes folks will argue that GPS time is synchronized to terrestrial atomic clocks, but don't spoil my epiphany - if we blow up all of those clocks, GPS time will keep going.
Anyway. Suppose I have a really, really good but portable atomic clock and a GPS clock and a box that subtracts the two. If I go up on a mountain or visit the dead sea, I can get the difference to behave differently. Same with going fast or staying still, or moving from pole to equator.
Question: So I wonder; where in the world does GPS time proceed at one second per second?
Auxiliary questions to indicate the scope of the answer I'm hopeing is possible:
Is there a map?
Is it two wobbly 1-dimensional rings at +/- 40 degrees latitude, trying to stay near zero GPS elevation?
Is it one or more 2-dimensional surfaces?
How high above Earth's surface does it reach in places?
How far below ground?
