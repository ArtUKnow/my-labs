Why did this astronaut select this magazine issue to hold in his official photograph?
Theodore Freeman was a USAF test pilot selected into the third NASA astronaut group along with such luminaries as Cernan, Scott, and Collins. Freeman was one of the four astronauts in that group who died during astronaut training - he died on Halloween Day 1964 when his T-38 jet trainer collided with a flock of geese near Ellington Field, Texas. The surviving members of his astronaut group all flew Apollo missions, some flew Gemini missions.
I was waiting in the lobby of the library named after him in Clear Lake City, Texas where his official NASA photograph hangs. I hadn't really looked at it before but I did that day, and I noticed that he was holding a magazine in the photo. I took a photo of the portrait and cropped out that part:
Research showed that this is the June 29, 1964 issue of Aviation Week and Space Technology (AW&ST):
I expected to find that this issue announced his astronaut group or some such. But no. There are a couple of Gemini related articles, one about a test of a sensor for spacecraft charging, and one about ingress/egress testing of the Gemini spacecraft. My access to the AW&ST archives is limited since I let my subscription lapse a decade or so ago, but I could not find anything that related to Freeman.
So I'm baffled as to why he would have picked this issue of this magazine to hold in his official photograph. So the question is:
Why did Freeman choose to hold this magazine issue in his official NASA astronaut photo?
