Round-trip radio signals (delay (range) and Doppler (radial velocity)) measurements are fit with trajectory models based on propagating in the gravitational field of the solar system.
But in addition to this very accurate but single-axis line of sight two-way measurement, there is for example another technique called "Delta DOR" or "Delta differential" where a one-way signal (from spacecraft to tracking station) is received by generally two tracking stations who share a coherent timebase so that they can triangulate in a way that I think is similar to how cell towers triangulate. (Correct me if I'm wrong)

Tracking JWST very precisely is important for propellant conservation and mission lifetime. As I recall there's a sort-of regular (two week?) cadence for tiny propulsive corrections of order cm/sec. To do this, deviations of JWST from it's planned halo orbit must be precisely measured.
