How do Sea Dragon style launches affect the Nozzle?
The Sea Dragon was a concept rocket to be towed empty to its marine launch-site, where it would be filled and turned upright through the weight of its propellant and some ballast. "Then it launches."
I don't understand what launching under water does to a nozzle and what steps are necessary to avoid failure.
How does the launch-procedure for a nozzle submerged in water look like? Does it require special nozzles? Are there restrictions on the engine? (such as regenerative cooling freezing the water during startup?) Is turbine-spinup via propellant flow even possible?
Background on why/what I'm asking:
I found no detail on whether the nozzle would get in touch with water, the closest being Wikipedia stating
The ballast tanks, which also served as a cap and protection for the first-stage engine bell, [...]
Assuming the cap is water-tight, does that imply the nozzle needs to be able to withstand outside pressure? The alternative would be to pressurize the bell.
How would such a cap be safely removed during startup? (without water sloshing in, pressure rising in the bell, bell-rim buckling, ...) It's basically an overexpanded nozzle at that depth.
Then again, is it maybe possible to just put a nozzle in water and start a pressure-fed engine? I've been pondering this and can't find a hard reason why it would be impossible. When turning on the engine, hot gases drive out the water and that's it.
