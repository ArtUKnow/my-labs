Due to scaling factors of cryogenic insulation, is there a size limit below which TNR rockets have no advantage over chemical rockets
The Isp of all rockets is limited by the rocket's exhaust gas temperature and the molecular weight of that exhaust gas. High temperature is good. Low molecular weight is even more good.
Thermal nuclear rockets’ (TNR) exhaust gas temperature is limited by the same metallurgy which limits chemical rockets’ temperature.
The Isp advantage of TNR is only because they can use hydrogen (H2) as exhaust gas rather than combustion products like H2O and CO2. H2 has lower molecular weight and resultant higher velocity for a given temperature than combustion products like H2O or CO2 .
The down side of using H2 as a propellant is that it must be stored cryogenically. The mass of cryogenic vessels adds significantly to H2 fuel mass. But that storage vessel mass does not scale linearly with fuel mass. The mass proportion of cryogenic tank verses mass of that tank's fuel is lower for large tanks than for small tanks.
This begs the question of what size spacecraft is the “breakeven” beyond which TNR have an advantage over chemical rockets for interplanetary voyages?
I assume chemical rockets, with their high thrust weight ratio (TWR) would be used for Hohmann style transfers while TNR with their low TWR ratio would be employed for continuous thrust (as in the Discovery in the movie 2001). Since H2 is consumed over the length of the voyage rather than mostly at the start, insulated storage is even more of an issue for TNR rockets than cryogenic chemical rockets.
Since radiation is a major limiting factor in space voyages Does human sensitivity to radiation impose a maximum distance on interstellar voyages regardless of radiation shielding or speed? and TNR promises to shorten the duration of voyages, the point at which they can deliver this advantage is of interest.
As TNR like DRACO are developed alongside chemical rockets like Starship, there will likely become a point where large TNR will be faster/better/cheaper than chemical rocket for interplanetary travel purely because of the tyranny of scale.
