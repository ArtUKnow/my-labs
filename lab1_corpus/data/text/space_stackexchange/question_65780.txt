Can infrastructure for generating a protective artificial magnetic field around a planet also be used to propel nearby spacecraft?
This is an attempt at "...maybe you scientists whizzes can visualize what I'm talking about and explain it better." from this closed question.
If a well-established colony on the Moon or Mars (for example) already has the technology and infrastructure needed to create a protective planet-sized artificial magnetic field, could such infrastructure potentially also be leveraged, on occasion, to create a dynamic and programmable magnetic field useful for accelerating (or decelerating) nearby spacecraft?
Let's assume that the spacecraft are purposefully designed to interact with the magnetic fields produced by the infrastructure on the planetary body.
If it is possible, how might the system work? What rates of acceleration or deceleration might be possible?
Update
I feel the need to constrain this question a little better. Let's assume that the colony has 100 million taxpayers and each contributes, on average, $10,000 2024 equivalent tax dollars to capitalize the infrastructure project. So a total budget of one trillion dollars. Let's also assume that today's high-temperature superconductors become as cheap, in the future, as today's copper wire is. Power costs the same as what it costs on Earth today.
