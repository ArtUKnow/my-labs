How much fuel does the SpaceX HLS need to do a round trip? Want to estimate the number of launches needed to refuel HLS
This image is from the all-hands meeting, right before IFT4 Musk announced Starship 2 and Starship 3. I know he likes to keep the imaginary pipeline going, all well and good, investors like to see a bright future!
In that meeting though, he dropped a bit of a bombshell for me (which I don't think anyone else has really focused on). Whilst discussing his outlines, he mentioned the ACTUAL performance of IFT3 (that is, its payload to orbit) in IFT3 configuration, was 45t. (I added that in red).
For IFT5 the plan (as I understand it) is to use tiles that are twice as strong and to add a silicon carbide blanket (that's another 5-8 tonnes in my opinion) to protect the ship in case some tiles come loose. I plan to assume for my calculations that these changes will double the mass of the thermal protection system and that this extra weight will further reduce the 45-ton payload capability.
Now I would like to estimate the number of refilling launches needed for Starship HLD as a function of the tanker-Starship's payload capacity. To do this, I need to estimate how much fuel and oxidizer must be delivered to refill Starship HLS while it's in Low Earth Orbit (LEO).
I assume that the first step would be to calculate a delta-v budget from LEO to the moon's surface and back for the Starship HLS mission architecture. Another aspect would be estimating the rate of propellant boil-off during various phases of the mission.
I realize that some of the input parameters (such as Starship HLS's dry mass, the boil-off rate, and the duration of the stay on the moon's surface) are unknown at this time so we'll need to state some (reasonable) assumptions for the time being until we know more. I'm more interested in learning how to do these calculations than I am in knowing the exact result.
