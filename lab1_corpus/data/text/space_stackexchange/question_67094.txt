Lunar power transmission using bare conductors laid on the regolith?
Electrical power transmission on the lunar surface will likely become necessary to join solar farms and lunar facilities, especially if water hydrolysis is used to make hydolox propellant.
Construction of conventional power lines with pylons and suspended cables would be a significant undertaking. Solar radiation is damaging to many common insulation materials. The large temperature fluctuations from lunar night to day (-175C to +127C) would change the tension in suspended wires.
Why not just lay the bare conductors on the ground? Regolith has about the same dielectric constant as Teflon or cross-linked polyethylene (2.35 vs 2.02 and 2.5) so regolith is an excellent insulator.
