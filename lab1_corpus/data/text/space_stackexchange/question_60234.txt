Could it be more cost-effective to refuel with on the lunar south pole produced methalox instead of refueling methalox in LEO, on the way to Mars?
In the article Prominent volcanic source of volatiles in the south polar region of the Moon it is calculated that 8 x 109 kg $CO_2$ has been vented billions of years ago by pyroclastic events in the Schrodinger basin to a 1400 km² area within 5⁰ of the lunar south pole, together with 1.4 times more water in a much wider area of 20,000 km².
So theoretically the Sabatier/Water Electrolysis Process (link from this question) could be applied in this region with the water and carbon dioxide mined to produce methane and oxygen.(methalox)
From the Marspedia article: "Elon Musk of Spacex recently tweeted that they would start producing methane on Earth using the Sabatier process for their Starship launches."
On the other hand, this article states that refueling a Starship in LEO "-should be surprisingly efficient, with perhaps 80% or more of the propellant launched remaining usable by the end of the process.", with Settled Cryogenic Propellant Transfer.
Question: If it appears that producing LOX/Liquid hydrogen on the lunar south pole is economic and there's sufficient $CO_2$ there, then can it be inferred what the most cost-effective way to travel to Mars is: refueling with methalox from Earth in LEO or any other alternative way of (also) refueling with on the lunar south pole produced methalox ?
