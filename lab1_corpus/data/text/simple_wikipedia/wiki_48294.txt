Leicestershire
Leicestershire (abbreviated Leics) is a county in central England. It takes its name from the heavily populated City of Leicester, its traditional administrative centre, although the City of Leicester is today administered separately from the rest of Leicestershire. The county borders onto Lincolnshire, Rutland, Northamptonshire, Warwickshire, Staffordshire, Derbyshire, and Nottinghamshire.
Leicestershire was recorded in the Domesday Book.
Geography
The River Soar and its tributaries form the main drainage basin in the county. The Soar is a tributary of the River Trent, which it joins at the point where Derbyshire, Leicestershire and Nottinghamshire meet. The River Avon and River Welland also flow along the southern edge of the county.
The geographical centre of England is in Leicestershire, near the village of Fenny Drayton.
The northwestern part of the county, around Coalville, is home to the National Forest, which also extends into Derbyshire and Staffordshire. The elevation of Leicestershire ranges from 20 to 278 metres. Its highest point is Bardon Hill.
