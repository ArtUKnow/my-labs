Medina
Medina, officially al-Madinah al-Munawwarah, is a city in the Hejaz region of Arabia. The city contains al-Masjid al-Nabawi (), which is the burial place of the Islamic prophet Muhammad, and is the second-holiest city in Islam after Mecca. The city is the capital of the Medina Province in Saudi Arabia.
The city existed for over 1,500 years before Muhammad's migration from Mecca under the name Yathrib. Under Muhammad's leadership, the city became the capital of a rapidly expanding Muslim state. It served as the center of Islam in its first century where the early Muslim community developed, becoming the capital of the Rashidun Caliphate under caliphs Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, until Ali, who changed it to Kufa. Medina is home to the three oldest mosques, namely the Quba Mosque, Prophet's Mosque, and Masjid al-Qiblatayn (). Muslims believe that the last-written surahs (chapters) of the Quran were revealed to Muhammad in Medina. These are called the Medinan surahs unlike the earlier Meccan surahs.
Only Muslims are permitted enter the sacred core of Medina or the center of the city. In addition to going for Ziyarah (visiting the prophet), tourists usually visit other mosques and landmarks in the city that have religious importance, such as Mount Uhud, Al-Baqi' cemetery and the Seven Mosques. The Saudi government has carried out the destruction of historical sites in both Medina and Mecca.
Names
Yathrib
Before the rise of Islam, the city was known as Yathrib (; ). The word Yathrib appears in an inscription found in Harran of the Babylonian king Nabonidus as well as later writings. The name has also been recorded in āyah (verse) 13 of surah (chapter) 33 of the Qur'an. and is known to have been the name of the city until the Battle of the Trench. According to Islamic tradition, Muhammad later forbade calling the city by this name.
Taybah and Tabah
A bit after the battle, Muhammad renamed the city Taybah (the Kind or the Good) () and Tabah (). Taybah is sometimes used as a nickname for the city today, and both names are put together to make another name for the city, Taybat at-Tabah ().
Madinah
The modern name for the city is Al-Madinah (), which is mentioned in some hadiths. The names ' () or ' ("City of the Prophet" or "The Prophet's City") and ' ("The Illuminated City") all come from this word. ' is the official and most commonly accepted modern name of the city, used in official documents and road signs in Saudi Arabia along with 'Madinah'.
Overview
Medina currently has a population of more than 1,300,000 people (2006). Medina is one of the three holiest cities of Islam, after Mecca and before Jerusalem.
Importance in Islam
Medina is the second holiest site in Islam after Mecca. That is why Medina is very important to Muslims. This is because Prophet Muhammad is buried in a mosque known as 'Masjid-e-Nabawi' or 'The Mosque of The Prophet' located in Medina. The mosque was built on a site next to Muhammad's home. Muslims believe that Prophets must be buried at the very same place that they die. So Muhammad was buried in his house. The tomb later became part of the mosque when it was expanded by the Umayyad Caliph Al-Walid I. The first mosque of Islam is also in Medina. It is known as Masjid Quba, (the Quba Mosque).
Like Mecca, the city of Medina only permits Muslims to enter. The haram (area closed to non-Muslims) of Medina is much smaller than that of Mecca, though. Many facilities on the outskirts of Medina are open to non-Muslims. In Mecca the area closed to non-Muslims extends well beyond the limits of the built-up area. Both cities' many mosques are the destination for large numbers of Muslims on their annual pilgrimage. Hundreds of thousands of Muslims come to Medina each year to visit the Tomb of Prophet and to worship at mosques in a unified celebration. Muslims believe that praying once in the Mosque of the Prophet is equal to praying at least 1000 times in any other mosque.
