Armenian genocide
The Armenian genocide was the mass killing and forced removal of Armenian people by the Ottoman Empire during World War I. In 1915, the Ottoman government started forcing Armenians to leave their homes. By the end of World War I, between 600.000 and 1.5 million Armenians had been killed or died on the journey.
By 2025, 34 countries have recognized the Armenian genocide as a genocide.
What happened before
Before World War I, the Ottoman Empire had many big problems. The Empire was losing its land in Europe (the Balkans). Many different groups of people lived in the empire, and some wanted to be free and start their own countries.
The Armenians were a large minority group. They had been asking for better treatment and more rights for a long time. But the leaders of the Ottoman Empire (know as the CUP) were very worried about the creation of an Armenian state. The Ottoman Empire had already lost a lot of land in the Balkans. They were afraid that Armenian nationalism would cause them to lose Anatolia, which was the heart of their Empire.
Returning to Istanbul, Enver Pasha largely blamed the Armenians living in the region for actively siding with the Russians. Despite the tensions, the Armenians had thrived under Ottoman rule. Most Armenians were better educated and wealthier than their Turkish counterparts, who were jealous of the Armenians' success.
The resentment was fueled by suspicions that the Christian Armenians would support fellow countries of fellow Christians, especially Russia, which shared an unstable border with the Ottoman Empire, as opposed to the Muslim Ottoman caliphate.
