Vice Admiral Sir Francis Drake (1540 – 27 January 1596) was an English sea captain, privateer, navigator, slaver, and politician of the Elizabethan era.
Drake became a powerful enemy to the Spanish Empire. He was the second sea captain ever to circumnavigate the world. Encyclopedia Britannica calls him "the most renowned [famous] seaman of the Elizabethan Age."
Life
Drake was born in Tavistock, England, and grew up in Kent. Drake and his second cousin, Richard Hawkins, led one of the first slavery trips to West Africa in 1567. It was illegal in English law to capture people and transport them, but in those days it was perceived as ok if they were already enslaved, non-Protestants or criminals!
[Drake and Hawkins] were attacked by Spanish vessels and only two of the six British ships survived (those led by Drake and Hawkins themselves). This, as well as other factors, contributed to fueling the [hostility] between Spain and England, leading up to the war in 1585 and subsequent [[Spanish Armada|[Spanish] Armada]]. By 1568, Drake commanded his own slave-trading ship, which he used to transport African slaves to the Spanish colonies in the Caribbean. after unsuccessfully attacking San Juan, Puerto Rico.
Drake's activities were legendary. He was a hero to the English but a pirate to the Spaniards.
