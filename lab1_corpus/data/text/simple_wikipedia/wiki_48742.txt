Julius Robert Oppenheimer (April 22, 1904 – February 18, 1967), commonly referred to as J. Robert Oppenheimer, was an American physicist. During World War II, Oppenheimer was the scientific director of the Manhattan Project (the project which built the first nuclear weapons). Oppenheimer is often referred to as "the father of the atomic bomb". So, Oppenheimer lost access to the government's atomic secrets and his career as a nuclear physicist ended.
In December 1963, he was given the Enrico Fermi Award by President Lyndon B. Johnson. In 2022, the federal government gave back Oppenheimer's security clearance.
Death
J. Robert Oppenheimer died of throat cancer in Princeton, New Jersey on February 18, 1967 at aged 62. His remains were cremated and scattered in the ocean near his beach house in Saint John, U.S. Virgin Islands.
Oppenheimer's wife, Kitty, died on October 27, 1972.
Their daughter, Toni, committed suicide in January 1977, at the age of 32, in St. John, U.S. Virgin Islands.
Pop culture
In 2023, Christopher Nolan directed a movie based on Oppenheimer's life with Irish actor Cillian Murphy playing Oppenheimer.

The meme appears to have sprung up between June-July of 2023, which corresponds to around the time that the Christopher Nolan film was released.
