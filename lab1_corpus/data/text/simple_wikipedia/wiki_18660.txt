Raleigh, North Carolina
Raleigh ( ) is the capital city of North Carolina as well as the county seat of Wake County.
Raleigh is the second most populated city in North Carolina, after Charlotte. Raleigh is known as the "City of Oaks" for its many oak trees.
The area is also nicknamed "The Triangle", as Raleigh is part of the Research Triangle area, which includes Durham and Chapel Hill. The "Triangle" nickname began after the 1959 development Research Triangle Park in Durham and Wake Counties. It is called the "triangle" because it is located in between Raleigh, Durham, and Chapel Hill (three cities) and their major universities.
The Research Triangle area makes up the U.S. Census Bureau's Raleigh-Durham-Chapel Hill Combined Statistical Area. As of 2025, Raleigh, North Carolina itself had a population of 63,575. As of the 2020 Census, the combined Raleigh-Durham-Chapel area had an estimated population of 2,221,205 people.
History
Raleigh was established in 1770. In 1788, it became North Carolina's state capital. The first state fair happened in 1853 and has continued to happen every year. Raleigh was not hurt by the Civil War, though did not grow much after the Civil War.
In the early 1900s, Raleigh had streetcars, but they got rid of them later. Raleigh got its first television station in 1956. In 1959, the Research Triangle Park was built. This caused people seeking work to move to the area. In the late 1900s, Interstate 40 and Interstate 440 were built, helping traffic.
Since the late 1900s, Raleigh has grown quickly.
November 28, 1988 tornado
On November 28, 1988, Raleigh was impacted by a F4 tornado.
The tornado formed after a very warm late November day. A line of thunderstorms that was spotted over the Charlotte area during the afternoon hours hit Raleigh just after 1:00 a.m. local time, however the National Weather Service (NWS) issued no tornado or severe thunderstorm watches for the area, as they thought the conditions for a tornado did not exist.
The tornado cost a conservative estimate of $77,000,000 in damages in 1988 U.S. dollars (roughly equivalent to $217.36 million dollars in 2026 USD).
The F4 tornado caused two fatalities in the city of Raleigh, and four in total.
2011 tornado
On April 16, 2011, an EF3 tornado struck Raleigh, killing 24 people in North Carolina and four in Raleigh.
Geography
Raleigh is in north-central North Carolina. It is in a hilly area. According to the United States Census Bureau, the city has a size of 149.60 square miles (387.5 km 2 ). 148.54 square miles (384.7 km 2 ) is land and 1.07 square miles (2.8 km 2 ) (0.72%) is water. The Neuse River goes through the northeastern part of the city. Most of the city is in Wake County, but a small part of Raleigh is in Durham County.
Cityscape
Raleigh is divided into many areas, and each of them use a Raleigh address and a ZIP code that begins with the numbers 276.
Climate
Raleigh has a mild climate.
The summers are hot, with an average high of about 90°F (32°C).
The winters are cool, with an average high of about 50°F (10°C). It snows about twice a year with a total of six inches (15 centimeters).
Every year, Raleigh also gets approximately 45 inches of rain.
Transportation
Raleigh has a well-developed road transportation infrastructure. Some freeways are Interstate 40, I-440, and I-540. Some highways are U.S. Route 1, U.S. Route 64, and U.S. Route 70, and N.C. Highway 50.
Raleigh also has a Amtrak station and a large airport.
