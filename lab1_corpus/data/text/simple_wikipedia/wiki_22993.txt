Orson Welles
George Orson Welles (May 6, 1915 – October 10, 1985) was an American director, actor, writer, and producer who is remembered for his innovative work in film, radio, and theatre. He is considered to be among the greatest and most influential filmmakers of all time. Welles was born in Kenosha, Wisconsin.
In 1938 Welles directed a weekly radio show, The Mercury Theatre of the Air. His October 30 broadcast of The War of the Worlds (radio drama) created much excitement.
Welles also made the movie Citizen Kane in 1941. Many movie critics think that this movie is the best movie ever made.
Welles died of a heart attack in Los Angeles, California, aged 70.
