Gaia
Gaia (), also spelled Gaea, is a very old Earth goddess in Greek stories. She is the Earth. She came out of Chaos, which was a big empty space. Gods and people saw her as the great mother. All gods and goddesses came from her. She had children with Uranus (the Sky) and Pontus (the Sea). In Roman stories, she is called Terra Mater or Tellus Mater.
Gaia was the first one in charge of the Oracle of Delphi. An oracle is a place where people learn the future. She sometimes told others about the future. She helped Zeus beat his father, Cronus, and win against the Titans. But later she became angry with Zeus and sent her scary son Typhon to fight him.
A writer named Hesiod, around 700 BCE, wrote that Gaia came from Chaos. She gave birth to Uranus (Sky), Ourea (Mountains), and Pontus (Sea). She married her son Uranus. They had 12 Titan children: Oceanus, Coeus, Crius, Hyperion, Iapetus, Theia, Rhea, Themis, Mnemosyne, Phoebe, Tethys, and Cronus.
Gaia also had three one-eyed sons called Cyclopes: Brontes, Arges, and Steropes. She also had three huge sons with 100 hands and 50 heads. They were called Kottos, Gyes, and Briareus.
With Pontus, Gaia had sea children named Thaumas, Phorcys, Ceto, and Eurybia.
All famous gods and goddesses came from Gaia. She is seen as the great mother goddess.
In the 6th century BCE, a religion called Orphism began. It was based on what the hero Orpheus learned in the underworld. In this belief, the world began with Chronos (Time). From Chronos came Aether (Light), Chaos, and Erebus (Darkness). Chronos made an egg in Aether. From the egg came Phanes, who made everything. Phanes had Nyx (Night), and then Gaia and Uranus were born.
Relations with Cronus and his children
According to the mythology, Uranus did not like his children. He hid them deep inside the earth. Gaia was very sad. She made a plan to stop him.
She made a strong sharp tool called a sickle. She told her sons that their father had done bad things and they should stop him.
Only her youngest son, Cronus, was brave. He hid and waited. When Uranus came to Gaia, Cronus used the sickle and cut him. He threw Uranus’ body parts into the sea. Blood fell on Gaia. From the blood came the Furies (Alecto, Tisiphone, and Megaera), the Giants, and the Meliae (tree spirits).
Cronus married his sister Rhea. Gaia and Uranus warned Cronus that one day his own son would beat him. Cronus was afraid. So every time Rhea had a baby, he swallowed it.
When Rhea was going to have Zeus, she asked Gaia for help. Gaia helped her hide in Crete. Zeus grew up and fought Cronus and the Titans in a big war called the Titanomachy.
In the tenth year of the war, Gaia told Zeus to free the Hecatoncheires and the Cyclopes from Tartarus, where they were locked away. Uranus had locked them up because he feared them. Zeus freed them, and with their help he won the war.
After the Titans were beaten and sent to Tartarus, Gaia became angry again. She sent her Giant children to fight Zeus and the Olympian gods. The Giants were huge and very strong. They had dragon scales on their feet and long hair.
An oracle said the gods could not win alone. They needed help from a mortal man who would become safe from harm by a magic plant. Gaia looked for this plant. But Zeus stopped the light from Eos (Dawn), Selene (Moon), and Helios (Sun) so he could find the plant first.
With help from Hercules, the gods beat the Giants.
Gaia then had one more child with Tartarus. His name was Typhon. He was the biggest monster ever. Typhon fought Zeus. Zeus beat him and buried him under Mount Etna.
Relations with the Oracle
The Oracle at Delphi first belonged to Gaia. Later she gave it to her daughters Themis and Phoebe. Later it went to Apollo, who built a temple there.
A writer named Pausanias said Gaia chose a mountain spirit named Daphnis to speak for her. Some stories say Gaia and Poseidon shared the oracle. Later Gaia gave her part to Themis. Apollo later received it and gave Poseidon an island in return.
After Gaia gave up Delphi, people still asked her about the future at Aegeira. There, her priestess drank bull’s blood to tell the truth. Bull’s blood was poison to other people.
Some stories say Apollo took the oracle from Gaia after killing Python, the snake who guarded it.
Future predictions
Gaia said Cronus would be beaten by his son. She also said Zeus and his wife Metis would have two children: a strong, wise daughter and a son who would become king instead of Zeus.
Zeus was afraid. He swallowed Metis. But she was already going to have a baby. The baby girl, Athena, later came out of Zeus’ head.
When Zeus and Hera married on the island of the Hesperides, Gaia gave them golden apples from a magic tree. The apples gave long life.
Stories with Apollo
Apollo killed the snake Python at Delphi. Gaia complained to Zeus. Zeus told Apollo to go away and clean himself. Apollo then led the Pythian Games to honor Python.
Some writers say Apollo chased a mountain spirit named Daphne. She asked Gaia for help. Gaia turned her into a laurel tree to save her. Apollo never forgave Gaia.
Motherhood of Erichthonius
Athena did not want to marry anyone. During the Trojan War, she asked Hephaestus to make armor for her. He tried to be close to her. She pushed him away. His seed fell to the ground.
The ground made Gaia pregnant. Gaia did not want the baby. Athena took the baby boy and named him Erichthonius.
Holy songs
In old songs, Gaia is called the “mother of men.” She feeds and gives life to all. Fruit, flowers, grass, rain, and stars come from her. She gives life and also takes it away.
One song says she feeds all who walk on land, swim in the sea, or fly in the air.
Holy places
In Athens, there was holy land for Gaia near a statue of Zeus and a temple for Cronus and Rhea.
In Phlya, people called her the “Great Goddess.”
In Patras, there was a statue of her near temples of Apollo, Aphrodite, and Demeter.
In Aigai (Vergina), there was a holy place for Earth with a very old wooden statue. The priestess had to stay pure and drink bull’s blood to test truth.
There were altars for Gaia in Tegea, Olympia, and Sparta. In Olympia, her altar was made of ash and was once an oracle. In Sparta, holy places for her were in Laconia, including one called Gasepton.
