Dalton (unit)
The dalton or unified atomic mass unit (symbol: Da, amu or u) is a unit of measurement for measuring the mass of atoms, equalling ¹⁄₁₂ of the mass of the carbon-12 isotope, the most abundant form of carbon atoms in nature.
History
The unit is named after English chemist and physicist John Dalton (1766 – 1844). 1 atomic mass unit is approximately 1.66 × 10 -27 kg, or 1.66 × 10 -24 g.
Atomic physics
In atomic physics, 1 amu can be expressed as roughly 931 MeV (M = 10 6 , 1 eV ≈ 1.60 × 10 -19 J) due to mass-energy equivalence, illustrated by ΔE = Δmc 2 .
