Vitamin E
Vitamin E (which is also called Tocopherol or Tocotrienol, E307, E308,E309) is a vitamin. They are fat-soluble and an antioxidant. A healthy immune system can fight off infection better. It can be found in the body and is stored in fat (lipid) cells. The synthetic form of vitamin E is less bioactive than the natural form, and the body uses it about half as much (efficiently).
Sources from foods
The body usually gets the vitamin E that it needs from diet, or by using up what it has stored. Because the body can store vitamin E, it is not needed every day in diet. Dietary sources of vitamin E include: 1mg of vitamin E (synthetic) is 1.1IU (international units). The amount of vitamin E that is needed daily is different in men and women and by age. While men need 4mg a day, women need 3mg. Vitamin E is supposed to help the healing of the skin after burns and injuries, and lower the risk of heart disease and cancer. There has not been enough evidence of adverse reactions caused by vitamin E overdose. appear to be safe. In addition to interacting with vitamin K in the body, vitamin E (when taken as a dietary supplement) may cause problems when it is taken with omega-3 supplements It can interact with medications which thin the blood (for example clopidogrel, warfarin, aspirin, and others), or anticoagulants. It interacts with the drug selumetinib. Vitamin E supplements should be avoided in people with bleeding disorders.
