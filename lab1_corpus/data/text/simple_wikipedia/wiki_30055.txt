Ṣalāt (, ') is the practice of prayer in Islam. It is one of the five most important duties for Sunni Muslims. To perform Salat, a person's mind must be ritually pure, which comes from ritual ablution, wuḍūʾ, or the washing and cleansing of his or her body. The salat is observed three or (most commonly) five times every day at prescribed times, depending on the sect.
Salawat is plural, and Salat is singular.
Name
Salat is an Arabic word that means to pray. Outside the Arab world, the most widespread and common term is the Persian word namāz.
Sunni
For Sunni Muslims, salat must be performed at five periods each day. These periods are measured according to the Sun’s movement. The call to prayer is essential before every obligatory prayers. Muhammad said: "The one who responds to Azan out of faith and seeking Allah's pleasure will be admitted to paradise."
In the ritual prayers each Muslim is in direct contact with Allah. There is no need of a priest as a middleman (While there is a prayer leader in the mosque - the imam - they are not a priest, but simply a person who knows a great deal about Islam.) Muslims can pray anywhere, but it is especially good to pray with others in a mosque. Praying together in a congregation helps Muslims to realise that all humanity is one, and all are equal in the sight of Allah. The prayer ritual, which is over 1400 years old, is repeated five times a day by hundreds of millions of people all round the world.
Shia
There are different subsets of Shia Islam. Some of them pray 3 times combined, others 5 times. Allah does not need human prayers because he has no needs at all. Muslims pray because God has told them that they are to do this, and because they believe that they obtain great benefit in doing so.
Hadith rejectors and Qur'anists
Most Hadith rejectors pray three times a day, deriving this structure from their interpretation of Qur'anic verses.
Qur'anists who follow the Ahl al-Qur'an movement founded by Ahmed Subhy Mansour and members of the Submitters movement founded by Rashad Khalifa perform five daily prayers. They claim that acts of worship—including the five daily Salat and the Hajj—were preserved through consecutive generational practice rather than through reliance on written hadith collections.
The Bida'iyya, a branch of the Kharijite sect known as the Azariqa—emerging specifically through the sub-lineage of the Khazimiyya—rejected the five prayers and prayed only two or three times a day. Ibn Hazm also credits this practise to another Azariqa offshoot known as the Bittikhiyya (or Batihiyya). The Bida'iyya performed two rakats (units) in each prayer. They further rejected hadith altogether.
Several people who reject hadith have opposed silent recitation in the Salat. Rashad Khalifa taught that the tone of voice during the prayer must follow the instruction of Qur'an 17:110: a person must not be too loud, and must not perform the prayer silently, but must use a middle tone. He said that during group prayer only the imam should be heard, while everyone else listens. This same view is held by Edip Yuksel and Ahmed Subhy Mansour. They believe the daily prayers must be spoken in medium, moderate voices. This differs from Sunni and Shia practice, which divide prayers into loud and silent types.
Ritual washing
Muslims must be clean before they pray. They make sure of this by performing ritual washing, called wudhu. Mosques have washing facilities for Muslims to clean their body before they begin prayer.
