Chopsticks landing
A chopsticks landing is a way to catch and land a very large rocket. It was first performed by SpaceX with their SpaceX Starship rocket in 2024. The name "chopsticks" comes from the two arms that catch the rocket, which bear a resemblance to chopsticks used for eating.
How it works
In a chopsticks landing, two large arms mounted to the launch tower catch the rocket as it returns back to Earth. They move to grab the rocket and slow it down. This is different from how rockets used to land, which was often on their own landing legs or in the sea.
The chopsticks can also lift the rocket and put it on the launch pad. This makes it easier to use the rocket again.
Alternate names
This method is also called the Mechazilla catch or the booster catching system. Some people call it the catching arms landing or mechanical arm recovery because the arms do the job of catching. Others may call it the tower catch landing or the Super Heavy catch system since it involves the tower catching the Super Heavy Booster. All these names describe the same idea of using arms to safely land the rocket without legs.
First chopsticks landing
SpaceX, a company that makes rockets, did the first chopsticks landing with their Starship rocket. This happened on October 13, 2024. It was a big step for space travel because it showed a new way to land very big rockets.
Why it matters
Chopsticks landings are important for a few reasons:
They can catch bigger rockets than before.
They make it easier to use rockets again.
They might make space travel cheaper.
