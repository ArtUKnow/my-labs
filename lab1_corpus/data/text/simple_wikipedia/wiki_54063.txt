Banská Bystrica
Banská Bystrica (German: Neusohl, Hungarian: Besztercebánya, Latin: Villa Nova), is a large city in central Slovakia, on the Hron river surrounded by the mountains Low Tatras, Veľká Fatra, and the Kremnica Mountains.
Banská Bystrica has about 76,000 residents and is the sixth largest city in Slovakia. It was founded by Germans invited by Hungarian kings during the Middle Ages. Before that, it was a settlement of Slavs, Slovaks, and Avars. After the Hungarians took control, it became part of Zólyom County. In 1255, it was given town rights by King Béla IV. Germans from Thüringen moved there, and the town grew wealthy from copper mining. In the late Middle Ages, the town built churches, houses, and fortifications. Today, Banská Bystrica is the capital of the Banská Bystrica Region and District. It is also home to Matej Bel University. Because of its history and nearby mountains, it is a popular destination for tourists in both winter and summer.
History
It was first mentioned in 1255 as a royal free mining town. The town had rich ore deposits, mainly copper, and to a lesser extent silver, gold and iron. As the deposits were depleted, the town has reoriented to the industry. During the World War II, the Slovak National Uprising broke out in the town.
