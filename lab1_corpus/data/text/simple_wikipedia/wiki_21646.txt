The West Bank is a territory in the Middle East. The State of Palestine claims the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, as part of its land. However, the territory has been under Israeli military occupation since the 1967 Six-Day War. The international community views it as an illegally occupied territory, while Israel considers it a "disputed territory".
The West Bank is referred to as Judea and Samaria by most Israelis. Since the 1993 Oslo Accords, the Israeli government and Palestinian leaders have had many talks to decide the future of the territory and the Gaza Strip.
Most people in the West Bank are Palestinians, but there are also many Jewish settlers living in Israeli settlements. The international community considers these settlements to be illegal under international law. Because West Bank Palestinians are not Israeli citizens, they are not allowed to vote in Israeli elections.
Geography
The West Bank borders Israel to the west and Jordan to the east. It touches the Dead Sea and the River Jordan.
Hills or desert make up most of its landscape. Summers are hot and dry, while winters are cooler and rainy.
Regions
The West Bank is divided into 165 Palestinian enclaves that are under partial Palestinian rule. The rest of the West Bank, including 200 Israeli settlements, is under full Israeli control.
Laws
Israeli settlers and civilians living in the West Bank (or traveling through it) are subject to Israeli law, and are represented in the Knesset. On the other hand, Palestinian civilians (who are mostly stuck in or confined to scattered enclaves), are subject to martial law and are not permitted to vote in Israel's national elections.
Names
The occupied West Bank is called "Judea and Samaria" by some right-wing Israeli settlers.
