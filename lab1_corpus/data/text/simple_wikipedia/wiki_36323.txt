Common Era
Common Era (CE; ) is a method used to identify a year. It means "a year in our time" (instead of a year a very long time ago). It is the system for recording dates used almost everywhere around the world today.
CE is an alternative to the AD system used by Christians, but the numbering is the same: the year CE is equal to AD (but usually we just say "the year is "). AD is an abbreviation of the . The CE method is used as an alternative to AD because not everyone is a Christian, but most people use the year numbering that was originally Christian. Some people use the name Christian Era () because it has the same acronym as the name Common Era.
When writing about calendars, an era is span of dates, starting from a particular moment in time. In the case of both CE and AD, that start date is after the date people think Jesus of Nazareth was born. Both systems start with year one.
Before Common Era
Before Common Era (BCE) is the system for the years "Before the Common Era". BCE uses the same numbering as BC (Before Christ). "CE" and "BCE" are placed after the year number. Thus we write "Our year is CE" or "Artaxerxes III of Persia was born in 425 BCE."
Usage of Christian Era notation began about 1615 among Christians in Europe, and has been growing among non-Christians who desire to be sensitive to Christians. “Common” Era is sometimes used instead, which is short for Common Christian Era.
No Year Zero
Neither system uses a year zero (0). Because of this, the year before 1 CE was 1 BCE, and the day before 1 January, 1 CE was 31 December, 1 BCE. This also means for example that Wednesday, 1 July, 5 CE was exactly eleven (11) years after Wednesday, 1 July, 7 BCE. The fact that there is no year zero is sometimes confusing for people trying to do math on dates between the two eras. One way to do the math correctly is by using this equation:
\{ \text{year CE} \} + \{\text{year BCE}\} - 1 = \{\text{number of years apart}\}
This is the same equation as finding the difference between a positive and negative number, minus one. The reason the final answer is one less is because the year numbering does not have a year zero. To use this equation on the example from before:
5 \ \text{(CE)} + 7 \ \text{(BCE)} - 1 = 12 - 1 = 11 \ \text{(years apart)}
