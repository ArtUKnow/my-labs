Iron Man
Iron Man is a fictional superhero who wears a suit of armor. His alter ego is Anthony Edward Tony Stark. He was created by Stan Lee, Jack Kirby and Larry Lieber for Marvel Comics in Tales of Suspense #39 in the year 1963 and appears in their comic books.
Origin story
Tony Stark is a rich businessman who uses his company, Stark Industries, to sell weapons to the American government. He is captured by a group of terrorists known as the Ten Rings, and is badly injured by a bomb used in the attack.
Imprisoned in a deep dark cave, Tony is ordered by his captors to create huge weapons of destruction for the Ten Rings to use against the world. However, Tony and his fellow prisoner, a man named Yinsen, plot to escape. Tony discovers that the bomb shrapnel in his chest could kill him, so he and Yinsen create a chest device that can keep him alive. Building off of this chest device, the two men design a suit of armor using the scrap metal and electronics given to them by the Ten Rings.
With Tony in the armor and not powered up, Yinsen begins the power-up process. However, the Ten Rings have discovered that their prisoners have betrayed them, and make their way towards the cave in order to kill them. In order to buy time, Yinsen takes a machine gun and fires wildly at the oncoming terrorists, but is shot dead by them.
Furious, and now at full-power, Tony rises from the table as Iron Man, and vows to avenge Yinsen's death. He makes his way out of the cave with ease, taking down dozens of terrorists on his way. Upon exiting the cave, Iron Man discovers a Ten Rings camp, and using a loudspeaker, tricks the dozens terrorists into escaping. Tony is then struck by the realisation that if he is to stay alive, he must remain in the Iron Man armor forever.
When he returns to America, Tony decides to become a superhero, as Iron Man. He discovers that his grey suit of armor scares the people he hopes to protect. He gives it a coat of golden paint, making him look more like a heroic knight.
Tony also discovers a way to stay alive without remaining in his suit forever. He makes the chest device smaller, allowing him to wear it under his clothing without the rest of the armor being needed.
Over time, Tony improves his Iron Man armor, eventually creating the iconic red and yellow one is he most known for. Dozens of Iron Man suits are created in the following years, all for various purposes, such as deep-sea expeditions, space exploration, and more.
Tony's adventures as Iron Man lead him to many places, including ancient Egypt, and a city at the core of the Earth. He later becomes a founding member of the Avengers when the Asgardian villain Loki threatens to take over the Earth.
In the Marvel Cinematic Universe, Iron Man's origin is very similar. The biggest difference is that instead of wearing a chestplate to protect his heart, he wears a small, powerful magnet called an Arc Reactor, which slowly poisons him across the movies. He eventually has the shrapnel in his heart surgically removed and no longer needs the Arc Reactor to keep him alive.
Personality
Tony Stark is a charismatic and eccentric businessman often seen by others as nothing more than an arrogant billionaire who loves flashy cars and expensive things. However, Tony is also very altruist and heroic.
Tony is very smart, and a talented inventor. Even before he became Iron Man, he was inventing things in his youth. In both the modern comics and the movies, he uses the arc reactor as a form of sustainable energy to help power the world.
Stark has very few close friends. Two of them are Pepper Potts, his personal assistant, who later becomes his love interest, and James Rhodes, an American soldier who operates the War Machine armor. Happy Hogan is the head of security for Tony's company, and in the early comics, is jealous of Tony for the love Pepper Potts has for him.
Powers
Tony Stark is a human, and was not born with nor given superpowers like many others were. His power comes from his suit of armor, which he uses to become the superhero Iron Man.
Iron Man is the most advanced suit of armor in the world. When wearing it, Tony can fly, shoot beams of energy from his hands, hack advanced computer systems, and cut through almost any material with special laser beams. The suit is also completely bulletproof, and gives Tony a great deal of strength, allowing him to fight very powerful beings.
The Iron Man armor is also able to absorb huge amounts of energy, allowing Tony to be hit by strong forces and not be hurt inside the armor. This is also what allows him to fly at high speeds, counteracting the G-Force.
Tony has hundreds of different Iron Man armors, which he uses for different purposes. For example, the Mark 5 armor from the movies can be carried in a suitcase and put on in seconds.
In the comics, Tony created an experimental virus called "Extremis", and after injecting it into his body, it allowed him to control his suit with his mind and summon it from wherever he was. Tony would eventually develop an armor that he could store in his body, and it would come to be known as the Bleeding Edge armor, or simple just the Model 37.
In the comics, Iron Man armors are called "Models", while in the movies, they are called "Marks". The armors in both formats are not the same, with the comic book armors being far more powerful than the movie ones.
Marvel Cinematic Universe
In the MCU, Iron Man is an important character across the events of the movie franchise. In the first movie, he created the Mark 1 armor with the help of Ho Yinsen to escape the Ten Rings, and was destroyed after Tony crash-landed following a launch into the air. It was later salvaged by the remaining terrorists and given to Obadiah Stane, Tony's business partner who hated Tony and wanted to kill him for control of his company. Obadiah was later defeated by Tony and Pepper Potts, who overloaded an experimental arc reactor to fry his suit.
Tony continued to make many suits over the next few movies. In The Avengers, Tony saved New York from a nuclear missile by sending it through a portal, but is left with PTSD after realising that they were not alone in the universe, and not as powerful as their alien invaders.
Obsessed with "putting a suit of armor around the world", Tony, with the help of Bruce Banner, creates Ultron, a powerful AI system which they plan to use to protect the world. However, Ultron turns evil, and destroys Tony's AI assistant, J.A.R.V.I.S. The Avengers later defeat Ultron, but the city of Sokovia is left destroyed.
In Captain America: Civil War, the governments of the world pressure the Avengers to be controlled by a government in order to prevent destruction. When the Sokovia Accords are presented to the group; Tony signs almost immediately, feeling great shame and responsibility for Ultron. Captain America refuses, and later becomes a fugitive. Tony recruits the help of Spider-Man and other allies to capture Captain America and his allies, but they fail, and Captain America goes on the run.
Meanwhile, Tony mentors a young Peter Parker and encourages him to be a better hero as Spider-Man than Tony could ever be. In Avengers: Infinity War, Tony, Doctor Strange, and Spider-Man go to space to fight Thanos, a powerful alien who is on a quest to collect the six Infinity Stones, in order to kill half of all life in the universe. Despite being helped by the Guardians of the Galaxy, the heroes fail, and Thanos succeeds in his mission.
Five years later, in Avengers: Endgame, Tony is retired and lives with his wife Pepper and their daughter Morgan in a cabin. Tony is asked by the Avengers to help them master time travel, so they can go back in time and collect Infinity Stones to bring everyone back with, but he refuses for the safety of his family. However, after remembering Peter Parker, he returns to the Avengers with a device that can allow them to time travel safely, with the help of a time machine created by Hank Pym.
Later in the movie, after the team uses the stones to bring everyone in the universe back, an alternate version of Thanos invaded their headquarters and threatens to destroy their universe with the stones. With their allies from across the universe, the Avengers lead a huge battle against Thanos and his alien army.
Tony fights Thanos, and successfully gets the Infinity Stones, using them to kill Thanos and his entire army. However, the power of the stones was too much for his body, and Tony dies in the arms of his friends and family, having sacrificed his life to save the universe.
A funeral is later held and is attended by the Avengers and their allies. Upon Tony's casket, Pepper places his original arc reactor, encased in a box reading "Proof that Tony Stark has a heart".
In other media
Iron Man has appeared in many different forms of media, including toys, movies, television series, and video games. He is also one of the main protagonists in the Marvel Cinematic Universe. Iron Man is played by Robert Downey, Jr. in the movies Iron Man, Iron Man 2, The Avengers, Iron Man 3, Avengers: Age of Ultron, Captain America: Civil War, Spider-Man: Homecoming, Avengers: Infinity War, and Avengers: Endgame.
Robert Downey Jr. is set to return to the MCU as Doctor Doom, with rumors speculating that his version of character was inspired by Iron Man.
