Public concern for Iwata's health began on June 5, 2014, when Nintendo announced that he would not be able to attend E3 2014 due to an unspecified health issue. On June 24, Iwata told shareholders that he had undergone surgery the previous week. Doctors had discovered a tumor in his bile duct during a routine check-up. The surgery was successful, and he returned to work in October after four months of recovery. Although he had lost a lot of weight, he stated that he was feeling healthier. Iwata made his first public appearance in a Nintendo Direct broadcast on November 5, but he looked very thin and pale. He seemed to accept these changes calmly. On June 12, 2015, Iwata even updated his Mii (a digital avatar used on Nintendo systems) to show his new, slimmer look.
Iwata was unable to recover from his bile duct cancer in the long term. He died from the disease on July 11, 2015 in Kyoto University Hospital, at the age of 55.
Buddhist funeral services for Iwata were held in Kyoto on July 16 and 17. Even though there was a storm caused by Typhoon Nangka, about 4,100 people came to pay their respects. Some of the people who attended were Reggie Fils-Aimé, Shigeru Miyamoto, Genyo Takeda, and Iwata's close friend Masahiro Sakurai. After the wake, Iwata's body was cremated. His ashes were buried at an unknown place within Kyoto.
For about two months after Iwata's death, Shigeru Miyamoto and Genyo Takeda jointly ran Nintendo. On September 14, 2015, Tatsumi Kimishima was named the new president and Iwata's official successor.
Legacy
Throughout his life, Iwata was known for his unconventional ideas and his impact on the gaming industry. Many people saw him as the face of Nintendo: playful, quirky, humorous, and fun. His skill in programming led many people to call him a "genius," and some compared him to a Japanese Bill Gates. His hands-on approach to running the company earned him respect from both developers and fans. However, some people criticized him for being too slow to move Nintendo into the mobile gaming market. After Iwata's death, Reggie Fils-Aimé said, "... it will be years before his impact on both Nintendo and the full video game industry will be fully appreciated."
In October 2015, a fan made an amiibo using Iwata's Mii avatar and sold it on eBay for US$1,900. All the money was donated to the Child's Play charity in his memory. Iwata was also given the Lifetime Achievement Award at the 2015 Golden Joystick Awards after his death, voted unanimously, for his influence on the gaming industry. He also received the Lifetime Achievement Award at the 2016 DICE Awards. At the 2016 Game Developers Choice Awards, David Hellman, the artist behind Braid, made a short animated film to honor Iwata. In July 2019, Hobonichi published a book in Japan called , written by Yasuda Nagata. The book includes parts of many "Iwata Asks" interviews and conversations with people close to Iwata, including Shigeru Miyamoto and Shigesato Itoi. Due to public interest, the Tuttle-Mori Agency planned to release the book in other languages. Viz Media later published the book in North America as Ask Iwata on April 13, 2021.
Video game appearances
During his life, Satoru Iwata appeared in a few WarioWare games. He first made a brief cameo in WarioWare, Inc.: Mega Microgames! during Wario's stage, where his likeness is on Wario's television screen thanking the player for playing the game. He had a larger role in WarioWare: Smooth Moves as "Shop Manager Iwata"; in the 9-Volt and 18-Volt stage, Iwata sells 18-Volt a Game & Watch, which he bought for 9-Volt to replace the one he broke by accident. Following Iwata's death, a few video games in the mid-2010s had tributes to him.
A tribute to Iwata, reading "This game is dedicated to our wingman who fell in battle," was placed in the credits of Star Fox Zero. Another possible tribute appears in The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild, a Nintendo Switch launch game that was still being made at the time of his death. In the game, a character who looks very much like Iwata asks the player to climb Satori Mountain to find a spirit called the Lord of the Mountain, who is also known as "Satori." Because "Satori" is very close to the name "Satoru," and because the Lord of the Mountain is seen as a guiding spirit, many people think this is a tribute to Iwata. Both this character and the Lord of the Mountain also appear in The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom.
In September 2017, modders found that a hidden version of the NES game Golf was built into the Nintendo Switch system. It could be accessed by moving the Joy-Con controllers in the same way Iwata moved his hands during Nintendo Direct presentations, but only when the console's date was set to July 11, the day he died. This feature was later removed by Nintendo in a system update later that year. In Pokémon Ultra Sun and Ultra Moon, players who visit the Game Freak building with a Pokémon from Pokémon Gold and Silver will find a character who talks about how someone helped them fit all the game data onto the Game Boy Color cartridge. This is seen as a nod to Iwata's work on Gold and Silver, where he helped include the entire world from Pokémon Red and Blue without making the new game smaller.
