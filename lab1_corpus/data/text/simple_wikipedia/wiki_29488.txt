South Ossetia
South Ossetia (, less commonly ), officially the Republic of South Ossetia – the State of Alania, or the Tskhinvali Region, is a de facto, disputed territory recognized as part of Georgia in the Caucasus region.
South Ossetia was a Soviet oblast (region) with some self-rule. When it tried to separate from Georgia in 1990 to become a separate Soviet-republic, Georgia abolished the self-rule. This led to the 1991-1992 South Ossetia War, after which Georgia only controlled parts of the region. Georgia tried to retake South Ossetia again in 2004 and in 2008. In 2008, Ossetia's fighters were backed by Russian troops and they gained full control of the region, but its separation from Georgia has only been recognized by a few other countries (Russia, Nicaragua, Syria, Venezuela) and Abkhazia (a similar place). It is de jure (officially) a part of the Georgian region (mkhare) of Shida Kartli.
Internationally regarded as a Russian occupied territory of Georgia, Georgia itself refuses to recognize rebel South Ossetia as an independent state. The government calls it by the medieval name of Samachablo or, more recently, Tskhinvali region (after the republic's capital).
South Ossetia is a geographical state that borders North Ossetia-Alania to the north which is not a separatist entity due to it being part of Russia.
South Ossetia relies heavily on Russian aid.
Sometimes, South Ossetia, Transnistria, Nagorno-Karabakh, and Abkhazia are named collectively as post-Soviet "frozen conflict" zones.
Geography
South Ossetia is situated at central Caucasus, a border between Asia and Europe. It occupies a part of Greater Caucasus range and the foothills of Kartalin Valley. South Ossetia is a very mountainous region.
