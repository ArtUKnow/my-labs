Chaos (mythology)
Chaos was the nothingness at the beginning of the world, described in the creation myths of Classical mythology. It was the state the universe (or cosmos) was in before it was created. Early Christians used the word "chaos" in a different way, with the word meaning disorder or randomness or confusion. Modern non-mythological use of the word has these meanings.
Chaos is often identified as a god, who filled the gap between Heaven and Earth. Born out of chaos were Nyx (Night) and Erebos (Darkness of the Underworld).
While not prevalent in any myths, Chaos was referenced during the Gigantomachy, in which it was described as being burned by Zeus's thunderbolt.
