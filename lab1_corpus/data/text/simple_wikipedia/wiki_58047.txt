The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker
The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker, known in Japan as , is the tenth game in The Legend of Zelda group of games. It was made by Nintendo EAD for the Nintendo GameCube. It went on sale in Japan on December 13, 2002, in North America on March 24, 2003, and in Europe on May 2, 2003.
Most of the game takes place on a huge ocean called the Great Sea, with small islands dotted across it. This was a big change for the series. Instead of walking across a countryside, the player sails from island to island in a talking boat named the King of Red Lions. The game is named after a magic baton, the Wind Waker, which the player uses to control the wind.
The Wind Waker was the first Zelda game to use cel shading, a way of drawing 3D graphics so that they look like a hand-drawn cartoon. This looked very different from Ocarina of Time (1998) and Majora's Mask (2000), which tried to look realistic. Many fans disliked the new look before the game came out, and some made fun of it by calling the game "Celda".
The story happens hundreds of years after Ocarina of Time, in what Zelda fans call the "adult timeline". Twilight Princess happens in a different timeline instead. Two later games follow on from The Wind Waker directly: Phantom Hourglass (2007) and Spirit Tracks (2009), both for the Nintendo DS.
Story
Background
The game opens with a legend told through pictures. Long ago, a young man in green clothes, known as the Hero of Time, saved the kingdom of Hyrule from an evil wizard named Ganondorf and shut him away in another world. But Ganondorf broke free and made a second attempt to seize the Triforce. The people prayed for the hero to come back, and he never did. In the end the goddesses answered by telling the people to flee to the mountaintops, then flooding the kingdom and sealing it at the bottom of the new sea. Reviewers also liked being able to pick up and fight with weapons that enemies drop.
The world is a grid of 49 squares of the Great Sea, and each square holds an island or a group of islands. Some must be visited to finish the story, and the rest are optional. Link sails between them with a sail on the King of Red Lions. The wind blows in one of eight directions, and a wind at the boat's back gives it full speed, while sailing into the wind is hard work.
Away from the main story there is a lot to do. Link can take photographs with the Picto Box, an in-game camera, and use them to finish quests. Items can also work differently at sea. The grappling hook, for example, becomes a crane for pulling treasure up off the sea bed, and once Link has bombs the boat can fire them from a cannon at enemy ships and sea monsters. Eiji Aonuma directed it, and Shigeru Miyamoto and Takashi Tezuka, who created the series, were the producers. The team, though, had run out of ideas for that style, and Aonuma disliked the video because it felt like more of the same.
Once the style was settled, the team decided to set the game on islands in an ocean, because that would look good in cel shading. That choice led to the sailing. That demo appeared at E3 in 2002 and was received much better. The game won Best Console Game at that year's Game Critics Awards.
Near the end of development, two dungeons were running late and were cut. The quest to collect the Triforce pieces around the Great Sea was put in to replace them. Kondo, who normally leads the music on Zelda games, helped with the score but was not the sound director this time. Much of the music is based on traditional Irish music and is lighter and happier than earlier Zelda soundtracks.
Release
People who pre-ordered the game were given a bonus disc holding a GameCube version of Ocarina of Time together with Ocarina of Time Master Quest, a harder version that had been made for the Nintendo 64's 64DD add-on but never released. The pre-order campaign was the most successful Nintendo had run up to that point.
Later in 2003, Nintendo gave away a disc called The Legend of Zelda: Collector's Edition. It held the first two Zelda games, Ocarina of Time, Majora's Mask and a 20-minute demo of The Wind Waker. It was never sold in shops, and could only be had through a console bundle, by registering a GameCube, or by subscribing to Nintendo Power.
On June 5, 2025, the original GameCube version was added to the Nintendo GameCube – Nintendo Classics app on the Nintendo Switch 2, alongside F-Zero GX and Soulcalibur II. It needs a Nintendo Switch Online + Expansion Pack membership.
Reception
The Wind Waker was praised by almost every reviewer. Metacritic worked out a score of 96 out of 100 from 80 reviews. It was only the fourth game ever to get a perfect score from the Japanese magazine Famitsu.
Critics liked the graphics that fans had complained about, often comparing the game to a cartoon you could play. Jeff Gerstmann of GameSpot wrote that the worries about the visuals had turned out to be unfounded, and that the character designs made the story hit harder. Reviewers also praised the controls, the fighting, the puzzles and the dungeons. It won the Excellence in Visual Arts prize at the 2004 Game Developers Choice Awards.
Ordinary players stayed split over the art style, more so in North America than in Japan. The game sold 4.6 million copies, well below the 7.6 million that Ocarina of Time had managed. Nintendo of America blamed the cartoon graphics for putting off older teenagers, who had been the series' main audience. Because of this, a planned direct sequel was dropped, and Aonuma persuaded Miyamoto to let him make a realistic-looking game instead, which became Twilight Princess. It came out in North America on September 20, 2013, in Japan on September 26, 2013, in Europe on October 4, 2013, and in Australia on October 5, 2013.
As well as better graphics and lighting, the remake fixed several things reviewers had complained about. A new item called the Swift Sail makes the boat faster and keeps the wind behind it, so the player no longer has to keep conducting. The Triforce hunt was made shorter and the Picto Box was improved. The Wii U GamePad screen holds the map and the items, so the player does not have to keep opening menus, and the game can be played on the GamePad alone. The Tingle Tuner was replaced by a Tingle Bottle, which let players leave messages for one another through Nintendo's Miiverse service. Miiverse was shut down on November 8, 2017, so that feature no longer works. By September 2023 it had sold 2.37 million copies.
