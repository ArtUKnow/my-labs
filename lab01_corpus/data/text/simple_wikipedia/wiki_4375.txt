Angle
When two straight lines come together, they make an angle. The two lines are called the sides of the angle, and they meet at a point. A flat surface (called a plane) also forms an angle when it meets another.
To represent an angle, Greek letters such as \alpha (alpha), \beta (beta), \gamma (gamma) and \theta (theta) are sometimes used. An angle indicates the space between its sides, or the amount of rotation needed to make one side coincide the other.
To measure the size of an angle, we use units called degrees. A degree is a standard unit and we use the symbol ° after a number to show that it is a number of degrees. We can use a decimal number or a fraction for part of a degree, but a degree can also be divided into 60 minutes (1° = 60'), and a minute can be divided into 60 seconds (1' = 60"). So 22.5°, 22° and 22° 30' are all the same angle.

Special angle pairs
In geometry, there are pairs angles having a special relationship with each other, making them interesting and convenient. There is a pair of angles called complementary angles to which the sum of their measure (wideness) is equal to one right angle (which is equal to \tfrac{1}{4} turn, 90°, or \tfrac{\pi}{2} radians). Supplementary angles are also two angles, this time their combined measure is a straight angle ( \tfrac{1}{2} turn, 180°, or \pi radians). Two angles that total to a full angle ( 1 turn, 360°, or 2\pi radians) are referred to as explementary or conjugate angles.
