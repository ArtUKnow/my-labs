Ariel Sharon
Ariel Sharon (February 27, 1928 – January 11, 2014) was an Israeli general and politician. He was Prime Minister of Israel from 2001 to 2006. He fought in many battles for Israel. He was member of the Likud political party for a long time. From 1981 to 1983, he was the Israeli Minister of Defence. As Minister of Defence, he did not prevent the Sabra and Shatilla massacre in Lebanon. In September 2000, he visited the Temple Mount in Jerusalem. Many people say this was the trigger for the Second Intifada. The Second Intifada was a big and long fight between Israel and Palestinians. Sharon fell into a coma in 2006. He never awoke from the coma, but died in 2014.
Early life
Sharon was born on February 27, 1928 in Kfar Malal, British-Palestine. As a teenager, he was in the Haganah group. Sharon fought in the 1948 Palestine War. Sharon then joined the Israeli Army in 1948. He fought in numerous battles.
Sharon started the group "Unit 101" in 1952. This group was part of the Israeli Army. The group is known for the Qibya massacre in 1953.
In 1956, the Suez War broke out. The Israeli Army lost 38 soldiers because Sharon ignored an order from his chief. Sharon also fought in the 1967 war. This 1967 war is also called the 6-Day War because it lasted for six days. Sharon was a major general in this war.
Sharon left the army in 1972 because he was not appointed IDF chief of staff. The IDF chief of staff is the highest officer in the Israeli Army. Sharon was not appointed because he often acted without approval from the Israeli Government. But he came back to the Army in the 1973 Yom Kippur War. After the war, he decided to go to politics.
Politics
Early steps
The Likud Party is an Israeli political party founded in 1973. Sharon was one of the founding members of Likud. A year later, in 1974, he was elected to the Knesset. The Knesset is the Israeli Parliament. In 1977, he became Minister of Agriculture. He was criticized for planning Israeli settlements .
Minister of Defence (1981–1983)
In 1981 he became the Minister of Defence. In 1983, he was forced to quit the government as Minister of Defence after he was found guilty for failing to prevent the Sabra and Shatilla massacre in Lebanon. In this massacre, thousands of Palestinian civilians were killed by the right-wing Lebanese Phalange political party. .
Later Ministerial Offices
Sharon was "Minister without portfolio" in 1983 and 1984. A Minister without portfolio is a member of the Government, but has no specific task in this Gevernment. Sharon was Minister of Industry and Trade from 1984 until 1990. From 1990 until 1992, Sharon was Minister of Construction and Housing. In 1996, Sharon became Minister of National Infrastructure. In 1998, he left this office to become the Israeli Foreign Minister. He left this office in 1999.
Second Intifada
The Israeli-Palestinian Conflict is an ongoing conflict between Israel and the Palestinian People over the geographic area of Palestine. The conflict started already before the 1948 Israeli Declaration of Indepence. Both sides claim overlapping territory in the area of Palestine. For example, both sides want to control the city of Jerusalem. Over the years, many people tried to end the conflict. In July 2000, three people made another attempt for peace. These people were the Israeli Prime Minister Ehud Barak, the Palestinian President Yasser Arafat, and the President of the United States Bill Clinton. The three presidents meet in Camp David. Camp David is a place in the United States, so this meeting was called the 2000 Camp David Summit. But the meeting ended without a result.
A few weeks after the Camp David Summit ended, Sharon went onto the Temple Mount in Jerusalem. This happened on September 28, 2000. The Temple Mount is also known as al-Haram as-Sharif. Sharon was on the Temple Mount for about 45 minutes. Many police officers were together with Sharon on the Temple Mount. Most Palestinians are muslims. So, they think that the Temple Mount/al-Haram as-Sharif belongs to the Palestinians. At that time, Ariel Sharon was not in the Israeli Government. But he wanted to get elected to the government. The Palestinian protesters feared that Ariel Sharon would take the Temple Mount/al-Haram as-Sharif from them after he would be in the Israeli government.
Ariel Sharon said this after he was on the Temple Mount: "The Temple Mount is in our hands and will remain in our hands. It is the holiest site in Judaism and it is the right of every Jew to visit the Temple Mount".
The next day, September 29, 2000, Palestinians protested again in Jerusalem. These protests were more violent than the day before. The Israeli police fought against the protesters. Four Palestinians died in this fight. But the protests did not stop. Rather, the protests expanded over the whole of Israel and the Palestinian territories. The protests increased even more in violence. So did the reaction from the Israeli Police and the Israeli Army. The Palestinians protested especially against the Israeli occupation of Palestinian territories. Occupation means that the area is not part of the state Israel, but Israel controls and governs the territories. The protesters were also frustrated that the 2000 Camp David Summit had ended without a result. Such large protests against a goverment are also called an uprising. The arabic word for uprising is intifada. Some people translate intifada as "shaking off". The Palestinians already protested from 1987 until 1993. This earlier protest is known as the First Intifada, so the protests in 2000 are called the Second Intifada. But Sergio Catignani, a professor in Exeter, says that the First and the Second Intifada are very different. Also Boaz Ganor, a professor in Herzliya, says that the Second Intifada was more violent than the First Intifada. In addition, many people use the name "al-Aqsa Intifada". Al-Aqsa is the arabic name for the islamic buildings on the Temple Mount/al-Haram as-Sharif. During the Second Intifada, about 1000 Israeli and about 3400 to 4900 Palestinians died. Many more were wounded.
Some people say the Second Intifada started before Ariel Sharon went to the Temple Mount. Other people do not agree with this statement. They say that Ariel Sharon's visit to the Temple Mount was the trigger for the Second Intifada. But they also say that there are many more causes for the Second Intifada.
He met with President of the United States George W. Bush and the Palestinian Prime Minister Mahmoud Abbas on June 4, 2003, in Aqaba. The three agreed to the "roadmap for peace". This was another plan to bring peace to the Israeli-Palestinian conflict. But the roadmap for peace also failed.
In 2003, he said Israel will get out of the Gaza Strip. This surprised many people, Around 9000 Israeli settlers left the Gaza Strip in August 2005.
On 8 February 2005, Sharon went to Sharm El Sheikh in Egypt. There, he met with Mahmoud Abbas. Abbas was the new President of Palestine. This meeting stopped the Second Intifada.
Also in 2005, Sharon founded the Kadima political party and left the Likud party.
Critics
Many people criticized Ariel Sharon for his actions. They say Ariel Sharon often broke the law. They also say Ariel Sharon was responsible for violence and people getting killed.

Sharon wanted a "'greater Israel' from the Mediterranean to the Jordan River". As a general, he forced Palestinians from refugee camps to leave the Gaza strip in 1969. Because this was against the will of these Palestinians, this is called a deportation. Sharon often planned and ordered Israeli settlements in the West Bank and Gaza. The West Bank and Gaza were (and still are) occupied territories. But the Israeli settlements are connected to the state of Israel. The Israeli settlements are like Israeli cities outside of the state of Israel. Many people see Israeli settlements as illegal and breaking international law. As Prime Minister, Sharon changed his mind. He wanted to remove the Israeli settlements from the West Bank and Gaza. This was also criticized by some people because Sharon did not spoke with the Palestinians about it. The critics say Ariel Sharon did not treated the Palestinians as equal partners.
One of the biggest criticism against Ariel Sharon is the Sabra and Shatilla massacre in 1982. At that time, many Palestinian civilians were in refugee camps in Sabra and Shatilla. Sabra and Shatilla are in Lebanon. The Lebanese Phalange party went into the camps and killed many of those Palestinians. Between 800 and 3500 Palestinians died. At that time, Ariel Sharon was Israeli Minister of Defence. This means, he was the chief of the Israeli army. He allowed that the Phalange party could enter the Sabra and Shatilla camps. The Kahan Commission was an Israeli committee. It investigated the Sabra and Shatilla massacre. The Kahan Commission found that the Israeli army had a duty to care for the Palestinians in the camps. But the army did not care. Some people even say that Sharon was "the Butcher of Beirut". But he was still in the Israeli Government until 1992. In 2001, a criminal proceeding started against Ariel Sharon. The case was before a Belgian court. Sharon was accused of having committed war crimes in Sabra and Shatilla.
Ariel Sharon was also criticized for his Temple Mount visit in September 2000 .
Others have praised him for his success as a military general. He died from kidney failure and stroke complications at the hospital in Ramat Gan. He had been in a coma since 2006. He was aged 85.
His state funeral was held on 13 January. He was buried in line with Jewish burial customs. It was held as soon after death as possible. The day before the funeral, Sharon's body and coffin were placed in the Knesset building for people to see. His funeral began with an official ceremony held in the Knesset chamber. Politicians from Israel and other countries attended. His funeral was then held at his family's farm in the Negev desert. Sharon was buried there beside his wife, Lily.
Leaders attended his funeral service included U.S. Vice President Joe Biden, former British Prime Minister Tony Blair, former Dutch Prime Minister Wim Kok, Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov, Czech Prime Minister Jiří Rusnok and German Foreign Minister Frank-Walter Steinmeier.
