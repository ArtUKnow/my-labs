Zaza language
Zaza or Zazaki (Zazaki: zazakî or Zazaki), also known by other names, is an Iranian language mainly spoken in eastern Turkey, and is the native language of Zazas. It belongs to the northwest-Iranian group of Iranic languages. The number of people who speak Zaza is not known exactly.
Name
While the language is known as Zaza or Zazaki in many Western sources, the language or dialects has been known by a variety of other names by the people speaking it.
Dimili
One popular name is Dimili or Dimli (Zazaki: dimilî, Dımılî), also known by some speakers as dimbilî. The term is believed to either come from a region in Northern Iran known as Daylam, or the Kurdish Donboli tribe, also known as Dümbeli, which has existed in regions where Zaza is spoken. Some scholars have researched that the word Dimili might from a region in Northern Iran known as Daylam due to the similarity to languages in Daylam like Talysh or Semnani. Other scholars disagree with this and say that evidence is weak when you look at other things than language. Zazas were also known as Tmbli in some Armenian sources, and the sound changes that could turn a word like Dunbuli into Dimili is also documented.
Kirmanjki
Another popular name is Kirmanjki (Zazaki: kirmanckî or Kırmancki), which should not be confused with Kurmanji. They do have the same etymology. The word kirmanckî/Kırmancki comes from kirmanc/Kırmanc, which generally means a Kurd or an Alevi Kurd. Some researchers believe it only has the meaning of an Alevi, , but other researchers believe that the term can have multiple meanings at the same time.
Kirdki
Another name for the language is Kirdki (Zazaki: kirdkî or Kırdki), which means Kurdish.
Classification
Zaza is classified as a Northwestern Iranian language, and is according to Glottolog a part of the Adharic languages and is closely related to the languages here. It has traditionally been considered a Kurdish dialect, but especially due to early phonological (sound-related) differences, a better description would be a language rather than a dialect. However, Zaza is phonologically very similar to Kurmanji, and has been highly influenced by it through history.
It has sometimes been considered to be a part of the Zaza-Gorani language group. A researcher, Shuan Karim, also reconstructed Proto-Zaza-Gorani grammar in a peer-reviewed presentation at ICIL 09.
According to Jost Gippert, it might be especially close with Gorani and Semnani because of similar grammar.
