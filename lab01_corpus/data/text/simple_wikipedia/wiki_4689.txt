Void (astronomy)
In astronomy, voids are the empty spaces between filaments. Filaments and voids are the largest-scale structures in the Universe. There are no or few galaxies in voids. Most voids have a diameter of 11 to 150 Mpc.
Especially large voids are the empty spaces without many superclusters.
These voids are sometimes called supervoids.
A 1994 official counting lists a total of 27 supervoids with a distance of up to 740 Mpc.
