Criterion for capture or escape orbit
Consider a planet and a small body move on Keplerian orbits around a central mass and
their trajectories intersect such that both approach each other.
Three-body considerations aside, what would be a simple criterion such that the body is captured by the planet?
And if it got captured what are peri- and apoapsis of the resulting elliptical orbit around the planet?
Example:
Assuming the relative velocity of the body w.r.t. planet $v_\infty$ is smaller than the escape velocity at SOI distance
$$v_\infty < v_\text{esc}(SOI) = \sqrt{2} v_\text{orbit}(SOI),$$
then if the apoapsis equals the SOI radius the body is captured with periapsis given by
$$\frac{\mu v_\infty^2}{v_\text{orbit}^2(SOI) \left(v_\text{esc}^2(SOI)- v_\infty^2\right)}. $$
Addendum: Elliptical capture
As long as the relative speed of the body w.r.t. planet $v_\infty$ is not too low, i.e.,
$$v_\infty^2 > \frac{2 v_\text{orbit}^4(SOI)}{v_\text{orbit}^2(R)+v_\text{orbit}^2(SOI)}, $$
where $R$ is the planet radius, an elliptical
capture orbit around the planet does exist such that we have speed $v_\infty$ at SOI distance = apoapsis.
