Is it likely that independent asteroid deflection attempts would cancel each other?
In a scenario where:
Two nations that are not in diplomatic relations with each other, are both on the potential impact path of an asteroid.
Independently, each nation discovers the danger and plans a kinetic impactor deflection mission. Let's assume they are aiming for the asteroid to completely miss the Earth, instead of just fine-tuning the impact location.
The missions are timed close enough to each other that they cannot detect the orbital effects of the other impact in time to readjust their plans. So, practically, at most a couple of months difference.
Is it likely that the deflection missions would partially cancel each other's effects?
Intuitively it seems that for each launch window, there would be a specific impact angle that would provide the greatest effect. Then it wouldn't make sense for two opposite effect missions to be simultaneously launched.
(This question is inspired by the scenario mentioned in this answer to "Could India possibly deflect the asteroid 2024 Y4?", but not limited in scope to that specific asteroid.)
