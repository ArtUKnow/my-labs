Modern books on NewSpace/Microspace, Agile Manufacturing, SysEng
I am looking for modern books on the topics of New Space1 and Microspace management2 and/or agile satellite manufacturing3. I am interested to understand lessons learned over the last 10-15 years, for example, how companies managed to build and test more than 7000 satellites in a short amount of time, in as much of an in-depth treatment as possible (examples like Starlink, OneWeb, etc). I expect that such a treatment would unavoidably sit at the cross section of system-engineering, project-management, logistics etc.
Here are some of the questions that I hope to answer:
What type of real-world trade-offs did those teams have to decide on? For example, how did they know where to draw the line in terms of necessary vs. sufficient vs. exhaustive unit testing of their spacecraft components? I know that those can be answered by playing the "it depends" card, but I kindly ask for modern attempts to formalize this to a framework just like Fleeter did (See ref 1), but on the scale of modern satellite production.
Were some components tested more than others, and if so which ones and why?
I know that such teams do a lot of work once the satellite reaches orbit, for example calibration of their equipment. Which of those procedures were deferred to be performed on-orbit rather than before launch, and why?
What type of logistics and procuring had to be set-up? For example, some of us may be aware of Toyota's Just-In-Time procurement framework. Are you aware of its space industry counterpart?
The goal of my question is to try to demystify the "art" of Microspace and rather look for resources that shed some light on it instead of treating it as an art of proliferated, cheaper, faster satellite procurement.
I am aware of:
The Logic of Microspace, by Rick Fleeter, published in 2000
New Space System's Engineering, by Ignacio Chechile, published in 2021
Space Mission Engineering: The New SMAD by James Wertz and many others, published in 2011.
