JAXA/NASA astronauts on a Shuttle mission; was it like dual citizenship/joint appointment or did one agency take precedent?
Professor and former astronaut Takao Doi does many things, including being a Space Shuttle mission specialist, an amateur astronomer and discoverer of two supernovae 2002gw IN NGC 922 and Supernova 2007aa in NGC 4030, and being apparently the fourth person or the third person to throw a boomerang in space and the first to throw one specifically designed to work in microgravity.
Both the Wikipedia biography and the Rochester Astronomy.org page about SN 2007aa mention Doi's "dual citizenship" type association with two space agencies:
Wikipedia:
Space career: NASA/JAXA Astronaut
SN 2007aa:
Discovered by Takao Doi (USA/Japan). Note that Takao Doi is not only an amature astronomer, he is also a JAXA/NASA astronaut.
Setting aside the question of whether it's NASA/JAXA or JAXA/NASA, what does this mean? Is it akin to dual citizenship, or an academic joint appointment? Was Doi simultaneously both for an extended period of time or did they have to be one or the other while participating in STS-87 and STS-123?
During STS missions of that era, did astronauts report to and follow instructions solely from one space agency or the other, or was there a "chain of command" in that if one agency doesn't say either way, then the other agency has jurisdiction?
