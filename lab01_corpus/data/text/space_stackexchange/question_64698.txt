Apollo 13's plutonium RTG re-entry into the Tonga Trench: Good shootin' or good luck?
Plutonium powered RTGs are encased to survive re-entry. According to the Wikipedia article on Apollo 13
RTGs were used to power … the scientific experiments left
on the Moon by the crews of Apollo … Because the Apollo 13 Moon
landing was aborted, its RTG rests in the South Pacific Ocean[6]
The RTG was carried on the LM, which separated from the CM more than an hour before splash down and re-entered on its own about 500 miles uprange from Apollo 13.
Apollo 13's final midcourse correction had addressed the concerns of
the Atomic Energy Commission, which wanted the cask containing the
plutonium oxide intended for the SNAP-27 RTG to land in a safe place.
The impact point was over the Tonga Trench in the Pacific, one of its
deepest points, and the cask sank 10 kilometers (6 mi) to the bottom.
