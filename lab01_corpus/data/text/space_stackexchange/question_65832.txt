How can I calculate the altitude my waterrocket will fly with a certain amount of pressure?
I am trying to calculate how high my waterrocket will fly before launching it at different pressure using the Tsiolkovsky rocket equation. However, I stumbled upon an issue since the altitude that it flies changes drastically depending on how much air pressure is in the bottle, but air has almost no weight, meaning that the delta V barely changed in the equation.
$$ \Delta v = I_{sp} \cdot g_0 \cdot ln \frac{m_{before}}{m_{after}}$$
The Isp was easy to calculate since one can just find out the force and divide it by the mass flow rate (g doesn't matter that much since it is cross out from the equation).
$$ I_{sp}= \frac{Force}{mass\ flow\ rate \cdot g_0} = \frac{m_{dry\ mass} \cdot acceleration}{\frac{mass_{water}}{acceleration\ time} \cdot g_0} = \frac{m_{dry\ mass} \cdot \frac{velocity}{time}}{\frac{mass_{water}}{acceleration\ time} \cdot g_0}$$

$$ I_{sp}= \frac{m_{dry\ mass} \cdot \frac{velocity}{time}}{\frac{mass_{water}}{acceleration\ time} \cdot g_0} = \frac{0.25 kg \cdot \frac{50 m/s}{0.5 s}}{\frac{0.6 kg}{ 0.5s} \cdot 9.81m/s^2} = 2.1237s$$
$$ I_{sp}= \frac{m_{dry\ mass} \cdot \frac{velocity}{time}}{\frac{mass_{water}}{acceleration\ time} \cdot g_0} = \frac{0.42 kg \cdot \frac{30 m/s}{0.7 s}}{\frac{0.6 kg}{ 0.7s} \cdot 9.81m/s^2} = 2.1407s$$
From the calculations I rounded the Isp to about 2.1s since the slight difference comes from the fact that measuring certain things from video footage isn't the best way to get the data and there are of course better methods such as using an altimeter 3 or other sensors. Overall the Isp doesn't really change at different pressures.
The issue that I am having is that to calculate the delta V of the waterrocket, one of these values have to change $I_{sp}$, $ m_{before}$ or $m_{after}$. The Isp and the dry mass ($m_{after}$) doesn't change if there is more pressure. However, the wet mass ($m_{before}$) does change if there is more pressure, but only very slightly.
$$m_{before}= dry\ mass\ + water\ mass\ + air\ mass$$
