Calculation of TLE-like elements from osculating state vectors
I have simulated a time series of state vectors for a geostationary satellite, and I would like to calculate corresponding TLEs for this satellite. The simulation includes perturbations from Earth’s spherical gravity, solar radiation pressure (SRP), and third-body effects from the Sun and Moon.
This question is a follow-up to an older post, where it was suggested that the best approach for this conversion is to wrap a least-squares solver around an an SGP4 propagator and estimate the SGP4 input elements that best fit the state vectors over a time interval. Note that my goal is not to produce the most precise possible TLEs, but rather to generate synthetic TLEs that resemble real catalog TLEs as closely as possible in their behavior.
I have implemented this approach, but I still have several questions about specific details, and I have been unable to find clear references. I would appreciate guidance on the following:
1) Residual scaling (position vs. velocity)
I compute residuals by comparing predicted $\mathbf{r}_{SGP4}$, $\mathbf{v}_{SGP4}$ with osculating $\mathbf{r}_{osc}$, $\mathbf{v}_{osc}$. However, position is in km while velocity is in km/s, so I am unsure how best to combine or scale these residuals.
So far, I normalize position by 42165 km and velocity by 3.07 km/s (typical GEO radius and speed), which brings them to similar magnitudes. Is this a reasonable approach, or is there a more standard weighting strategy when fitting SGP4 states?
2) Typical fit interval length for GEO TLEs
What time spans are typically used when fitting TLEs for GEO satellites? I am currently using a 3-day fitting window.
3) Fit alignment relative to the epoch
How is the fit window usually aligned with respect to the TLE epoch? At the moment, to produce a TLE at time t, I fit observations from the previous 3 days (i.e., the epoch is at the end of the fit interval). Is it more common to center the fit window on the epoch instead?
4) Distribution of observations used in real TLE generation
My simulated osculating state vectors are sampled at 72 points per day (one every 20 minutes). I would like to subsample these to better resemble the distribution of real observations used in TLE generation, but I am not sure what that distribution looks like in practice.
My understanding is that GEO TLEs rely mainly on optical observations, so the observation times would reflect local nighttime and the locations of observing stations. Are there typical numbers of tracklets per day or typical arc lengths that would be realistic to emulate?
5) Minimum data requirements after maneuvers
In the presence of maneuvers, the fit diverges if I include pre-maneuver data, so I restrict the fit to observations after the maneuver. Is there a typical minimum arc length or minimum number of observations required to produce a stable TLE solution after a manoeuvre?
--
6) Are manoeuvres intervals always excluded from fitting
The presence of manoeuvre in fitted interval will cause the fit to not converge correctly, therefore to get best possible TLE, we should restrict the fitted interval to not contain manoeuvre.
However, is this done always in practice? Because if this is not always done in calculation of real TLEs, then real TLEs will contain some divergences, I since I want my synthetic TLEs to resemble real ones as close as possible, I want to have these divergencies.
Thanks in advance for any insights.
