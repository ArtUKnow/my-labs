Currently
There are several plans to build constellations of communications satellites in LEO with hundreds to thousands of spacecraft in each.
Constellations provide service "everywhere" or at least over large bands centered on the equator of necessity, because each sub-satellite point on Earth's surface is constantly moving.
Geostationary orbits allow for (nearly) fixed sub-satellite points on Earth's surface, so they can be dedicated to specific regions or countries.
How do commercial broadcast satellites in GEO produce such carefully shaped signal footprints?
However, Geosynchronous orbits for communications satellites, though much bigger in size, occupy a much smaller amount of phase space than LEO orbits for communications satellites, because they need to "stay in their place" or assigned slots in longitude for two important reasons
signals interference
collision avoidance
The first one is of course extremely important - it affects commercial viability. Without clean signals the big expensive satellites become useless. The second one is super-important for two reasons, one is of course avoiding loss of the two expensive satellite that collide, and the other is avoiding populating GEO with debris.
So most (but not all) communications satellites in GEO occupy a very skinny little "tube" around Earth's equator. It's thin in the radial direction in order to stay put in longitude, and thin in the vertical (polar, latitude) direction so that receiving dishes on Earth can be "set and forget" - they can be pointed towards a satellite manually, the screws tightened, and they just stay linked.
This skinny little tube is divided up into longitude slots. There are schemes to populate a slot with more than one satellite, in cases where this doesn't cause interference, but only a few occupants can "safely" do this

If this catches on...
If a trend starts, and it becomes popular to populate GEO with many smallsats, the nature of responsible station keeping in GEO for safety and signals interference reasons may change, the same way that it has for LEO.
