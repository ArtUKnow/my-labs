I define the following constants:$$r1 = 1.4959e11, r2 = 2.2792e11, G = 6.6743e-11, M = 1.9891e30$$
Let n be the finite number of impulses, and assume that $$Δ\dot{r}_{ik}, Δ\dot{θ}_{ik}$$ are given for the kth impulse.
Here, since M is the mass of the Sun, we set the origin as the Sun, the positive direction of the r coordinate as the radial direction away from the Sun, and the positive direction of the theta coordinate as the counterclockwise direction.
Based on the Lagrange equations in polar coordinates, we set
$$2\dot{r}\dot{θ}+r\ddot{θ} = 0$$
$$\ddot{r} - r\dot{θ}^2 + GMr^{-2} = 0$$
By providing the four initial conditions:
$r_{ok}, θ_{ok}, \dot{r}_{ok}, \dot{θ}_{ok}$
we can use a numerical solution method in Python to calculate:
$Δr_{k}(t_k), Δθ_{k}(t_k), Δ\dot{r}_{k}(t_k), Δ\dot{θ}_{k}(t_k)$
letting $t_{k}$ be the time until the next impulse. The initial conditions can be obtained by summing the functions from the previous k turns.
Under the conditions that:
$$\sum_{k=1}^n Δr_{k}(t_k)=r2-r1$$
$$\sum_{k=1}^n Δθ_{k}(t_k)≡θ_{mo}+\sum_{k=1}^n t_k(GM/{r2}^{3})^{1/2} (mod 2pi)$$
$$\sum_{k=1}^n Δ\dot{r}_{k}(t_k)+Δ\dot{r}_{ik}=0$$
$$\sum_{k=1}^n Δ\dot{θ}_{k}(t_k)+Δ\dot{θ}_{ik}=(GM/{r1}^{3})^{1/2}-(GM/{r2}^{3})^{1/2}$$
What do you think is the best way to determine the number ($n$), content of ($Δ\dot{r}_{ik}, Δ\dot{θ}_{ik}, t_{k}$), and $θ_{mo}$ (Initial angular position of the Mars) to minimise the following expression?
$$\sum_{k=1}^n Δv_{ik}*\sum_{k=1}^n t_{k}$$
Note that the impulse $Δv_{ij}$ is given as an angle $α_{ij}$ counterclockwise from the $+r$ direction centred on the rocket's position.
Therefore,
$$Δ\dot{r}_{ij} = Δv_{ij}cosα_{ij}$$
$$Δ\dot{θ}_{ij} = \frac{Δv_{ij}sinα_{ij}}{r1 + \sum_{k=1}^{j-1} Δr_{k}(t_k)}$$
