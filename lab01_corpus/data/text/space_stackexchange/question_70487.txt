Were the NASA Skyfall mission visualizations taken from an older proposal, or is Skyfall the realization of the older proposal's plans?
While scrolling Project Rho, which is a nice hard-sci-fi website, I was looking through the list of engines they have documented and found this image:
I looked up the source of the image and found this PDF which indeed brings up a spacecraft that looks the same.
The reason I am curious about this is because this seems to be exactly the same spacecraft that has been seen numerous times in NASA imagery for the recently-announced Skyfall mission:
The similarities are obvious: a nuclear reactor on the end of a truss-stick, a cuboidal body with a particular pattern of radiator panels protruding from the edges, and ion engine pods and other miscellanies in back.
I find it believable that this spacecraft could have multiple mission targets over its lifetime. See this image found in the PDF, after a section documenting its multiple-mission profile:
Figure 4.2-4 also depicts how the NEP module and the Chemical Propulsion Module are used separately to propel the two cargo predeployment missions. The NEP module can propel all three landers to Mars on subsequent missions, when the first lander is not required to predeploy before the remaining two as a capability verification.
Emphasis mine, see page 31 in the linked PDF. This is also only one of the two discussed spacecraft, the other being a nuclear-thermal variant that seems like it would cost more based on the provided figures, but they both have multiple mission configurations, including cargo and human transport. It also seems to have a methalox booster to get it out of Earth orbit quickly and probably save more-expensive ion fuel.
So SR-1 and Skyfall could just very well be the space tug being discussed here and its first mission, respectively, rather than a completely-new mission that happens to use this old design.
My question is have this spacecraft's plans been around for a while and is now being taken up and repurposed for Skyfall, or was it always in development for general purpose and now this is just its first announced mission? Clearly the newest rendition (third image) is slightly different from the old report but it looks basically the same, and is indeed a nuclear-electric spacecraft like NASA's planned SR-1 ship. The report is from 2023, which is awfully recent in advanced-propulsion-systems-planning terms, so is the announcement of SR-1 and Skyfall just an announcement of a plan that has already been in development for a while, or was this spacecraft sitting on the shelf and then Skyfall etc. were asked for by Isaacman et al. and this spacecraft was brought up as the best option?
If it is the case that there is some statement from NASA that I have been unable to find that would indicate that they intend to use SR-1 for other purposes after Skyfall, that would answer my question, since it would indicate that it indeed seems to be the plan of the agency to follow the plan laid out in the linked PDF.
