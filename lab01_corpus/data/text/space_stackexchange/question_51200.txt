How much does the SpaceX Starship flex during the Flop maneuver?
Poring over the woes (and sucesses) of Starship prototypes SN 5-11, I had read they were trying a thinner stainless steel skin in later iterations.
The flop maneuver places the centers of drag at the ends (fins), while the center of mass is near the middle. This is not unlike the strategy of breaking a stick over your knee.
SN5 and SN6 performed flawlessly, but their bodies were subjected only to shorter term vertical loading, without the flop descent configuration.
Judging by the (notorious) gas leaks and explosions of 8-11, could excessive flexing (and potential plumbing damage) be avoided with a stronger, perhaps more squat design?
Successful re-entry vehicles, such as capsules and shuttles, seem a bit more symmetrical, which adds strength.
