How can one collect interstellar dust using lasers or magnetic fields?
In this episode from Isaac Arthur about building artificial stars, he mentions that most mass (ignoring dark matter and energy) is not contained in the stars themselves, but in the interstellar dust the lurks between them. Roughly a single cubic light year of the interstellar medium has the same amount of mass that our sun has.
Isaac explained, that a thin sheet of material could be used to scoop it up. Graphene, which weighs about 0.77 milligrams per square meter, could be used for this purpose. A thin sheet across a square light year would contain (365 * 86400s * 300 000 000 m/s)^2 * 0.77 mg = 6.89e25 kg of carbon. That's only 10 times the mass of earth, an amount that could be star-lifted from a star.
At 9:33 in the video he suggests that the dust would preferably be collected using lasers or magnetic fields instead of sending a large thin sheet or a fleet of ships to physically scoop up the dust. However, he doesn't elaborate on it. The latter of those also seems odd to use, since magnetic fields get exponentially weaker over distance.
