Can any electric rockets utilize in-situ asteroid materials as reaction mass?
Asteroids have materials of high value with potential for commercial mining but the rocketry required to support it appears to be one of the biggest challenges, with high costs. In mass terms the fuels or reaction mass for getting to and from the asteroid represent one of, if not the largest payload requirements; any rockets that require fuels or reaction mass sourced from Earth will be at a disadvantage compared to those that can utilize asteroid materials. High reliability and durability rockets that need little ongoing supply from Earth seems required to improve the cost effectiveness, which is essential to commercial viability.
Chemical rockets using H2/O2 appear possible using electrolysis of water extracted from carbonaceous material but, if I understand correctly, Hydrogen fuel needs very large tanks and other challenges and have lower efficiencies than solar electric (arc-jets?), so have made the question about the latter. Are there any combinations of rocket type and rocket "fuel" that could rely on relatively simple to process in-situ resources?
