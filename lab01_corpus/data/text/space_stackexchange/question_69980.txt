How to calculate Delta-v during a powered flyby
Assume a spacecraft approaches a body, say Mars (velocity $\vec{V_\text{M}}$), with heliocentric velocity $\vec{V_0}$ to do a flyby. After the assist we'd like to have heliocentric velocity $\vec{V_1}$ such that during the assist the thrusters have to be fired causing a $\Delta v$ for this maneuver.
What is the correct formula for the minimal $\Delta v$ needed?
Remark:
For about 6 months I thought
$$
\Delta v = \Big|\|\vec{V_0} - \vec{V_\text{M}}\|\ - \|\vec{V_1} - \vec{V_\text{M}}\|\Big|,
$$
which is the difference of the relative speeds (using the assist to get the desired direction), is correct. But it's not, as it gives different values depending on if we approach Mars from the leading (in front) or trailing side (behind), to lose or gain speed.
A correct formula should
be independent of the sign of $\vec{V_\text{M}}$ (i.e. $\vec{V_\text{M}}$ and $-\vec{V_\text{M}}$ give the same value)$\ \ $ (Update: this is not true, unless the other vectors flip signs as well)
give the same result if $\vec{V_0}$ and $\vec{V_1}$ are switched in the formula
