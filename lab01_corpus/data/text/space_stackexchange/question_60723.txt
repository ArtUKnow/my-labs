Can you identify this space travel related equation?
Does anyone recognize the equation carved on this object?
The only hint I have is that the back side has the word "orbit" carved into it.
It may be incomplete or incorrectly copied.
My best interpretation in MathJax:
$U_eR_o\sqrt{\frac{2g}{(R_ôh)}}$
My grandmother carved the object. She had a hobby of carving small figures and objects out of blackboard chalk. She made (among other things) chess figures and a scene with her brother posing with his new car.
This figure was presumably carved during the space race in the 1960s.
Grandmother was a grade school teacher, so I'm pretty sure she didn't understand it and just copied it from a magazine or newspaper article.
The mystery has been solved. Two things confused everyone:
Grandmother left an equals sign out of the equation.
She had to squeeze a plus sign in after things got too tight. The squished in plus sign looked like it was a "hat" on an O.
This is the correct form as deciphered and recognized by GandalfDDI in the accepted answer:
$$V_e = R_0\sqrt{\frac{2g}{R_0+h}}$$
This is a formula for escape velocity that uses acceleration at the planet surface together with the starting height above the surface.
Explanation:
$V_e$ escape velocity in meters per second
$R_0$ radius of planet in meters
$g$ acceleration at the planet's surface in meters per second squared ($m/s^2$)
$h$ height above planet's surface in meters.
Assuming for Earth:
$R_0$ = radius at the equator = 6,378 km = 6378000 meters
$g$ = 9.81 $m/s^2$
$h$ = 0 meters (starting at sea level)
Then:
$V_e = 6378000 \times \sqrt{2\times9.81/6378000}= 11180$ meters per second
That matches the value of 11.186 km/s given on Wikipedia:
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escape_velocity
Using h allows you to figure the escape velocity from any elevation above the Earth's surface.
