Could the Space Shuttle Reenter upside down?
I understand that the space shuttle utilized aerodynamic steering during its entry phase. While I am sure most of their control objectives could be achieved with very shallow bank angle, I am curious if any missions required more dramatic banking.
I can envision (quite vividly with Kerbal Space Program) utility for extreme bank angles if significant turning is needed. Additionally, I can envision utility for bank beyond 90 degrees if the descent rate needs increasing.
So the questions are as follows:
What bank angle bounded typical reentry maneuvers?
Are there any notable instances where extreme bank (beyond that which bounded typical reentry maneuvers) was employed?
Were 'Inverted' reentry attitudes ever used or considered?
To be clear, I am talking about bank angles independent of angle of attack. There is no conflict in these attitudes with heat shielding.
