Are there currently any (realistic) proposals using von Neumann machines?
I read a while back that MIT’s Microsystems Technology Laboratories has demonstrated an FDM 3D-printer that can fabricate complex mechanical parts, like linear motors, from ground-up with minimal post-processing. When I read this, the first thing that popped into my head was “well, if you can print servo motors and other complex actuators, and if NASA has already demonstrated 3D-printed rocket nozzles (and by extension 3D-printer nozzles) and if circuit boards can be printed from scratch compactly as well, then it seems to me like you could use MTL’s printer to print another copy of itself!” and my first subsequent thought was how potentially useful it would be to build a self-replicating robot that could handle interplanetary construction and ISRU for us before we decide we need humans on the Moon/Mars/Titan/etc.
Given the probable quantities of alumina that can be found in Lunar regolith to be utilized for ISRU, and the grants already being handed out for Lunar regolith mining and manufacturing in addition to NASA’s apparent increased ambitions for new space tech, it seems like the proposals and programs might not be that far off, even though a full-blown von Neumann machine is probably a few decades away.
So: are there any serious current mission proposals (probably in the not-near future, given the absent technology) that make use of von Neumann machines to do ISRU or accomplish other mission goals?
