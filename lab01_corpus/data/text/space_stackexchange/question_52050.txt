Does China pay for damages caused by deorbiting space junk?
When China launches a big rocket, they apparently don't control the deorbit and there are large enough pieces to strike the surface and even cause damage to buildings, as happened in the Ivory Coast last year.
Because the Earth's surface is mostly ocean, the debris will most likely land there (not good for marine life in the way!), as with today's debris landing in the Indian ocean. However, the uncontrolled deorbit seems to be designed in as a feature, not a bug, perhaps due to lack of liability for any associated damages.
If debris from such a rocket were to strike my home, car, kid, boat, passenger-aircraft-in-flight, etc., is there a claims process by which the Chinese launch authority will pay for those damages? (Assume there is no insurance held by the owner which would cover such damages, as the probability of any specific individual being affected in that way is very low.)
