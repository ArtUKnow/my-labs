Origin of the term "ducosyn"
While researching this answer, I came across the term "ducosyn," which as nearly as I can tell right now is jargon unique to particular parts of gyroscopes for the Polaris missile and their descendants, the Apollo IRIGs.
A ducosyn consists of a separate magnetic suspension assembly, a separate transducer microsyn mounted as a single coaxial-coplanar unit, and contains two separate stators mounted to the case and two separate rotors mounted on a common ring on the float assembly. The signal generator ducosyn was mounted on the positive output axis end of the float to provide magnetic suspension and serve as a transducer to provide an electrical analog signal proportional to the position of the float. A torque generator ducosyn was mounted on the negative output axis end of the float to provide magnetic suspension and serve as a transducer to convert an electrical error signal into a torque about the output axis.
(Figure from Draper Labs' March 1970 Design Survey of the Apollo Inertial Subsystem, the ducosyns are at either end of the assembly, labeled only by their parts: the suspension and the torque/signal generators)
"Ducosyn" would appear to be a portmanteau, perhaps of three words, but it must have some origin. I want to know if that origin is documented by any publicly-available references, including but not limited to documents preceding the 1970 MIT Labs document containing a definition of what a ducosyn is.
