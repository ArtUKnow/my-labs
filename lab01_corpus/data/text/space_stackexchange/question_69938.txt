Can solar power be made practical in the main Asteroid Belt?
If asteroid mining and/or rotating habitat colonisation are ever to be made practical, there will have to be a good source of energy in the main Asteroid Belt (the one between Mars and Jupiter); in the near-term, imports of nuclear material from Earth can work, but, as far as I can see it realistically being, a self-sustaining asteroid economy will require the Asteroid Belt to produce its own energy resources.
However, as far as I know, most asteroids are very low in fissile isotopes, making a nuclear-powered asteroid economy unlikely, and prompting the need to find an alternative energy source in the Asteroid Belt.
I have often heard it said that anywhere beyond the orbit of Mars sunlight becomes too diffuse for using solar energy to be practical, but I am sceptical; after all, mirrors for focusing sunlight do not have to weigh much, and, even if solar arrays have to be quite large, there is no shortage of room in space in which to put them.
Am I correct in saying that solar power could, contrary to what is often claimed, be practically used in the main Asteroid Belt, or is there, in fact, some reason I am not aware of which would make large solar arrays and/or focusing mirrors impractical?
