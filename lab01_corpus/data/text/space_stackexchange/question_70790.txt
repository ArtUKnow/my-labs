What open-source reference data is used to validate 6-DOF orbital rendezvous models
I am developing an autonomous 6-DOF mathematical model for orbital motion and rendezvous (specifically for a Soyuz-TMA and ISS scenario). The model is written in C++ and is completely independent of any game engine (no Unreal Engine, Unity, etc.).
I am currently at the stage of Verification and Validation (V&V) and looking for industry best practices regarding reference data.
What is already implemented in my model:
· Integrators: RK4 (fixed step ≤ 0.01s) and adaptive RKF45.
· Gravity: J2-J8 zonal harmonics and full EGM96 8x8 model, including Sun/Moon third-body perturbations and gravity-gradient torques.
· Atmosphere: 19-layer density model (US Std 1976 + MSISE-90) with F10.7 and Ap space weather parameters.
· Earth Orientation: Full J2000 to ECEF transformation (IAU-1976 precession, IAU-1980 nutation, GAST, polar motion).
· Target: ISS is modeled as an active multi-body orbital target with specific docking nodes (SM, MIM1, MIM2, SO1) and contact wrenches.
I have already passed internal unit tests for the control loops, but now I need to validate the model against external real-world physics.
My questions are:
What are the standard open-source reference datasets (e.g., JPL ephemerides, NASA orbital decay databases like ORDEM/DTM) used by the industry to validate orbital propagation accuracy over a 1-2 day period?
Are there any open-access academic papers or publicly available telemetry datasets containing actual Soyuz-ISS rendezvous trajectories (from 1 km to docking) that could be used as a benchmark for validating the relative navigation phase?
Any specific links to standards (like NASA-STD-7009), datasets, or papers on validation methodologies would be highly appreciated.
