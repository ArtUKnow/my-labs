Calculating the wall thickness in a hollow rotating cylinder space habitat
I was reading through the NASA Space Settlements: A Design Study from 1977 and they provided equations for the wall thickness of different space habitat shapes on page 111:
I wanted to calculate the wall thickness required for a hollow cylinder, since it wouldn't need all the air in the center, which becomes an unnecessary large portion of the mass in large space habitats. So in the equation for regular cylinder habitats, I replaced the equivalent pressure of pseudogravity, where $r_1$ is the outer radius and $r_2$ is the inner radius, with:
\begin{equation}
p_g = 1/2 \cdot \rho \cdot \omega^2 \cdot (r_1^2 - r_2^2)
\end{equation}
Since I am adding that, in the denominator I will also have to add $\omega^2$. For a hollow cylinder, the wall thickness becomes:
\begin{equation}
t = \frac{p_0 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \rho \cdot \omega^2 \cdot (r_1^2 - r_2^2)}{\sigma_w - \rho \cdot \omega^2 \cdot R} \cdot R
\end{equation}
However, I got inconsistent units and values that seemed off. Therefore I think this equation is false. Therefore, what is the equation for calculating the wall thickness required in a rotating hollow cylindrical space habitat?
Edit:
Since some of the comments mention the $\omega^2$ may be the issue, I want to clarify that the equation still isn't dimensionally sound. If we take the equation without the $\omega^2$ we get:
\begin{equation}
t = \frac{p_0 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \rho \cdot \omega^2 \cdot (r_1^2 - r_2^2)}{\sigma_w - \rho \cdot R} \cdot R = [m]
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
[m] = \frac{\frac{[kg] \cdot [m]}{[s^2] \cdot [m^2]} + \frac{[kg]}{[m^3]} \cdot \frac{1}{[s^2]} \cdot ([m^2] - [m^2])}{\frac{[kg] \cdot [m]}{[s^2] \cdot [m^2]} - \frac{[kg]}{[m^3]} \cdot [m]} \cdot [m] = \frac{\frac{[kg]}{[s^2]} + \frac{[kg]}{[s^2]}}{\frac{[kg]}{[s^2] \cdot [m]} - \frac{[kg]}{[m^2]}} = \frac{\frac{[kg] \cdot [m^2]}{[s^2] \cdot [m^2]}}{\frac{[kg] \cdot [m]}{[s^2] \cdot [m^2]} - \frac{[s^2] \cdot [kg]}{[s^2] \cdot [m^2]}} = \frac{[kg] \cdot [m^2]}{[kg] \cdot [m] - [s^2] \cdot [kg]} = \frac{[m^2]}{[m] - [s^2]}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
[m] != \frac{[m^2]}{[m] - [s^2]}
\end{equation}
The $\omega^2$ was there to try to fix this issue with the units, but it also didn't work. I probably shouldn't have added it, but it is not the core issue.
