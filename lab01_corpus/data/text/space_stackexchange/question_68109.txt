Has a radio frequency linear accelerator design been used for Ion thrusters?
Electrostatic ion thrusters use electrically charged grids to accelerate ions.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gridded_ion_thruster#/media/File:Electrostatic_ion_thruster-en.svg
In contrast, a linear accelerator (LINAC) exposes charged particle to a series of oscillating radio frequency electric fields along a linear beamline.
https://testbook.com/physics/linear-accelerator
Click the link for a nice .gif from Wikipedia:
https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/08/Linear_accelerator_animation_16frames_1.6sec.gif
Note that, in the above diagram, tubes become longer along the length of the beamline to compensate for acceleration of the particles. In medical and industrial LINACs, particles reach relativistic speed so additional energy mostly goes into increasing their mass rather than speed. As a result the spacing becomes uniform towards the discharge end of the accelerator.
Relativistic exhaust velocity should result in truly astronomical Isp.
Has the LINAC design been applied to ion thrusters? Does it have potential advantages over electrostatic thrusters?
