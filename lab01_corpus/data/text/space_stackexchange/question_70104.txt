How far away are we from continuous global satellite surveillance?
For decades now, it has been a popular conspiracy theory that "they" are monitoring everyone's movements through satellites. This hasn't been true, as even though high resolution imaging satellites have been available, they've had to focus on a small area at a time and were limited by their orbits. This has prevented global surveillance.
But recently satellites using SAR (Synthetic-aperture radar) have seen rapid development and cost reduction.
ICEYE commercially provides data with "resolutions up to 25 cm and scenes up to 120,000 km²" every 6 hours, using a constellation of about 20 satellites. Capture time for single images is reported to be only tens of seconds. Based on public financial information, the company appears to have spent a total of around 500 million euros during its existence, which is not a lot compared to space budgets of large military powers.
Being able to continuously record all outdoors activity globally at a resolution high enough to track individual people, day and night, and store it would have huge implications. It would tremendously simplify military intelligence operations and solving of crimes, but also affect privacy of citizens and enable more government oppression.
Is there some technological barrier that still prevents this from becoming a reality, or is it only a matter of time? (And how long time?)
