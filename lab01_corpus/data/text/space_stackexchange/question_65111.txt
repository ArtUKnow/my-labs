What figure-of-merit (FoM) is used to characterize the performance of a launch system's reentry-and-landing system?
I'd like to make a table that compares the performance of different launch systems such as The Space Shuttle, Falcon 9 plus Crew Dragon, and Starship. Engine ISP and thrust-to-weight ratio would be important metrics for launch. Another relevant performance metric might be the ratio of the rocket's dry mass to its wet mass or its payload.
However, all three of the aforementioned systems allocate a significant portion of the total mass that reaches orbit to reentry-and-landing system components, including, for example, wings, thermal tiles, head shields, propellant for landing burns, parachutes, landing gear, flaps, etc.
My first guess is that the figure of merit for a reentry-and-landing system would be something like...
$$\epsilon = (m_O - m_D - m_P)/m_O$$
Where:
$\epsilon$ is the reentry-and-landing system figure-of-merit (smaller is better),
$m_O$ is the total mass of everything that achieves a circular orbit,
$m_D$ is the dry mass of everything that was essential for achieving the circular orbit, and
$m_P$ is the mass of the payload.
Is there a reentry-and-landing system figure-of-merit like this that has already been established within the rocket launch industry and, if so, has it been documented anywhere?
