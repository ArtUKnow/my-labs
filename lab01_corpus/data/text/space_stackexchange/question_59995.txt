Using CEA for monopropellants
CEA (Chemical Equilibrium with Applications) calculates chemical equilibrium compositions and properties of complex mixtures and is the industry standard. Applications include assigned thermodynamic states, theoretical rocket performance, Chapman-Jouguet detonations, and shock-tube parameters for incident and reflected shocks.
I've only just begun to use CEA, and whilst I've found it to be very useful for bipropellants, monopropellants are a little weird.
Specifically, I was calculating the performance of 85% HTP monoporop at 15 bar and Cp/Ep of of 15, and found it to have a specific impulse of 1846 ms^-1 and a chamber temp. of 2069k. On Astronautix however, I found the chamber temp to be ~910k and the ISP 1147 ms^-1. This dissonance is too great to attribute to varying Cp, expansion ratios or even HTP concentration. Is there a special mechanic for monopropellants, rather than merely selecting "none" in the oxidiser menu, have I made a blunder elsewhere in the propellant setup, or have I misunderstood something else entirely?
