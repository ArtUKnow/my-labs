Some examples of an "increasing number of defunct satellites zip(ping) around in space, producing all kinds of weird bleeps and bloops"?
Gizmodo's June 24, 2025 NASA Satellite That’s Been Dead for 57 Years Sends Mysterious Signal to Earth describes an interesting event.
A radio transient event was detected by fast signal processing at the Australian
Square Kilometre Array Pathfinder, ASKAP, triggering a dump of all the digitized radio signals for offline analysis.
It might have been missed because it was so peculiar - the timing pattern across the large 6 km footprint of the array was not that of a plane wave front, it was curved. And the dispersion (different arrival times for different frequencies, i.e. "chirp") due to free electrons along the path was much smaller than typical for distant objects.
From the spherical wavefront across the ASKAP array, they were able to not only pinpoint the direction in the sky, but also the distance - only a out 4500 km.
The conclusions of their paper A nanosecond-duration radio pulse originating from the defunct Relay 2 satellite:
We consider an electrostatic discharge (ESD) or plasma discharge following a micrometeoroid impact to be plausible explanations for the burst.
However the last sentence of the original Gizmodo article gives me pause:
As an increasing number of defunct satellites zip around in space, producing all kinds of weird bleeps and bloops, it’ll become harder for scientists to distinguish whether it’s the cosmos talking—or just some piece of malfunctioning hardware.
Question: Is there really an "increasing number of defunct satellites zip(ping) around in space, producing all kinds of weird bleeps and bloops"? If so, what are some examples?
