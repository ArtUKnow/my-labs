SATGUS orbit question
Mark Rober launched a satellite named SATGUS which displays photos, and takes a picture of them with Earth in the background. Here is a video describing it and the video of the launch. At 6:02 Tim says "SATGUS is heading out into a sun synchronous orbit".
I'm curious as to exactly what that means. I interpret that as something like one orbit every 365 days (or some multiple of that), and you'd have to be out as far as the L1/L2 points to achieve that. But the current data at Heavens Above shows it orbiting about 15 times per day, a pretty typical orbital period. The only thing that stands out is the high inclination, giving it a polar orbit.
I would also expect it's ground track to either be in permanent daylight, or permanent night, or at the very least, more in one than the other. But the photo below shows it spending about equal times in both night and day. Based on the photos, it did not seem to have any major method of propulsion left to significantly change it's orbit.
