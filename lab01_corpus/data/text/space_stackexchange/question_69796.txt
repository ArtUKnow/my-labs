Can a showerhead nozzle greatly simplify engine construction without serious performance panelities?
Here we established that a nozzle-less engine cannot achieve an exhaust velocity above the speed of sound.
But the fluids continue to accelerate after leaving the engine. Which brings us to the next question:
As earlier, the objective us to simplify construction by simplifying the shape of the nozzle.
In this case, I am drawing inspiration from the gas-bleed variant of the aerospike. The approach is to place a plate at the end of the combustion chamber and drill several small holes in it. The area of the holes will be just a fraction of the plate area. As earlier, the sideways expansion of the plumes would cause them to interfere, but at higher velocity. This is supposed to create masses of slow moving high pressure gasses between the plumes that act as diverging nozzles for the faster moving gas.
But I have questions as to how well the pressure from these "gas bells" would transfer to the plate, given the flows that form them are moving supersonically at no less than a right angle. And about how happy the gas would be about the resulting powerful shock - we already have pressure loss issues in supersonic flows. And whether cooling a flat plate with holes and gas flowing every which way really is simpler than cooling a nozzle with flows running parallel to the coolant pipes.
