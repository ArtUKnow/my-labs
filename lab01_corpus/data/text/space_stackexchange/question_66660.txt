Could we convert lunar regolith into a trinitite landing pad with an atomic weapon?
My thinking here is that a modern thermonuclear weapon would not weigh very much and that by detonating one an appropriate distance above the lunar surface, the regolith could be melted into a hard flat surface for a lander to land on. Then the landers' landing gear mass could be reduced, lunar landings would be de-risked, the area immediately around the landing site would become a relatively dust-free environment, and utility vehicles could move around on regular wheels while unloading cargo and setting up a base. Overall, the strategy might result in a net payload mass saving for a program like Artemis.
The weapon would have to be detonated days or weeks before the landing attempt to give the molten regolith time to cool and harden.
I suspect that most of the nuclear fallout would fly off into deep space. Any that reached the moon would be dispersed widely, not creating a significant hazard. Is it possible that the pros of this idea could outweigh the cons?
