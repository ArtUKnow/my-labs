It was all going well until up to three-quarters of my project, I just realized "how exactly does a space-stabilized INS, actually stabilize?" NB: space-stabilized INS means that the gyro and accelerometer triads' orientation are kept stable with respect to inertial frame.
Because of all the topics that I've make a summary on, from the gyros (displacement gyro, rate gyro, rate-integrating gyro) and the gimbals (torquer motors, resolver or synchros), I still didn't find any deep explanation on how a space-stabilization mechanism actually works. All that I've got is from the book Fundamentals Of High Accuracy Inertial Navigation by Andrew A. Chatfield, and the book Inertial Navigation Systems Analysis by Kenneth R. Britting.
Since the space-stabilized system instruments an inertially nonrotating
coordinate system the gyros are nominally untorqued. ...Ideally the compensation commands cancel with the deterministic drift
and the platform is nonrotating relative to the inertial frame. Thus the
commanded platform angular velocity is equal to the desired platform
angular velocity which is equal to zero; $$ \omega_c^g = \omega_{ip}^p = 0 $$ where $ \omega_c^g $ specifies the commanded rotation rate, and $ \omega_{ip}^i $ being the desired angular velocity of platform. [Britting, Kenneth R. (1971)]
For stabilization to an ECI coordinate system, the platform remains untorqued
except for calibration torques. Thus, $$ \omega_c^p = \delta\omega_{cal}^p $$ [Chatfield, Andrew A. (1997)]. $ \omega_c^p $ denotes stable platform commanded rate, and $ \delta\omega_{cal}^p $ denotes small calibration rate.
