Has any planetary lander used the paratrooper’s lowering line technique to manage impact?
Paratroopers sometimes use a lowering line to land heavy gear before the jumper lands. They can dangle a heavy pack of durable gear on a tether so it lands first, or even release the gear at an altitude of a few hundred feet. The pack hits fast and hard. The jumper has a few seconds to decelerate since the chute is no longer supporting the weight of the gear. The jumper lands slower and lighter, unencumbered by gear.

Has any planetary or lunar lander used this technique, splitting payload items into impact resistant items (say a ground penetrator) and more delicate items?
The principle would apply to propulsive landers as well as parachutes.
This question was inspired by Mars Air braking and Landing which suggested that ejecting an "indestructable" subunit during re-entry would slow the speed of the parent re-entry vehicle. Such a strategy relies on momentum (mv) transfer, not kinetic energy (1/2mv^2) so it would be equally effective at low velocity as at high velocity.
If the lowering line technique scrubbed even a few m/sec off the main lander, it could "earn its keep" by reducing the mass of parachutes or propulsive landing rockets.
