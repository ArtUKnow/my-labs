Why did 65%-92.5% throttle cause excessive erosion of the LEM descent engine?
Wikipedia's article on the LMDE mentions that "operation between 65% and 92.5% thrust was avoided to prevent excessive nozzle erosion." This seems a bit counterintuitive as operating at full thrust would result in more nozzle erosion due to more hot gases being expelled.
Looking at NASA's Apollo engine specifications indicates that the LMDE was ablatively-cooled (similar to the ablative cooling on the RS-68), which seems a bit confusing as to why the aforementioned throttle range would cause more erosion of either the nozzle itself or the ablator. A possible theory I've had in my mind (with the lack of other relevant sources from my preliminary research) is that 100% throttle still caused significant nozzle erosion but was necessary for a large portion of the descent while deeper throttle control was only needed for the last few moments prior to touchdown.
Is there a particular reason to why this excessive nozzle erosion occurred?
