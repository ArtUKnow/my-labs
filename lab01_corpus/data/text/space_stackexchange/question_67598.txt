Growing plants on Mars
I've just been watching Scott Manley's video on Elon Musk's early plans to demonstrate a "greenhouse" growing relatively-familiar plants on Mars
He mentions that seeds would be in "eggbox-like" cells filled with a gel growth medium, which would at an appropriate point be hydrated from a water container provided it had not frozen (otherwise energy would have to be expended to thaw it).
Since there are numerous plants which can survive temperatures well below zero, and since a growth medium could presumably be engineered to be relatively insensitive to temperature, this makes the water supply particularly vulnerable in this type of experiment.
Leaving aside the possibility of an antifreeze mix (e.g. water plus some proportion of ethylene glycol) ** , could the water be replaced by some other compound with a lower freezing point which would either be processed by the growth medium or accepted directly by the plants?
** Updated: I feel I need to be more specific here. While my initial curiosity related to some alternative compound which the plant could in some way convert to H2O when needed, I'm not necessarily discounting an aqueous solution or mixture which could depress the freezing point while leaving H2O available for uptake. However I don't want to sound like the politician who suggested curing COVID-19 by injecting people with bleach.
