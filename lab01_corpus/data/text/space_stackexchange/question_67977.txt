Has a space mission ever failed due to an incorrect understanding of physics?
I just watched the excellent 3Blue1Brown video series with Terence Tao explaining how we learned about sizes and distances of objects in our solar system and beyond. The fact that we’re able to send space probes to planets, moons, and asteroids serves as a great confirmation that we really do understand sizes and distances, at least locally. It would be interesting if there were a counterexample to this — something like “our probe to Jupiter failed because we had the wrong orbital parameters” or “our probe got crushed by Venus’s atmosphere because we totally had the wrong values for the pressure there.”
Are there any examples of space missions that have failed where the root cause was either a fundamental misunderstanding of some physical principle or a completely wrong value for some mass, distance, radiation level, etc.? This would not include things like unit conversion errors (e.g. we had the right value but used it wrongly), engineering mistakes (e.g. we understood the conditions that were expected but didn’t build the probe correctly), or where the lack of knowledge was explicitly considered in the mission design (e.g., we knew there was a magnetic field but didn’t know how strong it would be until the probe arrived).
