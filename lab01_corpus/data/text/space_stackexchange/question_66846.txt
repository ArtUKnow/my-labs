How cheap would rocket fuel have to be to make Mars colonization feasible (according to Musk)?
Elon Musk tweeted recently:
SpaceX created the first fully reusable rocket stage and, much more importantly, made the reuse economically viable.
Making life multiplanetary is fundamentally a cost per ton to Mars problem.
It currently costs about a billion dollars per ton of useful payload to the surface of Mars. That needs to be improved to $100k/ton to build a self-sustaining city there, so the technology needs to be 10,000 times better. Extremely difficult, but not impossible.
Currently, launching Falcon Heavy costs \$90M. \$0.5M of this cost is fuel. But if technology costs were reduced by a factor 10,000, as Musk wants (through reuse, economies of scale, etc.), then fuel cost would dominate by a large margin.
Reaching Mars requires energy. On top of this, rockets waste most of their propellant accelerating the remaining propellant (rather than the payload).
Taking that into account (and ignoring the rocket's dry mass, for simplicity), I wonder how cheap the propellant would have to be to make the cost of reaching Mars equal $100k/ton?
