Why did Freeman Dyson suggest a speed limit of around 0.01c for "Super-Orion"
Jeremy Berstein had a piece at https://inference-review.com/article/reflections-on-project-orion reflecting on Freeman Dyson and Project Orion, and at the end he includes a foreword that Dyson wrote for what would have been a new edition of his son George's book about Orion. In the foreword Dyson wrote:
A second limitation of Orion is the upper limit on its speed set by the laws of nuclear physics. Even the most explosive nuclear reactions release less than one per cent of the mass-energy of the reacting atoms. When we dreamed of traveling to the stars on a mythical vehicle, which we called Super-Orion, we found that the speed limit set by the energy yield of bombs powered by fission or by fusion is about 2,000 miles per second. This is a hundred times as fast as we needed for exploring the solar system, but a hundred times slower than the speed of light.
Anyone know anything (or have any speculations) what sort of calculation Dyson was talking about that gave this conclusion? I know that specific impulse takes into account not only the momentum of particles expelled along the line of travel but also the efficiency, which is affected by how much of the mass goes in the wrong direction, and how much of reaction mass gets converted to radiation rather than massive particles. But for a given specific impulse there need not be any upper limit on the change in velocity as long as you are willing to make the ratio of fuel mass to payload mass sufficiently huge, right? Are there other issues Dyson might have been considering here?
