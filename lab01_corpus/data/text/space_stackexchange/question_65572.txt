GCRS and ICRS Frame in Optical Observations from near earth
Lets say I am trying to observe the night sky. My telescope has a pointing direction. I want to compare the RADecs of each star with another. Now I have 4 cases.
My telescope is on a satellite orbiting earth and I am trying to observe the sky. I define the pointing direction in ICRF. And compute the RADecs of the stars based on pointing direction RADecs in ICRF.
My telescope is on a satellite orbiting earth and I am trying to observe the sky. I define the pointing direction in GCRF. And compute the RADecs of the stars based on pointing direction RADecs in GCRF.
My telescope is on ground and I am trying to observe the sky. I define the pointing direction in ICRF. And compute the RADecs of the stars based on pointing direction RADecs in ICRF.
My telescope is on ground and I am trying to observe the sky. I define the pointing direction in GCRF. And compute the RADecs of the stars based on pointing direction RADecs in GCRF.
As I understand, in both scenarios (telescope on ground or orbit), I should get more accurate results when pointing is defined in GCRF frame as it takes care of the aberrations (added due to relativity theory).
However, in a few examples I see observations for earth orbiting objects in RADecs in just ICRF. Which seems to defeat the purpose of having GCRF.
If anyone has some clarity on the topic, please help me understand this. Thankyou.
