How to define sensor timestamping precision requirements
Consistently timestamped sensor measurements are important for Kalman filtering and for reconstructing events retrospectively. Unfortunately, sometimes people who aren't navigators design these systems, and there is limited or no synchronization between the oscillators for the different onboard sensors. Suppose you need to explain to these people the upper bound on error in timestamping of measurements between two or more sensors.
As background, I know of this happening at at least four different new space companies. Traditional aerospace organizations usually seem to understand the need to discipline to a single master oscillator (except maybe Boeing), but I've heard of a number of groups working on small launch vehicles that don't have any way of time-correlating, say, the accelerometer integration period with the GPS measurements.
One way to look at the requirement is "What is the sensor error resulting from timestamping error $\delta t$ on a measurement taken at wall time $t$?" where $\delta t = \tilde{t}_A(t) - t$ is the instantaneous difference between the internal clock on sensor A and a hypothetical IMU reference oscillator. So if I have a velocimeter taking a measurement $\tilde{v}$, it will also have an error $\delta v = a \delta t$ relative to the IMU measurement at time $t$.
I know that timing errors in acceleration and velocity measurements in Kalman filtering can translate into large position state errors. Where I'm less clear about it is in attitude filtering. While the theory of star tracker functionality is pretty straightforward, there are large differences in implementation that seem to make the attitude measurement error due to timing error a bit less clear. Moreover, the attitude error is directly related to body rate error, which differs by an exponent from the relationship between position error and acceleration error, so it seems like attitude-only systems should be less sensitive to timing error.
Is it possible to write requirements around timing from first principles? Alternatively, are there loose guidelines for timing requirements in different types of systems? Finally, are there other standards aside from PPS for disciplining onboard oscillators?
