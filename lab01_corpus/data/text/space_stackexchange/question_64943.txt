sending a satellite in the right direction
I suspect that the required accuracy of satellite velocity direction is very high. This is especially the case for low earth orbits.
The radius of the earth is 6400 km. The altitude of a low earth orbit satellite is about 400 km, which is much smaller than the radius of the earth. That is, the satellite orbit is actually a very tight one around earth, with the gap very small. Suppose we intend to put the satellite in a circular orbit, but the velocity of the satellite at release is not strictly in the tangent direction. This will result in a tilted ellipse orbit, which might touch the atmosphere at the other side of the earth.
I did not do the calculation, but it seems to me that we have to make sure the velocity of the satellite is in a very small angle.
Is the reasoning right? Does it pose any difficulty in realistic satellite launching?
