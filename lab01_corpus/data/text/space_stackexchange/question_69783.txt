How effective would Juno be at investigating 3i/Atlas?
3i/Atlas is a recently (as of August 2025) discovered object with a trajectory suggesting it originated outside the solar system that will be at closest to Earth's orbit when Earth is on far side of the sun hindering ground based observation.
If the space probe Juno current investigating Jupiter fully expended remaining fuel it would be possibly to briefly get within 20-50 million kilometers of 3i/Atlas, assuming generous performance from a somewhat aged engine and that the tank issues that caused the fuel to not be used for intended Juno mission do not kill the engine or fully detonate the space craft.
Juno was designed to investigate Jupiter and the radiation/magnetic field, and has sensors optimized for that with a camera for engineering purposes rather than science.
Assuming physics, politics and engine engineering align to get a brief pass within 20 million kilometers of an interstellar object such as 3i/Atlas, just how effective would the fitted sensors on Juno be at doing science given their intended mission doing long duration investigation of a gas giant? In particular can the fitted camera get more than a single pixel of an object single digit kilometers across at the quite substantial distances involved?
