How to calculate the optimal altitude for an orbital propellant depot
In the current Artemis program, the Starship Human Landing System (HLS) plan calls for the HLS Starship vehicle to be refilled in LEO from an orbiting propellant depot.
If the depot orbits lower, it will consume more propellant to reboost its orbit. If it is higher, then tanker Starships won't be able to deliver as much propellant to it on each run.
I'd like to create a tool where the user inputs their assumptions, and the tool returns the ideal altitude for the propellant depot and the number of refilling flights that will be needed at that altitude.
Reasonable assumptions/inputs might include:
The depot will need solar panels to power the refrigeration systems that mitigate boil-off, which will increase the depot's aerodynamic drag.
The amount of propellant that HLS needs to depart for the moon.
The amount of propellant that a tanker Starship can deliver to a 200km orbit.
The cross-sectional area of the propellant depot (including solar panels)
Propellant used to initiate each re-boost (perhaps through ullage thrusters needed to settle the propellant in the tanks)
That the depot needs to stay in orbit indefinitely, but an HLS Starship will depart for the moon perhaps once a year.
Inside this tool, I'll need to implement a back-of-the-envelope-style optimization methodology. I'll also need to document the limits of this methodology and its underlying formulas.
(Note: In case you would find sample numbers to be helpful, let's assume that HLS needs 1500 tons of propellant to depart for the moon, each tanker flight can deliver 40 tons to a 200-km LEO (but less to higher orbits), The cross-sectional area of the depot (with panels) is roughly the same as the ISS - 2,481 m^2 (ref), and the propellant overhead for initiating each re-boost burn is 100kg (this is just a SWAG).)
If additional assumptions are required, feel free to make them. Also, feel free to challenge my default assumptions. Keep in mind that this question is about the correct calculation methodology - not the actual result.
