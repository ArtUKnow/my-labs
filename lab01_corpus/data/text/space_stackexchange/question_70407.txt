How is Reid Wiseman's Earth photo so well lit?
In this gorgeous photo from the Integrity, as shown on APOD, the sun is "moving behind Earth's bright limb along the lower right." But this means we are looking mostly at the night side of the earth, so I would expect to see a bright crescent with an otherwise dark disc perhaps with city lights. Instead it looks like the full earth with the sun behind the camera. What made this picture possible? Is it just that cameras are so good now that they can make a moonlit earth look like day without completely blowing out the pixels on the sunlit crescent? (Well, I guess they are pretty blown out.) I'm thinking that has to be the answer, but there must be more to say. Any details about the particular cameras they are using would be interesting, for instance.
