Does SpaceX Starship have significant methane emissions?
SpaceX Starship apparently uses liquid methane and liquid oxygen as its fuel. Liquid methane is not ideal for its environmental problems (burning it creates CO2 which can't be captured from a rocket, and we don't have a cost-effective way of sequestering the emitted carbon dioxide from the atmosphere to create new methane via Sabatier reaction).
However, the ratio of oxygen to methane is according to Wikipedia only 3.6 : 1 and not the stoichiometric ratio of 4 : 1:
Raptor operates with an oxygen-to-methane mixture ratio of about 3.6:1, lower than the stoichiometric mixture ratio of 4:1 necessary for complete combustion, since operating a higher temperatures would melt the engine
Does this mean that a significant amount of the methane is left unburned? Methane is a very potent greenhouse gas, far more than carbon dioxide.
Or is the methane in the exhaust so hot that it would combine with atmospheric oxygen anyway, not leaving any unburned methane to the atmosphere?
