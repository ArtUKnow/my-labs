Why can't Voyager 1 finally "settle down" to a stable attitude, without needing "40 short puffs per day"?
CNN's September 16, 2024 47-year-old Voyager 1 spacecraft just fired up thrusters it hasn’t used in decades gives a short review of changes from one set of thrusters to another for each of the Voyagers as background. This is due to very slow clogging of propellant lines apparently caused by unexpected "silicon dioxide, a byproduct of the fuel tank’s rubber diaphragm aging".
But I was surprised to read:
Now, the spacecraft is traveling on an unchanging path away from our solar system, so it just requires one set of thrusters to help keep its antenna pointed at Earth. To fuel the thrusters, liquid hydrazine is converted into gas and released in about 40 short puffs per day to keep Voyager 1 oriented correctly.
It's hard to imagine torques at 24 billion kilometers from the Sun in interstellar space so significant that an active control system would be needed to constantly work to compensate them.
And if there's a diaphragm, I wouldn't expect unpredictable "sloshing" of propellant.
To maintain communication with Earth, Voyager 1 simply needs to maintain its dish pointed at the Sun, since at this distance, the Earth never deviates by more than about 0.4 degrees from the Sun. And since Voyager 1 is so far away and traveling in a direction only 4 degrees away from its vector from the Sun, the direction of the Sun from Voyager will be changing very slowly now.
Question: Why can't Voyager 1 finally "settle down" to a stable attitude, without needing "40 short puffs per day"?
