Best practices for applying second surface mirrors (OSRs) on satellite radiators – tape vs foil?
I’m a student currently working on my thesis about the application of second surface mirrors (optical solar reflectors, OSRs) in spacecraft thermal control systems, particularly for high-power GEO satellites. I’ve been diving deep into the thermal architecture of platforms like the Boeing 702, which use deployable radiators similar to those discussed in this great thread about Inmarsat-5’s shiny panels
.
From what I’ve gathered, those large reflective panels are deployable radiators covered with second surface mirrors — typically quartz or FEP with a reflective rear coating (Ag or Al). The mirrors provide low solar absorptivity and high infrared emissivity, allowing the panels to efficiently radiate heat to deep space while minimizing solar input.
In my own research, I’ve been comparing two main implementation options for OSRs:
ITO-coated silver FEP tape,
Silver FEP foil (non-adhesive, bonded separately).
The tape version seems about three times more expensive, but it also looks much easier to apply, especially for smaller or irregular radiator geometries and for lab-scale setups. However, I’m concerned about potential thermal contact resistance, adhesive outgassing, and long-term degradation (both optical and mechanical).
I’d really appreciate any advice or shared experience on the following:
In real spacecraft applications, when is tape preferred over foil (or vice versa)?
How critical is the bonding method for long-term performance in GEO conditions (UV, atomic oxygen, micrometeoroid environment)?
Are there best practices or recommended adhesives / surface preparation methods to ensure proper contact and avoid delamination?
Has anyone characterized how the ITO layer affects both optical properties and electrical grounding over mission lifetime?
If you’ve worked with OSR integration or thermal hardware testing, I’d love to hear your insights — even small practical tips would be very helpful.
Thanks in advance!
