When could we determine mass and final trajectory of an incoming alien “Project Orion” spacecraft during its braking phase?
NASA’s Project Orion (nuclear pulse propulsion rocket) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Orion proposed high thrust/high efficiency propulsion (ISP up to 6000 sec), which could potentially enable interstellar probes.
The pulse drive involves detonating nuclear explosions behind the spacecraft which transfer momentum through a pusher plate at the rear of the craft. For uncrewed probes, acceleration of 100g was proposed, with a detonation about once a second. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20000096503/downloads/20000096503.pdf
If the mission included an orbital insertion around the destination star (as opposed to a fly-by), the spacecraft would be flipped and the propulsion unit used for braking. The duration of braking would be almost as long as the duration of the acceleration phase. An observer in the destination star system could observe the pulsed nuclear explosions of the incoming craft.
If an alien craft as described in the above NASA document were to arrive in our solar system, which existing instrument is most likely to first detect it? At what distance and with which instrument could Earth-based observers be able to determine the destination orbit and mass of the visitor?
You can assume the aliens are using a fuel-efficient strategy to establish a closed orbit in our solar system. This allows us to assume the craft committed to its target orbital plane before entering the solar system. Plane changes are notoriously expensive in terms of delta-v and this expense increases as the craft approaches perihelion.
Likewise, we can assume the craft has no plan for a gravity assist maneuver at perihelion or it would have maintained its hyperbolic velocity.
Because it would lose the advantage of the Oberth effect, the craft is unlikely to "turn off the brakes" before perihelion and, in fact, is likely to have a finite duration burn through perihelion and a fair way to its aphelion in a high eccentricity orbit..
