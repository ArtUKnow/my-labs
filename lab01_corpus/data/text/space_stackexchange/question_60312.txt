How far could we see (horizontally), floating inside the acid clouds of Venus at 56 km and at 51 km height above the surface?
Screenshot of Fig. 4 in the article The microphysics of the clouds of Venus: Results of the Pioneer Venus Particle Size Spectrometer Experiment
From the Wikipedia section about the troposphere of Venus:
As manned ships sent to Venus would be able to compensate for differences in temperature to a certain extent, anywhere from about 50 to 54 km or so above the surface would be the easiest altitude in which to base an exploration or colony,...
That section shows a height/temperature/atmospheric pressure table with at 50 km height a temperature of 75⁰ C and a pressure of 1.066 atm., and at 55 km height, 27⁰ C and 0.531 atm. respectively.
That makes me wonder what would be easier manageable for a manned ship, 75⁰ C at the right pressure or 0,5 atm. at the right temperature ?
Moreover, as can be seen on the image above, from 51 to 55 km height the mass loading of the sulfuric acid droplets would be about 11 mg/m³, but at about 55 km there's a sharp decline to 3-4 mg/m³.
For comparison, the permissible exposure limit on the basis of a 8 h/day, 40h/week work schedule is 1 mg/m³ for sulfuric acid.
Edit: In the meantime I've learned that to determine the visibility it's more appropriate to use the graph of the extinction coefficient from the image above.
So what would be the horizontal visibility at 56 km with a 1 km⁻¹ extinction coefficient and at 51 km with a 3-4 km⁻¹ extinction coefficient measured inside the clouds ?
Is it possible to calculate the visibility with the formulae presented in this article and then "translate" it to the atmospheric conditions on Venus ?
