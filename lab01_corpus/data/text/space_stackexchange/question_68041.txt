Why are lunar landers having so much difficulty when Surveyor apparently did not?
The Surveyor program of the 1960s successfully landed several probes on the Moon. Although there were a few failures, it seems every vehicle which arrived at the touchdown phase completed a successful landing. On the other hand, it seems that of the recent lunar landing attempts, most that approach the touchdown phase get into trouble at that point. There was one which failed to correctly process altitude as it passed over the rim of a crater, leading to early shutdown of the descent engine and resultant hard landing. Two have fallen over on touchdown, severely limiting if not terminating their missions.
What accounts for these difficulties now that didn't seem to factor into the 1960s missions? Is it because of more challenging conditions at the landing site (were Surveyor sites chosen specifically to be of least risk)? Is it because of a trend in lander design (center of gravity)?
One would think that modern technology would enable far more reliable landings in far more challenging conditions than would have been possible/achievable in the 1960s.
Let's exclude Apollo from the comparison because of the human in the loop and consider only un-crewed landers.
