Will the Europa Clipper spacecraft be visible in 2026 when it flies by Earth again?
The Europa Clipper flight plan includes two gravity assists to get to Jupiter (which I can confirm is monumentally amazing that they managed to time it right) - one flyby of Mars in 2025 and then another flyby of Earth in 2026. Then in just four short years after that it will reach the Jovian system and start doing its job.
Being immensely interested in this particular spacecraft, and wanting to plan ahead a potentially very-fun night for some friends: will the spacecraft be visible from the northern hemisphere at night as it flies by Earth? Given that it'd be moving at escape velocity it'd be going a fair bit faster than the ISS and it would be very fun to sit out all night and wait for it to arrive (since they can pin down the perigee to the minute/second) and watch it fly by.
The primary details are
if it will be passing by the night side of the Earth, since it would definitely not be visible in the day,
if it's bright enough to be seen from the surface - I would guess it would be near sunrise/sunset given that, although it's flying by higher than very-visible Starlink satellites, it also is much larger,
which hemisphere it will be passing over - I myself live in the northern hemisphere and seeing it if it passed over the southern hemisphere instead would be quite difficult.
Is there any available information on this? NASA's mission timeline did not have any.
