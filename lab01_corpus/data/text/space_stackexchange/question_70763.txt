Space Elevator and Potential Energy
My idea of a space elevator is cables attached to the ground held taught by a counterweight above geosynchronous orbit. This would greatly decrease the cost of sending materials or ships past the orbit because some energy could be used to mechanically climb the cables (using electricity from the ground I assume running somehow along the cables) thus avoiding the losses from rockets having to carry their own fuel and accelerate that as well as the payload.
If we imagined a perfectly balanced system that was not attached to the earth with the cable was just dangling with the bottom at ground level, climbing the cable would pull on the cable and drag it down, hence we need the counterweight which puts tension on the cable from above geosynchronous orbit.
But the counterweight doesn't come for free, and doesn't remain static. Initially it must be boosted past geosynchronous orbit. When something ascends to geosynchronous orbit using the space elevator, doesn't that decrease the tension on the cable? If we send enough material up the elevator, won't that eventually lower the tension at ground level to zero and won't sending further material begin pulling the system down?
