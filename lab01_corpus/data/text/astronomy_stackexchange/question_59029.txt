Will there be any utility in stationing a space telescope a billion miles away to get very accurate parallax measurements?
I'm thinking a nuclear powered astrometry telescope in a Jupiter-distance orbit. Every five years, you could look at the same object with a parallax baseline of a billion miles. Push it out to Saturn for a baseline of 2 billion miles. That's 10X better than what we have now.
An astronautry satellite wouldn't have to be large; it only has to be large enough to see the furthest stars that show parallax.
Would establishing extremely accurate distance measurements for close stars, and the first parallax measurements for distant stars be particularly useful, Or are our numbers close enough?
Would it be worth the cost?
