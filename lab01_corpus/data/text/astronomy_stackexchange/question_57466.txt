Is It Possible to See the Sun's Corona Appearing to Flare Out From Behind the Moon During a Total Solar Eclipse?
I am not an astronomer, but witnessed the last two US total solar eclipses -- the first from a farm in Hopkinsville, Kentucky on August 21, 2017, and the second last Monday from Klyde Warren Park in Dallas, Texas. Following the first one, I posted the following message on Facebook: "Resolution of my smartphone is woefully inadequate to capture the view our group had of the moon fully eclipsing the sun for 2 minutes and 40 seconds, with the sun's corona flaring out from behind the moon. Truly awesome!"
I did not witness anything similar last Monday, and am now wondering if what I saw in Hopkinsville was an optical illusion. To be more explicit, what I saw last Monday was the sun's corona appearing to encircle the moon with a steady white light. On August 21, 2017 in Hopkinsville, what I (thought I) saw was the corona appearing to protrude in at least one direction from that circle of white light, as if there was a large flare or two.
Can anyone assist in explaining what I saw on August 21, 2017? Thanks.
