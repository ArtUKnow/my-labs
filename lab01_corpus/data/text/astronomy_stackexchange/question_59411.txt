Do bigger telescopes see more objects primarily because they collect more light, or because they resolve things better?
(Cross-posting from the Physics.SE because in retrospect, this should be the better SE to ask on)
I encountered this question in an exam recently (which I have simplified to focus on the core question). After describing two modern astronomy telescopes, one big and one small, the question asks why the first telescope is expected to detect more objects than the second one.
My first thought is that for obvious reasons big telescopes collect more light, hence they can detect fainter things, hence they detect more objects.
However, the answer key gives a different solution. It uses the Rayleigh criterion to say that the bigger telescope can resolve more things, hence it should detect more objects.
I'm wondering which effect dominates. Both effects clearly lead in the same direction, but in practice, which effect is the primary reason why bigger telescopes detect more objects? I'm especially interested in the ratio of newly-detected dim objects against the already-discovered-but-newly-resolved objects.
NB: The question is about radio telescopes detecting pulsars. I imagine some extra considerations here are that 1) pulsars can occur in binaries, making the latter effect more important; and also 2) radio telescopes work by interferometry, so you don't need a single big telescope, you can make do with lots of small telescopes separated by a large distance. However, I suspect both of these effects are well beyond the level of the exam, and it would not be appropriate to expect a student to know them.
