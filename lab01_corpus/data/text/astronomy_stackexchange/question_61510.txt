Why don’t we observe the stellar wobbling of the Sun?
If one calculates the location of the center of mass of the Sun-Jupiter system, they obtain that the CM is about $1.06\ R_\odot$ away from the center of the Sun, so it lies above the solar surface, and the system revolves around this point with a period of roughly $12\ \mathrm{yr}$. Yet on Earth, we do not observe the Sun wobble around an external point on the sky, even though the amplitude of the oscillation is comparable to the angular size of the Sun. As the mass of the Earth is negligible compared to the star and the giant planet, we are roughly orbiting the CM of the Sun-Jupiter system, so consequently, we should observe this stellar wobbling with the synodic orbital period of Jupiter. What is the resolution to this apparent paradox?
