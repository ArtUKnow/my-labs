what is the significance of mean radius as planetary datum for elevations?
From what I understand, a planet's datum, or reference surface, is an approximation of its shape - typically either a geometric model (eg a sphere or ellipsoid) or one based on its gravitational potential (the geoid). The mean planetary radius seems to be commonly used as the datum. What exactly makes this choice intuitive or suitable as a baseline for measuring elevations? Is it because the mean radius represents the planet's surface before topographic deviations from geological processes such as plate tectonics or impacts? So a negative elevation is the outcome of phenomena that have 'dug into' the 'baseline surface', and a positive elevation from those that have 'raised' it? For instance, on Earth, tectonic activity creates mountain ranges and ocean basins, resulting in elevations both above and below the mean radius. On the Moon, large topographic variations from the mean radius have been caused by large impactors, creating deep basins below the mean radius and raised regions from impact-related uplift and ejecta. Is this line of reasoning correct? Is there more to it than that?
