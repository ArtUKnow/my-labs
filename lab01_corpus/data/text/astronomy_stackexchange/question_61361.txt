Identification and discovery of interstellar objects
Considering the recent announcement by NASA of the discovery of a new interstellar object, 3I/ATLAS, I would like to ask a few questions about the process
that leads to the identification of an object as being truly interstellar in origin. As far as I understand, it is possible in principle for a solar system object to have its orbit perturbed to become hyperbolic with a velocity at infinity that would make it unbound to the Sun.
Is this only possible if the object has a close enconunter with a planet, or other sufficiently massive body, at some point? Why can't a hyperbolic orbit be produced by impacts and deviations in the outer solar system, for example?
If I understand correctly, according to Do et al 2018, the possibility that 'Oumuamua could have originated in the solar system was ruled out when
it was verified that its reconstructed orbit had never led it close to a giant planet. Quoting from the introduction:
Its incident velocity is too high to have originated as a cometary object deflected by close passage to a giant planet (de la Fuente Marcos & de la Fuente Marcos 2017), indicating that ‘Oumuamua is truly an interstellar object.
Does this mean that the only mechanism that could impart the required velocity to an object of comparable size is deflection by a giant planet?
Also, according to the estimates found in the same reference for the number density of interstellar objects within the solar system, there should be "several of these objects in the inner solar
system at any given time". This refers only to 'Oumuamua-like objects, if I'm not mistaken, so the overall population density should be even higher when considering other types of ISOs.
If that's the case, why has the discovery rate been so low so far (3 objects over almost a decade)? The quoted article refers only to Pan-STARRS as a "detector", even assuming that its individual discovery rate could be comparatively low with respect to the expected total number of objects, it should still be much higher. Moreover, when considering all active survey telescopes, such as indeed ATLAS and NEOWISE, the overall discovery rate should be even higher, certainly not just 3 objects over a decade.
