Could other theories account for the presence of Cosmic Microwave Background (other than a Big Bang)?
Some scientists say CMB is "proof" of the Big Bang event, as no other source accounts for its presence (that I know of). However, I can think of a few things that seem to do so:
Dark matter is considered to account for around 70% of the overall mass of the universe. No one is fully clear on what it is, but we can assume that if stars are formed from nebulae, and nebulae are only visible with stars to illuminate them, it's possible that unaccounted for mass is simply from those nebulae that haven't yet condensed into new stars. However, that doesn't mean there wouldn't be activity to note from this, and 2 ideas therefore come to mind:
Nebulae material, like a lot of conventional material, might simply absorb higher frequencies of light, and what we observe from the blackness of their occlusion is lower frequency EMR, AKA microwaves and radiowaves.
Nebulae matter that is in the process of condensing into stars may be emitting friction heat, which would spike on the infrared and microwave frequencies. These pre-stars may not be hot enough to see distinctly in the blackness (especially if refracted within their own, dense nebulae), but there might be enough of them to amount to an effect we could consider "background radiation", as well.
On a tangential note, dark matter might occlude light from reaching us beyond a certain range, limiting how much effect our telescopes can get. We might not be seeing farther than we are only because of this, and not because of there being "no existence" beyond the furthest reaches visible light could even have reached to reveal anything (if any argument exists to this effect, that is).
Do any of these sound like they might account for CMB?
