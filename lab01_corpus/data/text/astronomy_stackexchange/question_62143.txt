Can someone please clarify why the angle KPX is (90+alpha)?
Question:
In the standard geometric construction of ecliptic coordinates on the celestial sphere, let
K be the north ecliptic pole,
P the north celestial pole,
X is the position of an object on the celestial sphere,
α the object’s right ascension,
and the zero point defined by the vernal equinox.
When forming the spherical triangle
KPX, many derivations state, without much explanation, that the angle at
P satisfies ∠KPX = 90∘+α.
I am having trouble understanding geometrically why this angle is so.
