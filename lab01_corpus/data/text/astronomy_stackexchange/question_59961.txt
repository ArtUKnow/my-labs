Naive deduction of Hubble's law from Cosmicflows-4 dataset
Mathematician here, with very poor background in astronomy and astrophysics.
I'm teaching a statistics course this semester, and I try to supply my students with real datasets and real-world examples of everything we do. When discussing linear regression, I immediately turned to some examples from astronomy, where a linear regression historically led to major discoveries. H. S. Leavitt's result on the brightness and period of some Cepheids is one such example, and I was under the impression that Hubble's law was another. So I tried to look into some suitable datasets.
First I looked into the Nearby Galaxy Catalog (link, another). The data there does indeed show the linear trend, see the image below (the data points and the regression line).
However, the slope of this line (which should, given enough data, approximate the Hubble's constant) is 55.8, with an STD of 1.9 (under the standard regression assumptions). This is rather far from the modern estimates (although is coincides with an estimate "from early 1970s", as Wikipedia put it). My guess is that since the dataset only contains the data on nearby galaxies, the resulting slope is affected too much by the peculiar velocities. Thus a larger dataset is needed.

Here the distances are not written explicitly in Mpc, but rather as distance modulus. There are several distinct estimates, but one seem to combine most of them (the column titled "DM" in the dataset).
