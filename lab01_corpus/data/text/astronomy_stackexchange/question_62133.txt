How do I select the correct frame for computing stellar aberration?
I'm trying to understand how stellar aberration correction is computed and am finding seemingly contradictory explanations across different software packages and references.
SPICE:
The documentation describes aberration as a "raindrop effect" where the photon velocity relative to the observer is "the photons' velocity relative to the solar system barycenter minus the velocity of the observer relative to the solar system barycenter." It explicitly states that:
stellar aberration is independent of the velocity of the target.
Their implementation computes:
Let r be the light time corrected vector from the observer
to the object, and v be the velocity of the observer with
respect to the solar system barycenter.
sin(phi) = v sin(w) / c
where w is the angle between r and v.
This appears to be true regardless of what reference frame you're computing in. So whether you're computing the apparent position of an asteroid in an EARTH centered J2000 frame, or a JUPITER centered J2000 frame, the velocity of the observer relative to SSB is used.
GIANT (NASA) Implementation:
Source Code
Written out, given $\vec{r}$ as the inertial vector from the observer to the target, $\vec{v}$ as the inertial velocity of the observer:
$$
\vec{a} = \frac{\vec{r} \times \vec{v}}{|\vec{v}|} \ \ \ \ \text{aberration axis}
$$
$$
\sin(\theta) = \frac{|\vec{a}|}{|\vec{r}|} \ \ \ \ \ \text{velocity sin angle}
$$
$$
\phi = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{|\vec{v}|\sin\theta}{c}\right) \ \ \ \text{aberration angle}
$$
$$
\vec{r}_a = R(\hat{a}, \phi) \ \cdot \ \vec{r} \ \ \ \text{aberrated vector}
$$

Implements stellar aberration given the line of sights in frame a and velocity of b with respect to a, computes line of sights in frame b
My confusion:
If I observe an object at rest relative to me, I would expect no aberration — our relative velocity is zero. Yet SPICE's formulation, taken at face value, seems to suggest that computing aberrations is and should be done relative to the solar system barycenter regardless of what frame you're computing in and what target you're looking at. This feels like it implies that I could measure the velocity of the earth by pointing a camera at my wall, marking some points, and measuring how they aberrate throughout the year. This, obviously, is ridiculous. And so I must be missing something.
The GIANT and SONIC implementations seem to only use "inertial" as the assumption about the frame, not necessarily SSB. Yet this raises even more questions to me... how do I pick a frame? They do not seem to be equal. Say, for the OSIRIS-REx mission in orbit around Bennu, I tried to compute the velocity (stellar) aberration of Jupiter, but I took the Bennu centered frame as the frame to compute relative to, I'd get (within a rounding error) 0 aberration since the spacecraft is moving at mere cm/s relative to Bennu. Yet if I just changed frames to a SSB centered frame, it's now moving at many km/s and the aberration would be meaningful.
I have heard it said that aberration is about how direction within a frame change due to motion in that frame, yet, going back to my OSIRIS-REx example, what if I simply aligned my Bennu centered frame with the ICRF, just picking a different origin for example?
Perhaps for a more concrete example, say I have 3 things:
A spacecraft with a known position, velocity, and attitude
A single point with a known position and velocity
A collection of star directions in ICRF at the observation time
How do I compute the velocity (stellar) aberration of the stars, and the point object? For the stars, computing the camera's velocity relative to SSB makes intuitive sense since the star catalogue would be built relative to our solar system, so our motion relative to our solar system seems the most reasonable choice. But what about for the point object? I could imagine selecting any number of frames where I could apply the formulas above, but it isn't clear to me how that'd work?
This drives me to feel like SPICE's decision for always computing relative to SSB is somehow deeply motivated, yet this again raises my concerns of: "that would imply aberration would occur between me and the wall of my room".
Any help understanding this would be greatly appreciated.
