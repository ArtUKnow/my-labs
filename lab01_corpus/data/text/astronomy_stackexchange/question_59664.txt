What would happen if a central massive object were removed from the centre of a galaxy?
For example, assuming a more or less standard galaxy, similar to our own in characteristics of a few hundred billion stars and a supermassive black hole at the centre; what would happen if the black hole just disappeared?
It seems that most galaxies have a central massive object, which is either a black hole or a very dense cluster of stars. There is some debate as to whether the galaxies with a cluster have an as yet unobserved black hole inside the cluster or not.
Based on my admittedly very limited understanding of orbital mechanics in a planetary system, removal of the central star would cause massive disruption of the orbits of the planets; they would essentially just enter Newtonian straight-line motion as no gravitational force is pulling them into rotation around the central object. However, this is because the star is much much more massive than the rest of the system. For our solar system, the sun makes up ~2x1030 kg and the rest is about 2.7x1027 kg, so the sun is about 99.9% of system mass.
However, in the case of a galaxy the central massive object is only a very tiny fraction of the total galactic mass. In the case of the Milky Way, the Sagittarius A (Sag A) black hole is about 4.3 million solar masses1, against the total mass of the galaxy of 1.28x1012 solar masses2, so Sag A makes up 0.0003% of the galactic mass. Even if both those numbers are out by an order of magnitude in the right directions, (i.e Sag A 10x larger and the galaxy 10x smaller), Sag A would still only be a tiny fraction of the total mass.
So. Magical being comes and just disappears the black hole; one instant it is there, the next it's gone.
The effects would propagate out at light speed. Central stars directly orbiting the black hole would enter motion affected by other nearby massive objects, which presumably would be other stars and be pretty chaotic. However, these masses would presumably be gravitationally bound to each-other in some manner and would eventually form some sort of barycentral (?) cluster?
Further out presumably at least until the light-speed gravitation wave hits them, stars will continue rotation more or less as normal about the centre of mass of the galaxy, so there might not be any disruption?
Is my understanding correct?
