Warped rings of extrasolar planets or "warm Saturns"
I read a little bit about extrasolar gas giants and planets with rings, and stumbled upon the paper "Warm Saturns: On The Nature Of Rings Around Extrasolar Planets That Reside Inside The Ice Line" by Schlichting and Chang in which they state that extrasolar planets with semi-major axes of <1 AU that fall inside the ice line may have rings made of rocks. They say that these rings would be warped "such that their rings will lie in the planet's equatorial plane inside of (the laplace radius) $R_L$, but coincide with the orbital plane outside of $R_L$" (p.5). I've tried to find pictures that show what 'warped rings' could look like but searching for 'planet with warped rings' gives me different results (I think?), namely planet-forming discs, not rings around an existing planet.
What would these warped rings likely look like? Pictures of planets in the solar system with rings usually show the rings as even and flat, aligned with the centre of the planet (unless changed by seasons). Would warped rings be 'misaligned'? Or would different rings have different angles?
