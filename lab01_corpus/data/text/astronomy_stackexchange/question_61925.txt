How would one take a simulated Gaussian function absorption line and match it to a given spectrograph resolution?
I have a gaussian function to simulate an absorption line, of arbitrary spacing in wavelength (e.g., 1000 - 2000 Angstrom, with Δλ = 0.01 Angstrom).
I would like to 'simulate' what a given spectrograph would see, e.g., HST COS/JWST NIRSpec. Since my wavelength range is in rest frame, the current procedure I am thinking of is first shifting to 'observed' frame (picking a random redshift), and then convolving with a Gaussian function to match the instrumental dispersion (σ_instrumental). I would then rebin the spectra to match the spectrograph resolution (Δλ), and deredshift again to rest frame.
Should I be convolving the Gaussian in velocity space?
Also, different spectrographs quote different combinations of dispersions/resolutions/pixel sizes/central wavelengths etc, and it is not always clear that R = λ/Δλ, σ_instrumental = c/2.355*R = (Δλ/λ)(c/2.355).
Is there something in this simple approximation I am missing?
EDIT
Figure 1 in this paper: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.00231v1 shows a spectrum degraded to lower resolution but before and after instrumental broadening.
Some papers convolve to match the resolution, and some to match the instrumental broadening, and then rebin to match the Δλ of R = λ/Δλ. I am confused as to whether I should convolve to match the dispersion, and then rebin to match Δλ (after converting Δλ from Angstrom to km/s) before or after redshifting back to rest frame.
