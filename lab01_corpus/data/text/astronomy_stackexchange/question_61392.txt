If the mass of black holes were only distributed around the event horizon, would all infalling objects be deflected into inspiralling collision paths
Some black hole properties which might be expected to be located at the singularity get transfered to the event horizon. Entropy is one such, time dilation is another. A true singularity will have infinite density and therefore infinite gravitational time dilation. Time should stop at the singularity, but instead it stops at the event horizon. It seems at least plausible that mass could be another such property. Hence the question.
All collisions involving black holes that have been detected to date from their resulting gravity waves, are inspiralling, tangential collisions. It is possible though that this may be because direct, radial, plunges, do not produce much in the way of a gravity wave signature. So a supplemantary question is 'have radial collisions with a black hole ever been observed'?
