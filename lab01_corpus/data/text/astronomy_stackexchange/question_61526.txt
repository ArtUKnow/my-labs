Are some meteors pieces of satellites?
According to this paper: spatial flux of meteorite falls a yearly estimate for masses $>50\,\rm g$ hitting Earth would be around 17,000. To my mind that does not seem like a lot, so I would not expect to see an event, and indeed I have not. However, twice in as many months in almost exactly the same spot I have seen (and been quite scared by) bright objects coming straight down but burning up at the last moment before impact. It was the speed and sudden appearance which affected me most, because my human nervous system felt very slow in response.
I would estimate the two events occurring only 50 metres apart spatially, and that the trajectory ended 60-100 metres from the ground. While I feel more sure that the objects came down (on different nights) at a very close distance to each other, I can't help but feel that my estimate of the height is some kind of optical illusion, even though my nervous system responded and felt like these were very near misses. So, I want to ask:
Taking into account that these objects appeared very differently in the sky to shooting stars: they were brighter, whiter, and came straight down, and that there are now so many artificial satellites visibly patrolling the globe, some in chains (I counted more than 20 in one chain) could it be that these falling objects weren't extra terrestrial but pieces of satellites finding their way back to the ground somehow, deflected from orbit by collisions? Also, would they really burn up so close to the ground and what is the likelihood of a person being struck? Two within the space of a few weeks seemed a bit close for comfort.
