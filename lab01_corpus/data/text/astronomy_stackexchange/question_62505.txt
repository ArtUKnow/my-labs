Can the gravitational wave background be studied with just one millisecond pulsar?
Pulsar timing arrays (PTA) use large sets of millisecond pulsars (MSPs) to search for low-frequency gravitational waves. Currently, the main goal of PTAs is the detection and characterization of a stochastic gravitational wave background (GWB) formed by supermassive black hole binaries, which should manifest as a common red noise process. The process is expected to cause correlations between the timing residuals of pairs of pulsars based on their angular separation in the sky, as described by the Hellings and Downs curve.
To find this spatial correlation, you need a lot of pulsar pairs, and therefore a lot of pulsars. Current PTA data sets have many tens of pulsars, with the MeerKAT Pulsar Timing Array's 4.5-year data release, for example, clocking in with 83 (Miles et al. 2025). This requires a lot of observing time, which is not easy to get.
Obviously, you can't detect spatial correlations with only one pulsar, and an array of only a few pulsars won't do a whole lot more. However, are there any ways to search for, or extract any kind of information at all about, the stochastic GWB from a single pulsar? In other words, can any nanohertz-band gravitational wave science be done with observations of just one MSP?
