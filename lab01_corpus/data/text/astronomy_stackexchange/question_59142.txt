Two-element quasi-monochromatic multiplying interferometer- how does it work?
I am studying the notes provided by NRAO (Essential Radio Astronomy).
https://www.cv.nrao.edu/~sransom/web/Ch3.html#S1.SS5
I do not understand how the two-element quasi-monochromatic interferometer works. Consider a point source observed by the two dishes on their axes, with an angle $\theta$ wrt the baseline $b$ connecting them. The geometric delay $\tau_g$ of the arrival of a plane wavefront to one antenna wrt the other is given by
$\tau_g = \frac{b \cos\theta}{c}$,
where $c$ is the speed of light. The output voltages (suppose the antennas are identical)
$V_1 = V \cos(\omega t)$,
$V_2 = V \cos[\omega (t - \tau_g)]$
are multiplied and time averaged yielding the response
$R = (V^2/2) \cos\phi$,
where $\phi = \omega \tau_g = (2 \pi b \cos\theta)/\lambda$.
Then, since the amplitude $V^2 \propto S_\nu (A_1 A_2)^{1/2}$, where $S_\nu$ is the flux density of the source and $A_{1,2}$ are the effective areas of the antennas, and since for the effective area we have (consider a 1D uniformly illuminated aperture of size $D$ for simplicity)
$A \propto \mathrm{sinc}^2(\frac{D}{\lambda} \cos\theta)$,
we finally have for the response:
$R \propto \mathrm{sinc}^2(\frac{D}{\lambda} \cos\theta) \times \cos(\frac{2 \pi b \cos\theta}{\lambda})$.
Now, I do not understand why we have to consider the variation with $\theta$ of the effective area of the antennas, since we always observe the source on the axes of the dishes. $\theta$ changes because the Earth rotates and so the geometric delay changes, but the dishes move to hold the source stable on their axes, so we should always have the same effective area, and not a variation with $\theta$.
