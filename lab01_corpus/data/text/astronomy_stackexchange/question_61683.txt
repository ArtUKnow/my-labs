Consequences of higher stellar density for the development and maintenance of life on planets
Assume that all astronomical objects retain their current sizes, but the distances between any two stars is only 1/10 of what they actually are, e.g. Sirius is not 8.8 l.y.s away but only 0.88 l.y. away, while Arcturus is not 36 l.y., but only 3.6 l.y. and Betelgeuse not 643 l.y. but merely 64.3 l.y. and even the amazingly bright Deneb not 1650 l.y. but only 165 l.y. Thus if one went outside at night every star would suddenly appear 5 mag.s brighter than they do now!, while the number of stars visible in any given 3D region of space would be roughly 1,000 times higher than it actually is. Now what I want to know is what difference would such a denser stellar neighborhood make, if any, on the probability of life originating, and more important, being able to sustain itself for the 3.5+ billion years that we know it actually has, -- and that despite at least 5 mass extinction events?
At first it seems that the stars are so very very far away from one another, even by the greatest distances relevant to the solar system, that reducing the distances between any two of them could hardly make any difference at all. But such a view is hardly obvious since the smaller distances could have untoward consequences, like, just to mention a few off the top of my head, disturbing the solar systems' planetary orbits, [especially in the meteorite belt], or unleashing enough dangerous materials from the Kuiper Belt and the Oort Cloud -- presumably 1,000 times as many! -- to rain down on Earth's unprotected flora and fauna to, at least over 3.5+ billion years, completely extinguish all life on Earth.
The answer to this question also has obvious consequences for where we should, and should not, be looking for alien life since we know that there are many galactic regions which, while being comprised mostly of main sequence stars like the ones around us do in fact have stellar densities on the order of 1,000 times our own. O.K. I've now got this little conundrum off my chest. But can any of y'all answer it for me?
