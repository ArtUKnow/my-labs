Why are strangelets considered as a threat?
Modern research papers still deem the existence of stable strange matter to be most likely possible in the core of neutron stars. The composition of the core of a neutron star is clear to me. The reason why the core of a neutron star is composed primarily of hyperons is because it is favourable for hyperons to exist in an energetically stable state when exposed to extreme density and pressure. After many up and down quarks gain a Fermi energy that allows the creation of strange quarks with the emission of a W-boson, then strangelets can appear which remain stable due to the conditions of the neutron star core.
However, I become confused when physicists say that these strangelets can be hazardous as they can transmute other matter in the universe to strange quarks. When the core of the neutron star becomes exposed (like during a kilonova with another neutron star) and some strangelets are released into the universe, how are they said to be "infectious" with normal matter? If the existence of stable strange matter is only proved when normal matter has an abundantly high Fermi energy, then the minimal pressure and density of the universe should make these strangelets decay almost immediately as the Fermi energy goes back to normal and up and down quarks have a desirable energy state to exist in. So if strangelets are only formed in the cores of neutron stars, how is it possible they can exist externally?
