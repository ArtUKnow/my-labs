How could Big bang happen everywhere if space is expanding
Reading different articles and watching science videos I realise that Big Bang happened everywhere. However, this 'everywhere' was a very small volume of space and that due to expansion has grown into current size. Also, the expansion happens because every point in space is expanding.
This brings me to my question that can we really say at this moment of time that Big Bang happened everywhere?
Even if we ignore what is happening at the Planck scale due to this intrinsic expansion, atleast we can say that more volume is being added with every moment of time. So how can we say that this newly added volume have a history that stretches back to the Big Bang? Because if the newly added volume's causal history can be traced back to Big Bang then it would imply that somehow at a given moment a definite volume of space somehow divides to create more volume. I say this because I don't see any other way to preserve a causal chain for the newly added volume.
