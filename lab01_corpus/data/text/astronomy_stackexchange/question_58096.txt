How to count observable number of galaxies at a given latitude considering interstellar extinction?
If galaxies are uniformly distributed in space, then in the absence of extinction, the number of galaxies per square degree brighter than magnitude m is given by $$\log_{N_0}(m) = 0.6m + C$$
How will the formula look like if extinction in the interstellar medium is counted? I know that a galaxy situated at galactic latitide b with apparent magnitude x (without extinction) will have the apparent magnitude due to extinction: $$m(b) = x + \frac{\Delta m}{\sin(b)}$$
where Delta m= extinction in direction of galactic pole.
It makes sense to me that substituting m(b) in the first expression will give me the observable number of galaxies at galactic latitude b. But in the textbook I am using (Fundamental Astronomy), a different expression is given-
$$\log N(m, b) = \log N_0 (m - \Delta m(b))$$
which makes no sense to me. Could anyone provide me with the correct formula?
