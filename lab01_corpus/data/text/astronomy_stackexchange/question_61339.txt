Why Proton Density and Radial Velocity Measured by Wind/3DP Looks So "Discrete"?
I was checking WI_PM_3DP dataset, and notice that the density and radial velocity data seems to exhibit some distinct "levels". Since the moments are derived from counts across all energy levels, why do they show this discrete behavior? Are there specific instrumental or data processing factors contributing to this effect?
Edit 20250701:
The ion moments are from the ion electrostatic analyzer of the 3DP instrument suite on board Wind spacecraft, which is now running at the L1 point of the Earth-Sun system.
Electrostatic analyzers screen particles with different energy per charge by applying electric fields with different strengths to the flying particles. If all detected particles are assuemd to be protons (with its mass per charge as 1), the velocity distribution function (VDF) could be derived from the raw data. We can then derive the particle number density, velocity vector, and temperature tensor from the VDF.
SWA EAS – Caveats to use of Science Data Products shows how moments are calculated. Loosely speaking, the integral is the number density, the weighted average is the velocity, and the variance is the temperature.
The WI_PM_3DP level-2 dataset is released by NASA and is accessible on CDAWeb. It contains moments of protons and alpha particles.
Edit 20250701-2:
Here are some examples of VDF. As you can see that the VDF is actually some kind of probability density function (PDF). The velocity is actually the expected value of this PDF. In other words, the velocity value is not directly measured, and that kind of "discrete" situation I mentioned above, should not occur, I guess.
Edit 20250709:
I found a footnote in the page 9 of Notes for the Wind 3DP Instrument that may be relevant to this question, but I'm not sure if this is the answer since I don't know how to process level zero data of Wind/3DP (though the PIs open-sourced some IDL programs that appears to do this job).
Note that in survey mode the data structures typically take 25 data points at 14 different energies while in burst mode they take 64 data points at 14 different energies.
So a possible answer is that, these data that show discrete behavior are so-called survey mode data, which are calculated from too few raw count rates and thus retain some of the discrete characteristics from the count rates.
This is the last edit, as I have decided not to use this dataset anymore, due to its unexpected and unexplainable behavior (for me). WI_PLSP_3DP with a much lower resolution (~24s) might be a more robust choice.
