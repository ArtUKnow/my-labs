Why is Jupiter's rotational angular momentum so much greater than its orbital momentum?

"Rotational angular momentum" distinguishes it from orbital angular momentum which, surprisingly(?), is 10,000 times less:
the orbital angular momentum of Jupiter is approximately 1.7 by 10 to the power of 33 KG square meter per second which is about sixty times greater than the total angular momentum of all the other planets in the solar system combined

jupiter rotates at a speed of 28,273 mph making it the fastest rotating planet in the solar system
But that's virtually identical to its orbital velocity:
Jupiter orbits at 29,220 mph
