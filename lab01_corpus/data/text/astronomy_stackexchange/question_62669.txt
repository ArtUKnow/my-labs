What physical mechanisms dominate the empirical length saturation of dissolving open cluster tidal streams in the Galactic thin disc?
In standard Galactic dynamics (e.g., Binney & Tremaine 2008, Section 8.3; Spitzer 1958), stars escape an open cluster when internal two-body relaxation and external Galactic tides push them beyond the cluster's Jacobi tidal radius:
r_J = [ (G * M_c) / (4 * A * (A - B)) ]^(1/3)
where M_c is the cluster mass, and A, B are the local Oort constants (A ≈ 15.3 km/s/kpc, B ≈ -11.9 km/s/kpc).
In an idealized static Galactic potential, escaped stars are expected to shear into leading and trailing tidal arms whose physical length L_stream grows linearly with dynamical time Δt:
L_stream ≈ 3 * Δv * Δt ∝ (r_J / R_0) * v_0 * Δt
However, when analyzing dissolving open clusters in the Solar Neighborhood using Gaia DR3 astrometry and 6D phase space (tracking conserved Jacobi energy E_J = E - Ω_c * L_z), observational surveys (e.g., Meingast & Alves 2019; Kos et al. 2024; Sharma et al. 2025) indicate that tidal tails do not grow indefinitely. Instead, the ratio of stream length to tidal radius appears to hit an empirical saturation ceiling:
(L_stream / r_J)_infinity ≈ 4.0 - 5.0
beyond which the coherent, kinematically cold stream structure (velocity dispersion σ_v < 1 km/s) is completely disrupted or dissolved into the thin-disc field background.
My Questions:
Which perturbative process dominates this empirical truncation in the Solar cylinder (d ≤ 1.5 kpc)?
Is the stream length primarily limited by vertical disc-crossing shocks as the cluster oscillates through the midplane (z = 0) on a timescale T_z ≈ 70 - 90 Myr?
Or is the dominant disruption mechanism impulsive tidal heating from encounters with Giant Molecular Clouds (GMCs), as modeled analytically by Spitzer (1958) and Gieles et al. (2006)?
Phase-Mixing vs. Physical Destruction: Analytically, does this saturation represent the physical unbinding and dispersal of the stream stars into the general disc field (where energy dispersion ΔE_J exceeds stream coherence), or is it an observational selection horizon where epicyclic phase mixing simply reduces the surface density below the local thin-disc Poisson noise?
I would appreciate insights grounded in Fokker-Planck approximations, N-body simulations, or analytic tidal tensor formulations.
