Finding azimuth of sunrise from solar declination and latitude (spherical trig)
Inspired by this blog post, I've been trying to make a diagram showing the spherical trigonometry of the sunrise equation.
I'm using the diagramming package Asymptote, which I am learning as I go along.
From the blog post:
[A]t sunrise or sunset, the sun, pole star, and horizon point below the pole star will form a right-angled spherical triangle, with hypotenuse subtending an angle $\pi/2-\delta$ and vertical side subtending an angle $\phi$.
For latitude $\phi$ and solar declination $\delta$, the solar hour angle $\omega$ is given by
$$\cos(\omega) = -\tan(\phi)\tan(\delta).$$
This is the angle between noon (due South) and the location of sunrise. The blog post derives the formula very directly using spherical trigonometry.
Since the triangle has a right angle at $\text{C}$ ("the horizon point below the pole star"), it seems that it's possible to easily determine the azimuth of sunrise from the following spherical trig formula $(\text{R1})$:
$$\cos(c) = \cos(a)\cos(b).$$
The angle of sunrise/sunset from North is given by $\arccos \left(\frac{\cos(b)}{\cos(a)}\right)$, in other words $\arccos \left(\frac{\cos(90^\circ-\delta)}{\cos(\phi)}\right)$.
To clarify: $c$ is the angle between the sun and the point on the horizon that lies due North. $a$ is the latitude. $b$ is $90$ degrees minus the solar declination. These three are the edges of the same spherical triangle as discussed in Tao's blog post.
I guess this isn't very accurate, but a few superficial checks seem to suggest that the formula $\arccos \left(\frac{\cos(90^\circ-\delta)}{\cos(\phi)}\right)$ gives a good approximation of the azimuth as the centre of the sun is geometrically on the horizon. This all seems a lot simpler than, but geometrically consistent with, other derivations of the sunrise equation, eg https://astronomy.stackexchange.com/a/44799/56309 which refers to http://web.archive.org/web/20240324212405/http://star-www.st-and.ac.uk/~fv/webnotes/chapter7.htm.
