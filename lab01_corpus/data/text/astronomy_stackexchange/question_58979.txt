A planet after a moon-generating impact
Nascent terrestrial planets quickly have their surfaces cool down.
Formation of crust then yields formation of a figure.
What happens to the already formed crust and the figure after an energetic collision sufficient to produce a small or middle-size moon (not a large one like our Moon)?
Will the crust and figure stay about unchanged?
Or will the collision shake and warm up the planet's surface layers so
that the old shape will be gone, and formation of crust and figure will start anew?
More specifically:
Let a planet be spinning at a rate $\Omega$ and have a dynamical oblateness $J_2$ corresponding hydrostatically to $\Omega$.
After a hit, the planet's rotation rate becomes $\Omega^{\,\prime}$. Will the planet retain the oblateness value $J_2\,$, or will it acquire a new value $J_2^{\,\prime}$ corresponding to $\Omega^{\,\prime}\,$? An answer to this question is defined by how the crust (or most of it) survives the collision.
