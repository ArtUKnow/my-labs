Is it possible for planets or stars near a kilonova to accrete large amounts of r-process elements in the kilomova ejecta?
The main avenue for forming r-process elements is neutron star collisions. There was a question about ejecta from them potentially forming planets. If they did form, they would be absolutely filled to the brim with heavy r-process elements. The only good answer isn’t too optimistic about planet formation. But is there another way for r-process rich objects to form?
Specifically, the scenario I propose is this. A free-floating object performs a flyby of a pair of neutron stars, right as they collide. The kilonova spews out r-process elements, and our object is hit by them. I say object because this will work for pretty much anything you might find in space, but the exact scenario depends on what it is.
If the object is something like an asteroid or rocky planet, it gets caught in a maelstrom of these r-process elements. Some will be deposited on its surface. The object will emerge from the kilonova enriched in these r-process elements. If the planet has an atmosphere, these elements might be incorporated into it too.
If the object is a gas giant/sub-brown dwarf or brown dwarf, more possibilities arise. First, they could accrete some of it, and in this case the r-process ejecta mixes with the gas evenly. The result is an object enriched in these r-process elements throughout its structure. Second, if the object is a massive brown dwarf, just below the hydrogen burning threshold, then it could gain enough mass by absorbing ejecta to exceed the hydrogen burning threshold and become an absurdly metal-rich red dwarf star. And there are a pair of more interesting possibilities. The first is that some amount of ejecta falls into orbit about it, forming a disk. The second is that the object already has a protoplanetary disk, which gets showered with prodigious quantities of r-process elements. A kilonova seeded the solar system with a large percentage of its total supply of such elements from a thousand light years away (and 80 million years before the Sun formed). If the solar system had been, say, 10 light years away instead, imagine how many of these elements there would be in the makeup of all the planets, moons and asteroids (many of these are highly-sought after by humanity, so this could be a boon for civilisations as well if they develop in such exotic places). In both cases, you could have an entire system of asteroids/planets chock-full of exotic heavy elements. If the object becomes a low-mass star by absorbing ejecta as mentioned above, this could be an entire solar system like our own. Maybe even a place where civilisation develops, on a planet filled with elements that we literally enslave children to mine on Earth.
