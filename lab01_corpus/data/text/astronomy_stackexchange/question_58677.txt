Assuming the expansion of the universe somehow abruptly stopped tomorrow, how long would it take for all galaxies in the observable universe to merge?
Assuming that after abruptly ending expansion, the universe remained completely static until the end of time, how long would it take for every single galaxy in the observable universe to merge into one gigantic ultramassive galaxy?
Discounting any galaxies or galatic remnants, stars, gas etc. that are ejected during mergers, assuming it was possible for any object to reach an escape velocity that would make it permanently unbound from anything else in the universe in this scenario.
As an aside, how massive would this final universal galaxy be, and how large? And would this process take longer than the time required for every galaxy in the universe to dissolve via stellar encounters and devouring by the supermassive black holes? Although presumably the super- and hypermassive black hole remnants could continue on merging with each other long after galaxies themselves have dissolved.
I have purposely used the observable universe for this scenario, and not the whole universe, as we don't know how large the entire universe is. Obviously, if the universe is incredibly large, or even infinite, galaxies would dissolve and black holes decay via Hawking radiation before such a universal galaxy could form.
