Is the Schwarzschild metric obsolete?
It is already well known that the modern theory of a black hole is that it also incorporates spin.
The majority of black holes which originate from massive super/hypergiant stars which all have a very small amount of rotation. After the collapse to a black hole the spin increases to a noticeable value due to the conservation of angular momentum.
Supermassive black holes in galactic nuclei also have a considerable amount of spin (for example, the M87 black hole has a spin parameter of a>0.75 according to Vyacheslav Ivanovich Dokuchaev) because of the constant accretion of matter.
The 2 points above prove that all black holes that are measured or theorised are Kerr black holes. So why is the Schwarzschild metric still used and is not considered obsolete? I would assume that the Kerr metric is the primary metric to describe the spacetime and geometry of black holes. I would assume the Schwarzschild metric is out-dated also because it was formulated in 1916 where black holes were a new concept and no one knew what they would look like or the physics of them. So I am wondering what's so special about it if Karl Schwarzschild derived it to describe the first theory of a black hole which he assumed was perfectly symmetrical. Not to mention that modern theories reject the 0D singularity which is considered instead true by the Schwarzschild metric. On the other hand, I am aware that the Oppenheimer-Snyder model proves the Schwarzschild metric's reliability to describe black holes as the model proves the creation of a Schwarzschild black hole from asymmetrical stars. However, even this model does not incorporate the spin of the star which makes it more biased to modern observations.
