Understanding Section 8.4 of "Structure formation in the Universe" by T. Padmanabhan
I am reading Section 8.4 (The masses of galaxies) of "Structure formation in the Universe" by T. Padmanabhan.
In this section, the author tries to estimate the mass of the galaxies comparing the dynamical time and the cooling time.
He finds
$$ t_{\text{dyn}} = \dfrac{\pi}{2}\,\left( \dfrac{2GM}{R^3} \right)^{-1/2} $$
and
$$ t_{\text{cool}} = 8\times 10^6\,\text{years}\,\left( \dfrac{n}{cm^{-3}} \right)^{-1}\,\left[ \left( \dfrac{T}{10^6K} \right)^{-1/2} +1.5\,f_m\,\left( \dfrac{T}{10^6K} \right)^{-3/2} \right] $$
where $M$ is the mass of the overdensity, $R$ its radius, $n$ is the numerical density, $T$ the gas temperature $f_m$ is the fraction of metals. In $t_{\text{cool}}$ the first term is for bremsstrahlung, the second for recombination.
The author then calculates $t_{\text{cool}}/t_{\text{dyn}}\propto M$ and imposes it to be equal to 1 to find the critical mass for the collapse.
He finds $$\dfrac{t_{\text{cool}}}{t_{\text{dyn}}}\approx \dfrac{M}{9 \times 10^{11}\,M_\odot}$$ however it relies on the temperature being $T=10^6K$ if I impose, for example, $T=10^5K$ I would obtain $$\dfrac{t_{\text{cool}}}{t_{\text{dyn}}}\approx \dfrac{M}{10^{10}\,M_\odot}.$$
My question is why that choice of the temperature? Are there physical reasons for this? I understand $T=10^6K$ is the separation temperature between a cooling led by bremsstrahlung and one led by recombination, but any other temperature less than that value would have worked out as well (right?)
