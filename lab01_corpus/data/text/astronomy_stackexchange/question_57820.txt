Computing error bar for vertex of parabola-fit lightcurve
I have a long-term lightcurve for my source, to which I have used orthogonal distance regression to fit a quadratic model [expected flux]=a[year]²+b[year]+c.
Part of our data analysis includes finding the peak time of this lightcurve -- that is, the vertex of the fit-parabola. For each parameter, of the parabola, we have an uncertainty. Calculating the year-value of the vertex is as trivial as year=-b/2a. However, performing uncertainty propagation using the standard uncertainty propogation equation ($δf=\sqrt{\sum{(\frac{δf}{δx}*δx})^2}$) on this quickly shows a mathematically unstable result which offers an uncertainty range of nearly 400 years, which is non-physical given that this entire dataset is on the scale of about a decade, and not hundreds of years.
I am wondering what statistical methods one would use to compute a numerically reasonable error bar for the vertex of a parabola given a, b, c, δa, δb, and δc.
