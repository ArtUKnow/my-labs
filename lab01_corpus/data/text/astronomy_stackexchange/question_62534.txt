Black hole universe
It's been said that the radius and amount of matter (estimated) for the observable universe, roughly correspond to the Schwarzschild radius. Or in other words
$$R_{ObsUn} \approx \frac {2G}{c^2} M_{ObsUn}$$
at least within the error of our measurements. So the question sometimes arises "Are we inside a black hole?"
I'm not primarily interested in whether these figures are actually correct, but my question is:
What would we expect to observe from within a Gly-sized black hole (assuming such a thing could exist)? Would the observations be inconsistent with what we really observe, and if so, how?
For example, although spacetime on planetary or even galactic scales might be approximately Minkowskian, would we expect to see a clear cosmological redshift or preferential direction pointing toward the center? Is this different than what we observe with the Great Attractor?
