Is the total energy of the Universe not invariant?
Consider a Universe which has only one object (bigger particle). The object exploded (somehow) into two smaller particles (with some release of energy). Consider the two particles to have masses M and m, and their speed be V and v respectively. Momentum Conservation demands MV = mv.
From the frame of an observer (not included in Universe), the total energy of the Universe is 1/2MV^2 + 1/2mv^2. But from the frame of the particle with mass M, total energy of the Universe is 1/2m(v + V)^2, while it is still different w.r.t particle of mass m. Why this discrepancy? Cannot we consider the total energy of the Universe to be the same w.r.t every observer, or in other words, cannot we consider the total energy of the Universe to be a physical constant? (Have we to include the radiation energy of explosion as well?)
