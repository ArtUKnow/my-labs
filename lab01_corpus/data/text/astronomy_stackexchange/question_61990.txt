What are the possible origins of the large mass and spin of the black hole merger GW231123?
It is believed that black holes between 60 and 130 $M_\odot$ can’t form via core-collapse of massive stars, because stellar cores at such masses will be destroyed in pair-instability supernovae (PISN). Yet LIGO detected a merger of a 137 $M_\odot$ and a 103 $M_\odot$ black hole, which carry normalized spins of 0.9 and 0.8, respectively. The very high spins also made it difficult to accurately determine the masses. But if the estimation is reliable, how can we explain the masses of the two black holes?
While it’s tempting to assume that the two black holes were also mergers from smaller black holes like GW190521, such near mass mergers are extremely unlikely to result in a spin parameter as high as 0.9, which requires both black holes spinning at extreme speeds and perfect alignment between their spins and orbital angular momentum. Another possible explanation is that a stellar core above 140 $M_\odot$ can collapse into a black hole via photo-disintegration. But like PISN, such a scenario only occurs in massive stars with slow spins.
