Absence of Pressure in the relativistic stress energy tensor
In Dodleson, Eq. 3.20 of Chap. 3, the relativistic stress-energy tensor is given as:
$$T^{\mu}_{\nu}(x,t) = \frac{g}{\text{det}(g_{\alpha\beta})} \int \frac{dQ_1dQ_2dQ_3}{(2\pi)^3} \frac{Q^{\mu}Q_{\nu}}{Q^0} f(x,Q,t)
\tag{$1$} \ ,
$$
where $Q^{\mu}$ is the four momentum and $f(x,p,t)$ is the distribution.
I can identify the above expression to be the integral form of $\rho u^{\mu}u_{\nu}$, with the four velocity, $u^{\mu} \propto Q^{\mu}$.
But then, the stress energy tensor for a relativistic fluid is
$$T^{\mu}_{\nu} = \left(\rho + \frac{P}{c^2}\right)u^{\mu}u_{\nu} + g^{\mu}_{\nu}
P \ ,
\tag{$2$}
$$
where $P$ is the pressure.
My question is that I can identify the expression in Eq. (1), with the $\rho u^{\mu}u_{\nu}$ only. I don't see the pressure being captured in Eq. (1), and we know that the pressure is not negligbible for a relativistic fluid.
The expression in Eq. (1) is used for linear perturbation analysis. Why is the pressure not captured in Eq. (1)?
