Red Shift Puzzle
I understand redshift and how it relates to relative motion, but my question is:
It takes millions or billions of years for light from the most distant objects to reach us here on earth. The most distant objects exhibit the greatest red shift. Does this not indicate that these extremely distant objects were moving away more quickly in the past, since their red shifted light has taken so long to arrive?
Surely the only way to determine if universal expansion is accelerating would be to study the red shift of individual distant radiation sources in isolation over time to see if there is any change. Is this done? If not, how is the accelerating universe hypothesis supported?
