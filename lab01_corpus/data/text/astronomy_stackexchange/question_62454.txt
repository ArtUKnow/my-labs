Did Mangalyaan of ISRO actually photograph the comet C/2013 A1 Siding Spring?
On 12th November, 2014; ISRO tweeted images of Mangalyaan a.k.a. Mars Orbiter Mission (MOM) observed comet C/2013 A1 Siding Spring during its closest encounter with Mars on 19th October, 2014.
The images show a fuzzy dot at the center of the image and ISRO said the following:
"Mars Color Camera onboard the orbiter has captured the Coma(top bright portion) of the Comet partly, as it approached MOM during last 40 minutes of imaging session before Mars-Comet close encounter.
Sequence of MCC images show the Coma move closer to MOM from 1.8 lakh to 1.3 lakh kms. between 17:44 to 18:25 UT on October 19, 2014.
The image in the centre shows a streak (right top to centre ofthe frame) radiating out of the Comet's nucleus (out of frame), possibly indicating the jet from Comet's nucleus. Usually Jets represent outgassing activity from vents of the comet-nucleus,releasing dust and ice crystals. The outgassing activity gradually increases as the comet moves closure to the Sun."
To view the actual images, I took 2 approaches:
I visited Mars Orbiter Mission Long Term Archive (MOM LTA).
The images actually show a faint, fuzzy blob in the center, which does move in the sequence of images between 17:44 to 18:25 UT on October 19, 2014.
Approach 1:
I downloaded the images in PNG format and then tried to view each band (R, G, and B) in Python using a colormap set to "terrain", and it shows that the emissions are somehow coming out vertically from the center of the image (especially from the green band). Even on increasing the brightness manually, the emissions do appear to come out of the target at the center. This might possibly be due to the image quality constrains as it was viewed directly using Microsoft Photos application. (image attached below).
Approach 2:
I downloaded the data as it is and viewed the images through MCC PDS Viewer. On playing with some values of image geometry and image colors, I got a better and yet brighter image of this and still the fuzziness of the object at the center does exist and a faint-fuzzy extension appears to the lower-left. This is much better resolved than Approach 1 (image attached for reference)
So my question is:
Did ISRO actually photograph the comet Siding Spring/its tail, and if it was actually photographed, then how to determine what is at the center of the frame?
