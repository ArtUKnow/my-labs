Why is the lunar disk completely black during solar eclipses?
I noticed that during solar eclipses the lunar disk looks completely black, it is impossible to see any relief details on it. At other times, the unlit part of the Moon is quite clearly visible.
An eclipse occurs during the day when the illuminated side of the Earth faces the Moon, so the surface of the Moon is illuminated by sunlight reflected from the Earth, just as the Earth is illuminated by moonlight at night.
Earth's light is many times brighter than moonlight, since the earth's surface area is larger.
For this reason, during an eclipse, the surface of the Moon should be especially visible, but everything happens exactly the opposite?
