Solar Depression irrelevant with Twilight Emergence?
So far, we've been fixated on the idea that twilight emergence based on the solar depression (solar height below horizon). What if twilight emergence based on the constant time before sunrise or after sunset, instead of using the solar depression?
I still assume twilight begins 72 minutes before sunrise (and ends 72 minutes after sunset) regardless of geographical latitude. which 72 minutes is traditionally the time it takes to travel one parasang/farsakh (= 4 miles) on horseback or on foot. [and 18 degrees was roughly derived from this, assume if observer located at equator or low latitude, which secant of latitude value is not significant]
(regards in Saudi Arabia, dusk is fixated 90 minutes after Sunset [or 120 minutes when Ramadan] and dawn is fixated at 18.5 degrees solar depression, Indonesia (and formerly M'sia and S'pore) using 20 for dawn and 18 for dusk, Egyptian Geological Survey using 19.5 for dawn and 17.5 for dusk, Karachi University using 15, Europe and North America using 12 or 6 (when summer) etc.)
I realize this twilight phenomenon is very complex because it is also influenced by the surface brightness of the moon, a natural light pollution that can delay (before sunrise) or accelerate (after sunset) the onset of twilight (as explained in this paper).
