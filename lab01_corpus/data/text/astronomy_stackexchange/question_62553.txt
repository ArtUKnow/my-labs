Can a chlorine atmosphere exist over a water ocean?
The Potential Feasibility of Chlorinic Photosynthesison Exoplanets suggests that atmospheric chlorine could be a biosignature if photosynthesizers extract electrons from chloride instead of water. But is it actually possible for a chlorine atmosphere to exist over a water ocean? Cl2 reacts with water to form HCl and HOCl, and HOCl photolyzes to form HCl and O2, so it seems to me that the same conditions that are supposed to favor the development of chlorogenic photosynthesis would also drive the abiotic replacement of free chlorine by free oxygen, leaving oxygen as the most easily detectable biosignature.
Am I missing something?
