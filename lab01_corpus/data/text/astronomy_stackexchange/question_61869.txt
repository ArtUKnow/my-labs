Could an exoplanet's atmosphere have significant quantities of heavy noble gases?
Earth's atmosphere is approximately 1% argon, which is actually surprisingly small amount, and only trace quantities of krypton and xenon. Argon isn't that rare in the universe, apparently its abundance is about one fifth of nitrogen's. Also, they are so much heavier than hydrogen or helium that they shouldn't readily escape from atmosphere, and being nonreactive, almost all of them should end up in atmosphere rather than being bound by minerals.
Apparently the mass of Earth's atmosphere is in a 1:1,200,000 ratio when compared to entire mass of Earth. So, assuming that Earth would have retained all krypton and xenon, and they would all be in the atmosphere, calculating from their abundance in the universe, there should be around 1200000 * 4 * 10^-6% = 4.8% of krypton and 1200000 * 1 * 10^-6% = 1% of xenon. There should be massive quantities of argon, unless much of it escaped into space. Now this is a very crude approximation, but it shows that they are much rarer than they "should" be.
So, is it possible that some exoplanet would have argon rather than nitrogen making up bulk of the atmosphere, and possibly krypton being in single digit percentages rather than just trace amounts?
