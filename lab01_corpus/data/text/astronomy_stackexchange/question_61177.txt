Is there such a thing as a non-solar analemma?
Context
There is a good understanding what a (solar) analemma is, there are many questions about it on this site and also intermittently trending videos showing it, like this one on Twitter: https://x.com/BGatesIsaPyscho/status/1924363389188427868. There is also discussion about how analemmas look on other planets or how it would look on hyphotetical planets with different configurations (axial tilt, etc.).
My question
Is there such a thing as an analemma for another planet? What I have in mind is that what kind of shape would I get if I would photograph the same planet (let's say Venus, Mars, or Saturn) from the same place on Earth at the same time day after day, year after year.
Note
This should not be confounded with orbital resonance (cf. for Venus here https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus#Orbit_in_respect_to_Earth ), which gives an almost closed shape if the resonance is imperfect. I.e., I don't think that Venus' analemma would be the shape of the well-known "Pentagram of Venus", or would it? Nevertheless, I assume that it would not be a closed shape for a planet, unlike for the central star in a system.
