Why aren't Turnbull's top 5 Terrestrial Planet Finder targets in HabCat?
In 2002, Margaret Turnbull and Jill Tarter created the Catalog of Nearby Habitable Systems (also called HabCat), a list of 17,129 solar systems that where we are more likely to find habitable worlds. According to Turnbull's Wikipedia page she later made a short list with similar goals, "her top candidates for the Terrestrial Planet Finder (Epsilon Indi, Epsilon Eridani, 40 Eridani, Alpha Centauri B, and Tau Ceti)." The Wikipedia page for Tau Ceti quotes Turnbull further about this list of five "these are places I'd want to live if God were to put our planet around another star". Looking back, I see that four of these stars were in the list of possible stars in the 1964 paper Habitable Planets for Man by Steven Dole.
I decided to download the HabCat, and look for those five stars in there, and I was surprised to discover that none of them are on that list. Why don't these stars qualify for the HabCat? I looked through Turnbull and Tarter's paper Target Selection for SETI. I. A Catalog of Nearby Habitable Stellar Systems and I'm not clear on why.
I'm really interested in this for a small hobby science fiction project. If I find out that my favorite nearby solar systems are bad places to find life, or even bad places for humans to set up outposts, I will be sad.
Some ideas I have:
Maybe these five are places where earth could thrive, but it is unlikely that an extraterrestrial intelligence could have evolved there for reasons such as a young age of the system.
Maybe those solar systems really are unlikely to have habitable worlds. For example, I see that the HabCat has rules on metallicity, variability, number of resolvable components, and certain dynamics of binary systems. That raises another question of why they qualify for the Terrestrial Planet Finder, and I can ask that question later if no one explains it here.
I notice that the HabCat contains no stars within 3.33 parsecs (10.8 light years) of Earth even though most HabCat stars have HabCat neighbors closer than that. That makes me wonder if the HabCat ignores very close stars because they were already identified as good candidates elsewhere. I also wondered if there were technical reasons for this, for example, if their rules for uncertainty in parallax led to filtering out bright stars. (My lay understanding is that the Gaia project lacks bright stars for similar reasons.)
One or more of these stars are in the HabCat, and I just made a mistake when searching for their HIP and HD numbers.
