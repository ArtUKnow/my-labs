What's wrong with CDs as eclipse glasses?
Solar eclipses are certainly impressive, but we are always warned to wear "approved" glasses, and not to use smoked glass or CDs. Obviously the manufacturers of eclipse glasses will say this, but what about opthalmologists and astronomers?
I realise that inadequate protection can cause permanent eye damage, and that CDs may have tiny holes. But I also understand that it takes several seconds for retina damage, and a hand-held CD will always wobble so that any micro-holes will not remain on one spot on the retina.
I have viewed several eclipses using CDs (and one, about 1955, with glass smoked by teacher with a candle -- I was about 7). So, anecdotally, CDs are not always ineffective.
What are the facts?
