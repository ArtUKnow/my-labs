Why hasn’t LIGO detected more kilonova events after GW170817?
One likely explanation is that gamma ray bursts (GRBs) are extremely rare and most are too far away. However, because GRBs are highly collimated, only a small percentage pointing towards the earth can be detected. Gravitational waves emitted from close binaries should follow the pattern of typical quadruple antennae, which means they can be detected in a much wider range of directions. As a result, as long as we have more than 3 detectors to pinpoint their locations, even if a nearby GRB is not pointed to us, we should be able to detect the afterglow of a kilonova.
