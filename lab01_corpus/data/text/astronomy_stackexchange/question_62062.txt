Calculating the altitude of a satellite in polar orbit for a given distance between successive ground tracks
I'm working through Orbital Mechanics for Engineering Students - Curtis (first edition). Problem 2.7 on page 101 is as follows:
It is desired to place a satellite in earth polar orbit such that successive ground tracks at the equator are spaced $3000$km apart. Determine the required altitude of the circular orbit.
Here is my attempt at solving this problem. As the satellite orbits, the earth rotates beneath it at an angular speed of
$$\omega = \frac{2\pi}{86164}\approx 7.29211\times10^{-5}\,\text{rad}s^{-1}$$
where $86164$ is roughly the length of the sidereal day in seconds. The arc length $s$ of a point on the equator traced out during a time $t$ is given by
$$s = R_E\theta = R_E\omega t$$
where $R_E$ is the radius of earth. So we want $s = 3000\,km$ after the satellite has completed one orbit. That is, $t$ must be equivalent to the orbital period of the satellite. Therefore
$$ t = \frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{\mu}}r^{3/2} = \frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{\mu}}(R_E + z)^{\frac{3}{2}}$$
where $\mu = Gm_E$ is the gravitational parameter of earth, and $z$ is the altitude of the satellite above earth's surface. Putting this all together we have
$$s = R_E\omega\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{\mu}}(R_E + z)^{\frac{3}{2}} $$
and after rearranging we get
$$z = \sqrt[3]{\mu}\left(\frac{s}{2\pi R_E\omega}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}} - R_E$$
Plugging in $s = 3000\,km$, $R_E = 6378\,km$, $\mu = 398600\,km^3s^{-2}$ and $\omega = 7.29211\times10^{-5}\,\text{rad}s^{-1}$ we get
$$ z \approx 1110\,km$$
However, the answer in the textbook is given as $1440\,km$. I'm not certain where I've gone wrong.
