Can black holes warp space-time into a closed "sphere", and drop out of the Universe?
Gravity wells are depicted as a "dent" in a two dimensional sheet (extrapolated in thought to three dimensional space denting into a fourth spatial dimension); a black hole presents a well deep enough that an "event horizon" appears. The event horizon is always shown circular--it is actually spherical, like the "photosphere".
I saw a presentation once where (it was theorized) when the gravity well becomes deep enough, the gravity begins to lose influence on the space around it--the "well" ceases to be a linear curve and starts to "neck" (narrow at the entrance). If the well becomes deep enough ("critical mass"), the video proposed that the neck, could "snap"--the warp around the singularity would become spherical, the "sheet" would snap back to flat, and the object would reside beneath normal space-time, its gravity no longer affecting nearby matter. One could travel to the location in safety. How then could "supermassive black holes" exist?
