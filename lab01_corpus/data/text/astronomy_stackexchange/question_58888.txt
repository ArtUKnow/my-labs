Why aren't planets easily visible during daytime?
Let's consider stars. They emit a certain amount of radiation. If you move 2x farther from the star, the radiation reaching you follows the inverse square law so you get 1/4 times the amount of radiation. But the angular size of the surface area of the star is reduced by a factor of 1/4, so the area that emits light has the same surface brightness, as measured by magnitudes per arcsecond squared.
So stars should be visible during the daytime, right, because they should be as bright as the sun as measured by surface brightness? They should have the same surface brightness no matter how far away they are, excluding effects caused by the expansion of the universe.
Not so. Alpha Centauri has a radius of 1.2 solar radii or 8.47 * 108 meters,
but a distance from us of 4.344 light years or 4.1 * 1016 meters, so the angular diameter is about 0.0085 arcseconds. Such a point source of light is no longer a point source after Earth's atmosphere, with typical seeing conditions modifying it to have a radius of about an arcsecond, so the surface brightness after our atmosphere is not the same for Alpha Centauri and the Sun.
However, for planets I can't prove that they shouldn't be visible during the daytime. Consider for example Venus. It is closer to the Sun than our Moon, so it should be brighter than the Moon. However, it has a radius of 6051 km and a distance from us of 38 million kilometers (when the dimmest, when sunlight only reaches the half of the planet not visible to us) to 261 million kilometers, so the angular size should vary from 66 arcseconds to 9.6 arcseconds. The atmosphere doesn't distort much such a large circle of light. So Venus should be clearly visible during the daytime. The same can't be said for Jupiter, since it is only 3.8% as bright as the Moon due to the inverse square law.
But I certainly haven't observed Venus during the daytime, or Mars which should be visible too according to my theory of surface brightness.
So why aren't planets easily visible during the daytime, since at least the inner planets should be brighter than the Moon and Mars should be very bright too, although bit dimmer than the Moon?
Is it because you would need binoculars or a telescope to make what is a point source of light according to our eye, into something larger than a point?
