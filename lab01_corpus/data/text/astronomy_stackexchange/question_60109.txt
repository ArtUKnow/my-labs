Does our sun experience Earthlike auroras?
Very (OK, very) simplistically, the Earth has a magnetic field and the Sun has a magnetic field. The Earth experiences auroras due to charged particles emitted by the sun.
Does our sun experience auroras in the same way? Or are there so few charged particles coming in from extrasolar sources that there aren't enough to make it happen? Or does it happen and, given the enormous size of the sun's magnetic field, the naked eye simply can't see them?
